# Chapter 819

*Monday the Seventeenth Day of October, the Ninth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-Third Charge on That Cistern and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Line of the Fifteenth Settling at About Four and the Foot of That Sheet Read After the Settlement and Standing at One Hundred and Twenty-Eight Thousand Five Hundred and Ninety Out and One Hundred and Seventeen Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty Short, the Woman of About Thirty-Four of the Saltmarch Going Back up That Coast Road on the One O'Clock Boat, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Monday the seventeenth day of October, with that market open from six and a fine morning with the wind in the north-east and the first of that weather coming, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Monday, being a rota day and a day with no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the twenty-third and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of tomorrow. The cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-third time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it. The line worked on Saturday the fifteenth settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet at about four today and the total at that foot is read after the settlement and not before it, and it stands at one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety pence out, being eight hundred and three marks, nine shillings and twopence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-second days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this ninth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and seventeenth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and nineteenth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-seventh day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-third night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-first day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and eleventh day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-eighth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-ninth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fiftieth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out and the leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its forty-fifth day a step to the left of that refusal. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eighteenth day and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the seventeenth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and seventeen days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The cistern was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-third time and about four people were standing near it and none of them walked on any of it, and that is three charges running inside seven days and it is the busiest week that cistern has had since before this run of days began.

The line settled at about four. The keeper wrote the figure into the foot of that sheet in front of about four people, being eight hundred and twenty-two pence, being five marks, one shilling and tenpence, and read the two figures of the foot afterwards and not before it. One hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight added to eight hundred and twenty-two is one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety less ten thousand six hundred and forty is one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty, and both of those were said out loud and both of those are on the face of that sheet.

---

The ordinary work of that Monday was four boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

The four new crews were on their twenty-ninth day and did the day's work in about an hour and three quarters under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine said that they had told him in the second week that they could do it faster and that he had written it down at the time, and nobody has asked to see what he wrote.

At about one the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch went out of that yard with her basket and got on the one o'clock boat up that coast road, and about nine people watched her go and about nine did not, and she said out loud at the gate that she would come down again when there was a reason and not before, and that she had a bed booked at the far end for the winter if there was one.

The keeper said out loud, to about four people, that nobody in this harbor was going to write her name down, and that if anybody wrote her name down he would find it out, and that she had not come down here to be put in a book.

---

The woman of thirty of no office came into that yard at about three with her own page and was in it until about five, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about four, and they were in the same yard for about an hour and neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page and neither of them signed the other's page.

Neither of them said one word to the other about the woman who went up the road at one. About four of that nine noticed that neither of them said anything and about four of that nine noticed nothing at all.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-ninth day of that run of days.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those eleven was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

About half past five on that Monday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and two words, and the cistern came first in it and the settlement came in the middle of it and the woman going up the road came last in it, and no count of anything came in it.

"**That butt at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-third time and about four people were standing near it and none of them walked on any of it. Three charges running inside seven days is the busiest week that butt has ever had in that lane since before this run of days began.**"

"**The ordinary work of that Monday was four boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge with the door propped on a brick, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it. The four new crews were on their twenty-ninth day and did the day's work in about an hour and three quarters under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine said they had told him in the second week that they could do it faster and that he had written it down at the time, and nobody has asked to see what he wrote.**"

"**The line settled at about four. The keeper wrote the figure into the foot of that sheet in front of about four people, being eight hundred and twenty-two pence, being five marks, one shilling and tenpence, and read the two figures of the foot afterwards and not before it. One hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight added to eight hundred and twenty-two is one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety out, being eight hundred and three marks, nine shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety less ten thousand six hundred and forty is one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty short, being seven hundred and thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence.**"

"**At about one the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch went out of that yard with her basket and got on the one o'clock boat up that coast road, and about nine people watched her go and about nine did not. She said out loud at the gate that she would come down again when there was a reason and not before, and that she had a bed booked at the far end for the winter if there was one.**"

"**And the rest of it. The woman of thirty of no office came into that yard at about three with her own page and was in it until about five, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about four, and they were in the same yard for about an hour and neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page and neither of them signed the other's page. Neither of them said one word to the other about the woman who went up the road at one, and about four of that nine noticed that neither of them said anything about it at all and about four of that nine noticed nothing at all in that yard.**"

"**A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything, and it is the sixty-ninth day of that run of days. Eleven hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, every one of them the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against.**"

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and thirty-second days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line and is neither shut nor struck.**"

"**The two sheets at that end stall are on their fiftieth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and the sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen, and the wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August. A man of about twenty-nine has now gone three hundred and seventeen days without hearing anything in the left side of his head and no figure is set against that figure and he is not on the water. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor has closed a ninth day without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for four days down a road and four days up it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had thirty-six lines on it.

---

**THE CISTERN WAS CHARGED AND IT WAS THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-THIRD TIME AND IT WAS FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THAT FOOT STANDS AT ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND NINETY PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND SEVENTEEN THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND FIFTY PENCE SHORT, AND THE TOTAL WAS READ AFTER THE SETTLEMENT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SECOND DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the seventeenth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the ninth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Monday and was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-third time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it, and that it is three charges inside seven days; that the ordinary work of that said Monday was four boats off that mole before noon and a length of line cut to length against a gauge, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their twenty-ninth day and did the day's work in about an hour and three quarters; that the line worked on Saturday the fifteenth settled into the foot of that sheet at about four and the total at that foot was read after the settlement, and that one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight added to eight hundred and twenty-two is one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety out, being eight hundred and three marks, nine shillings and twopence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty short, being seven hundred and thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that at about one the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch went up that coast road on the one o'clock boat and said out loud at the gate that she would come down again when there was a reason and not before; that the keeper said out loud to about four people that nobody in this harbor was going to write her name down and that if anybody wrote her name down he would find it out; that the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about three until about five and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about four and neither of them said one word to the other about the woman who went up the road at one; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything, and that it is the sixty-ninth day of that run of days; that eleven hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day; that the two sheets at that end stall are on their fiftieth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and that the sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen; and that the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the seventeenth day of being on that community's own page four days up that coast road.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the keeper said out loud that nobody in this harbor was going to write that woman's name down, and that the reason he said it out loud in about four people instead of saying it to her at the gate is that saying it to one person is a promise and saying it in four is a rule, and that a rule about names made by a keeper in a shed is about the only kind of rule this yard can make that sticks; that the reason she said she would come down again when there was a reason and not before is that four days of road is four days out of a community of about four hundred people and that anybody who comes down once too often stops being a person who comes from that place and becomes a person who visits this one; that the reason the four new crews said out loud that they had told him in the second week and that he had written it down is that a man who writes a thing down about his own work is a harder man to improve and a better man to work for, and that about four of that nine have worked for him longer than they meant to.*
