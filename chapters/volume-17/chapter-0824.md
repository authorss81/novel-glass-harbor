# Chapter 824

*Saturday the Twenty-Second Day of October, the Fourteenth Day, an Ordinary Working Saturday With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement in It, a Man of About Twenty-Three off the Morning Boat Standing at That End Stall at About Half Past Eleven Reading a Board in Chalk and Asking Out Loud What the Two Figures Are For and Nobody Answering Him, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Saturday the twenty-second day of October, with that market open from six and a grey close morning that turned to a thin rain about eleven and kept at it until about three, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Saturday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-fifth. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Saturday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fourteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twenty-second day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-fourth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixty-second day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-eighth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-sixth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and sixteenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-third day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-fourth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-fifth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-third day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-second day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-two days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday was four boats off that mole before noon and a hatches out of the water by two in the afternoon in rain that came in sideways off the water, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their thirty-fourth day and had the morning's work off in about two hours of it under the man of about thirty-two, and about two of that nine said out loud, while they were doing it, that they would not be here this time next week.

---

At about half past eleven on that Saturday a man of about twenty-three came in off the morning boat with a bag and a coat that had been wet the whole way and stood at the end of that row of stalls and read the chalk board.

He read it twice. He put his finger on the six hundred and seventy-two and then on the forty-eight and then on the line of words underneath, and then he asked the man of about fifty-eight of that market, out loud, in the ordinary way, whether those two figures were what the place four days up the coast road had lost or what it had saved by not hiring the boat.

The man of about fifty-eight said out loud that he did not know and that he had put it up on a board because a board was the thing he had, and that he had meant the lost one when he wrote it and that if he had meant the other one it would read different.

The keeper was three stalls along and came over and stood at the end of that awning and heard the whole of it, and he did not answer it and neither did anybody else in that lane, and about four people who were standing there said nothing at all and one of them went back inside.

The man of about twenty-three said out loud that that was the first place he had been in three weeks where somebody had written down a figure and would not tell him what it was for, and then he asked one more thing out loud, being whether there was any work going in that yard below the market for a man who can splice and haul, and the keeper said out loud that there was not any today and that he would say the same on Monday, and that a man who can splice and haul will not starve in this harbor for a week.

The man of about twenty-three took his bag off the boards, said out loud that the figures were all right by him and that he would want to know what they were for before he took his money off anybody, and went off up the lane toward the fish market and did not come back into that row.

Nobody was cross with him about it and nobody wrote his name down and nobody has thought about him since, and the man of about thirty-two was not in that lane at all today.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one and then again from two until about four. The man of about thirty-two had the page of routes in that shed from one until five. Neither of them was in that market at half past eleven and neither of them heard the man of about twenty-three until about four, and neither of them raised what he had asked.

The second lay's bench was taken at ten by a man of about thirty-four and given up at two, and nobody asked him a single thing while he was on it, being the seventy-fourth day of that run of days.

On two sheets that went out of buildings in that harbor on that Saturday seven hands went down, and each of the seven was at work on somebody else's line, and the page of days carries seven against this day.

---

About half past five on that Saturday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and ninety-one words, and the ordinary work came first in it, the man off the morning boat came in the middle of it, and those two figures in the chalk came at the end of it, and this reading carries no count of any kind.

On their fifty-fifth day that pair are still one size between them with a hand's breadth to separate them and nothing struck through underneath that correction, and the leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its fiftieth day, and the sheet of the fifteen gaps is still fifteen, and that wall in the room over that chandler's store is bare.**"

"**Three hundred and twenty-two days of quiet out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, with no figure standing against it and none of his own days counted against it. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. There are nine hulls two sea miles off that mole head and fourteen days of this run of days have now gone by with not one of them mentioned by anybody in this market.**"

Nobody was thanked for a stranger who asked a fair question and went off up the lane, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty-one lines on it.

**A MAN OFF THE MORNING BOAT READ TWO FIGURES IN CHALK AT THAT END STALL ON THE MORNING HE CAME IN OFF THAT BOAT AND ASKED OUT LOUD WHAT THEY WERE FOR, AND NOBODY IN THAT LANE ANSWERED HIM. A BOARD IN CHALK IS NOT A PAGE AND THE FIGURE ON IT CANNOT BE TAKEN OFF IT BY ANYBODY WHO WAS NOT THERE WHEN IT WAS WRITTEN. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the twenty-second day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the fourteenth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Saturday and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that the ordinary work of that said Saturday was four boats off that mole before noon and a hatches out of the water by two, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their thirty-fourth day and had the morning's work off in about two hours, and that about two of that nine said out loud while they were doing it that they would not be here this time next week; that at about half past eleven a man of about twenty-three came in off the morning boat with a bag and a coat that had been wet the whole way and stood at that end stall and read that board twice, and that he asked the man of about fifty-eight of that market out loud whether the two figures were what the place four days up that coast road had lost or what it had saved by not hiring the boat; that the man of about fifty-eight said out loud that he did not know and that he had meant the lost one when he wrote it and that if he had meant the other one it would read different; that the keeper came over from three stalls along and heard the whole of it and did not answer it and neither did anybody else in that lane; that the man of about twenty-three said out loud that that was the first place he had been in three weeks where somebody had written down a figure and would not tell him what it was for, and then asked out loud whether there was any work going in that yard below the market for a man who can splice and haul, and that the keeper said out loud that there was not any today and that he would say the same on Monday; that the man of about twenty-three went off up the lane toward the fish market and did not come back into that row, and that nobody was cross with him about it and nobody wrote his name down; that the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one and from about two until about four, and that the man of about thirty-two had the page of routes in that shed from about one until about five, and that neither of them was in that market at half past eleven and neither of them said one word to the other about what he had asked; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything, and that it is the seventy-fourth day of that run of days; that seven hands went onto two sheets that left buildings in that harbor, and that the seven were other people's hands; that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; and that the two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-fifth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the keeper did not answer the man off the morning boat and that the reason he did not is not that he had no answer, and that he had one ready in about a second and every one of the three or four answers he could have given would have been about this harbor and not about the place the figures were for, and that a person standing there with a wet coat is owed an answer about his own morning and not about another community's autumn; that the reason the man of about fifty-eight did not know whether his own board was a loss or a saving is that he is not a keeper of anything and keeps a stall and a slate, and that a person in that position can be sure of what he meant when he wrote a thing and not at all sure of what it will be read as being, and that this is the ordinary condition of about nine people out of ten in that lane; that the reason nobody wrote the man's name down is that nobody in that lane writes anybody's name down, and that the one list of names that exists in this harbor is a page of six names that a man of about fifty-one has said nothing about for four hundred and sixty-four days, and that those are not the same thing and have never once been confused; that the two of those four new crews who said out loud that they would not be here this time next week said it while they were working, and that a thing said at work is a different thing from the same words said after, and that this yard has four new crews going off in five weeks and has found that the ones who said it at work were the ones who went.*
