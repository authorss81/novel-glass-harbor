# Chapter 822

*Thursday the Twentieth Day of October, the Twelfth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-Fourth Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand, a Reason Written in Chalk on a Board at That End Stall and No Name on It and the Keeper Saying Out Loud That a Board in Chalk Is Not a Page and Does Not Go on Any Sheet, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Thursday the twentieth day of October, with that market open from six and rain in from the north-west by about ten that stayed in until about two and then went away in patches, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Thursday, being a rota day and a day with no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the twenty-third and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-fifth and the next charge after this one is on the morning of Monday. The cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-fourth time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety pence out, being eight hundred and three marks, nine shillings and twopence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twelfth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twentieth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-second day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixtieth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-sixth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-fourth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and fourteenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-first day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-second day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-third day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-first day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twentieth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about nine that butt at the back of that market was filled for the hundred and ninety-fourth time, fourteen trips by hand, and about four people stood near it and not one of them put a foot on any of it, and the keeper put the hundred and ninety-fourth against the day on that rota slate at about one and read nothing out of it.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was four boats off that mole before noon in rain that got into everybody and a hatches out of the water by two in the afternoon, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

---

At about ten, under the awning of that end stall, in about nine people and in the rain, the man of about fifty-eight of that market took a board of deal off the back of his own stall and rubbed the face of it with the back of his hand and wrote on it in chalk.

He wrote two figures and then he wrote a line of words under them, and the figures were six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and forty-eight pence, being four shillings, and the two together are seven hundred and twenty pence, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence. The line of words under the two figures said that on the first day of October a figure was read off a community's own page four days up that coast road as the figure of what a crossing costs, and that on that figure the hire of a boat for four days was not taken and three days of the work of one man of that place were not worked and not paid, and that the figure is correct and was correct when it was read and says nothing about what a crossing costs.

He wrote no name on that board. He stood it up against the end of his own stall where the rain came under the awning and would get at it in about a week, and then he said out loud, to those about nine, that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again, and that in nine years he has watched three figures come off boards in that lane and every one of them was a figure somebody did not like by the end of the week.

The keeper was at the end of that awning at about ten and heard all of that and said out loud that the man of about fifty-eight was right about boards, and that a board in chalk in this market is not a page and is not going on that sheet and is not going on the wall in the room over that fish market either, and that a wall in this harbor takes four kinds of thing and this is not one of them and he was not going to propose one on a wet morning.

About four of that nine said nothing and about four said out loud that a wet board with a right figure on it was better than nothing at all, and nobody argued with the man of about fifty-eight and nobody helped him.

---

The keeper said one more thing out loud at about eleven at that same stall, to about nine people, and it was about what a day of a man comes to.

He said that a man-day in that yard is sixteen pence, being one shilling and fourpence, and that three of them is forty-eight pence, and that the two figures now in the chalk on that board are the whole of what came out of a right figure and that neither of them is a figure of money that this sheet takes in or pays out. He said that the second of those two figures is three days of a man's work and that a day of a man's work is not a figure of money in this harbor, it is a day, and that there is no column here for a day and there has never been one and he is not going to invent one on a wet morning either.

Nobody in that nine asked him what a day ought to be worth and nobody said out loud that the forty-eight pence was a small sum. The man of about fifty-eight turned the board round so the chalk was out of the rain for a minute and then turned it back and went back to his stall.

---

The woman of thirty of no office was at that yard from about ten until about two with her own page and stayed in this harbor today. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from two until five with the page of routes and was nowhere near that market at ten or at eleven, and the first time he saw that board was about half past two.

About four of that nine noticed that he looked at it for a while and then went out into the rain, and about four of that nine noticed nothing at all.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay held that bench from about ten until about two and nothing was said to him in it that wanted an answer back, being the seventy-second day of that run.

Six hands were put onto three sheets that went out of buildings in that harbor on that day, and every one of the six was somebody else's hand and not the hand that wrote it, and the page of days carries six against this day.

---

TWO FIGURES AND A REASON WENT UP IN CHALK ON A BOARD AT THAT END STALL WITH NO NAME ON IT, AND A BOARD IN CHALK IS NOT A PAGE AND IS NOT A LINE ON THAT SHEET. *First column, entered on Thursday the twentieth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the twelfth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Thursday and was a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that the cistern at the back of that market was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-fourth time and it was fourteen trips by hand and it was walked on by none of it; that the ordinary work of that said Thursday was four boats off that mole before noon in rain and a hatches out of the water by two, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their thirty-second day and did about an hour and three quarters of the day's work under the man of about thirty-two and stood out of the rain at the end of that mole for the rest of it; that at about ten under the awning of that end stall, in about nine people, the man of about fifty-eight of that market wrote two figures and a reason in chalk on a board of deal off the back of his own stall, being six hundred and seventy-two pence, being four marks, two shillings and eightpence, and forty-eight pence, being four shillings, and seven hundred and twenty pence together, being four marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that the reason said that on the first day of October a figure was read off a community's own page four days up that coast road as the figure of what a crossing costs, and that on that figure the hire of a boat for four days was not taken and three days of the work of one man of that place were not worked and not paid; that he wrote no name on that board and stood it up against the end of his own stall; that he said out loud that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again; that the keeper said out loud that a board in chalk in this market is not a page and is not going on that sheet and is not going on the wall in the room over that fish market, and that a wall in this harbor takes four kinds of thing and this is not one of them; that the keeper said out loud at about eleven that a man-day in that yard is sixteen pence, being one shilling and fourpence, and that three of them is forty-eight pence, and that a day of a man's work is not a figure of money in this harbor and that there is no column here for a day; that nobody asked what a day ought to be worth and nobody said out loud that the forty-eight pence was a small sum; that the man of about thirty-two did not see that board until about half past two and then went out into the rain; that the woman of thirty of no office was at that yard from about ten until about two with her own page and stayed in this harbor on the said day; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything, and that it is the seventy-second day of that run of days; that six hands went onto three sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and that the six were not the hands of whoever wrote those three; that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety pence out, being eight hundred and three marks, nine shillings and twopence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand nine hundred and fifty pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-seven marks, two shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; and that the two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-third day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the man of about fifty-eight wrote his own two figures on his own board in his own chalk instead of carrying somebody else's, and that what put those two figures on that board in his own hand rather than carried them off somebody else's is that a person who copies a figure has quietly put that person's writing under his own without meaning anything by it, and that he would rather be the man who wrote the wrong thing here than the man who copied a right thing; that the keeper let it stand and did not stop him and did not put it anywhere else, and that the reason for letting it stand is that a figure a stranger can walk up to and read is worth more to the four hundred people four days up that road than the same figure in a drawer in a building they have no reason to enter, and that this is the second time he has said that and he will say it a third time; that the reason he said out loud at about eleven that there is no column here for a day is not that he thinks a day is not worth money, and that every day of the week is worth money in this yard and is paid at about four, and that what he meant is that the thing on that board is a day that somebody else did not get and not a day this yard has done and not done, and that a sheet with twenty-six lines on it is a sheet of work already done; that about four of that nine spoke and about four said nothing and nobody helped him with the board, and that is the ordinary way a thing gets left standing in that lane and he has stopped expecting otherwise.*
