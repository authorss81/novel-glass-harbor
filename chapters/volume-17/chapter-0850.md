# Chapter 850

*Thursday the Seventeenth Day of November, the Fortieth Day, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Second Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Argument Not Being Resumed at Any Point in About Nine People on This Thursday, and at About Four in That Yard the Man of About Thirty-Two and the Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying Four Words Between Them Which Are Not About the Rule and Neither of Which Is Written Down by Either of Them, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty-Three and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Eighty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Thursday the seventeenth day of November, with that market open from six and a morning with a hard blue look out of the north-east and a frost on that cobble lane that had not been there on Monday and stayed in the shade all day, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe is on Sunday the twentieth of November and the next charge is on the morning of Monday the twenty-first day of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-second day of November. That butt was charged at about nine and it was the two hundred and second time and it took fourteen trips by hand and not one of them was walked on. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixty-third days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fortieth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and forty-eighth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fiftieth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and eighty-eighth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fifty-fourth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventy-second day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and twenty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and forty-second day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and fifty-ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninetieth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-one days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its forty-ninth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its forty-eighth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and forty-eighth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about nine that butt was charged for the two hundred and second time and it took fourteen trips by hand and nobody walked on a single one of them, and the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried one of them and then stood at that door for the rest of that morning and asked nobody for anything, and that is the sixteenth day he has been in that harbor.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was two boats off that mole before noon with that frost still in the shade of it and a mooring ring set in the stone at that mole head and the cold coming off the iron off it, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their sixtieth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had stopped expecting anything out of that shed and that a fortnight of expecting things off a shed is enough of it for anybody.

---

Nobody argued about a page in that shed on this Thursday.

About four of that nine said out loud at about one that they had noticed on Wednesday morning that they were no longer listening out for it, and that about four of those nine had not been able to say when they stopped, and that nobody had said out loud that they were going to stop.

The keeper said out loud that he has been keeping a slate at a stall for a long time and that he has noticed the same thing about this harbor as about himself, and that a thing which is asked out loud every day stops being heard inside a week, and that the whole of what happened on Tuesday at two happened because nobody in that shed had heard the two of them in about four days before it.

Nobody asked him what he meant by that and he was not asked one question about it.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundredth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one and was in that shed from about one until about three. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about three and said nothing out loud at any point in it, and neither of them said anything to the other one about Tuesday or about Wednesday or about any of it.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those twelve was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

At about four, in that yard, at the end of that lane, the man of about thirty-two said "Kettle's boiling." and the woman of thirty, who holds no office, said "I know."

That is four words between them and about four of those nine were inside that shed at the time and not one of them heard either of those two say either of them.

Neither of them wrote either of those words down. Neither of them has written one word of this fortnight down on anything, and the other's page carries tonight nobody's hand but the one that has been on it all fortnight, and that sheet with the fifteen gaps in it is where it stood in September, and there is no fifth kind of thing on the wall of that room over the chandler's store, and the two of them went up that lane together at about ten past four and were in that house before the light went.

They are still married. That is the whole of what that Thursday says about it.

---

About half past five on that Thursday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and sixty words, and that butt came first in it, the forty days came in the middle of it, and the four columns came at the end of it, and the four words said at about four in that yard are not in this reading and are not in any column and are not in this run of fifty days anywhere except on the face of this day.

"**From about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundredth day of that run. The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one and was in that shed from about one until about three, and he was in that shed with her from about one until about three and said nothing out loud at any point in it.**"

"**At about four, in that yard, at the end of that lane, the man of about thirty-two said that the kettle was boiling and the woman of thirty, who holds no office, said that she knew. That is four words between them and about four of those nine were inside that shed at the time and not one of them heard either of those two say either of them at all that day.**"

"**Neither of them wrote either of those words down, and not one of the two of them has ever put their own hand on the other's page, and that sheet with the fifteen gaps in it is where it stood in September, and there is no fifth kind of thing on the wall of that room over the chandler's store, and the two of them went up that lane together at about ten past four and were in that house before the light went off that cobble.**"

"**Twelve hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left that harbor that day. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelfth pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings.**"

"**Two hundred and sixty-third days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty days and is neither shut nor struck. Eighty-one days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its seventy-sixth day.**"

"**It is three hundred and forty-eighth days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and forty days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for four words said at about four in that yard with nobody in it but the two of them, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had sixty-seven lines on it.

THE COUNT OF THE SECOND READING STANDS AT SIXTEEN AND WAS TAKEN ONCE ON THE THIRTIETH DAY AND IS NOT TAKEN AGAIN. NEITHER SIGNED THE OTHER'S PAGE AND THE TWO OF THEM ARE STILL MARRIED AND FOUR WORDS WERE SAID AT ABOUT FOUR IN THAT YARD AND NEITHER OF THEM WROTE EITHER OF THEM DOWN. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-THIRD DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**
