# Chapter 823

*Friday the Twenty-First Day of October, the Thirteenth Day, the Line of the Nineteenth Settling at About Four and the Foot of That Sheet Read After the Settlement and Standing at One Hundred and Twenty-Nine Thousand Eight Hundred and Ninety-Four Out and One Hundred and Nineteen Thousand Two Hundred and Fifty-Four Short, the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud in About Four People at About Three That He Could Put Four Shillings in His Coat and Walk up That Road and Is Not Going To, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Friday the twenty-first day of October, with that market open from six and a hard clear morning with the ground hard and white at ten and the sea a long grey swell running in under a sky that had no weather in it at all, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Friday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the twenty-third and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-fifth. The line worked on Wednesday the nineteenth settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet at about four today and the total at that foot is read after the settlement and not before it, and it stands at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirty-sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twenty-first day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-third day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixty-first day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-seventh night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-fifth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundredth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and fifteenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-second day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-third day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-fourth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-second day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-first day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-one days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

On that Friday the ordinary work was three boats off that mole before noon on that long grey swell and a hatches out of the water by three in the afternoon, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews came to their thirty-third day and had the whole of the day's work off in about an hour and three quarters with the man of about thirty-two over them, which is what they did on their twenty-ninth day and a quarter of an hour less than they did last Saturday. About four of that nine have not come back since the second week and about four are still there.

The board at that end stall has been in the weather since Thursday morning and the top of the six hundred and seventy-two has run down the face of it and stopped about a hand's width above the forty-eight, and the man of about fifty-eight of that market looked at it at about ten and said out loud that it would go in about a week and that he was not going to chalk it up again, and went and did his own work.

---

The line settled at about four. The keeper wrote the figure into the foot of that sheet in front of about four people, being one thousand three hundred and four pence, being eight marks and two shillings, and read the two figures of the foot afterwards and not before.

One hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety added to one thousand three hundred and four is one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four. One hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four less ten thousand six hundred and forty is one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four. Both of those were said out loud and both of those are on the face of that sheet in the keeper's hand and both of those will stand there on Monday morning.

---

At about three, in that yard, in about four people, the man of about thirty-two said a thing out loud about what he could do and about what he was not going to do.

He said that a man-day in that yard is sixteen pence and that three of them is forty-eight pence, being four shillings, and that the whole of what is owed to one man four days up that coast road for three days he did not get is four shillings and a bit over. He said that he could put four shillings in his coat this afternoon and be on that board by Thursday morning, and that he has thought about it since about half past eleven and has not stopped thinking about it.

He said that he is not going to, and that the reason is not that four shillings is nothing, and that it is that the four hundred people four days up that coast road have a figure in chalk on a board that any one of them can walk up to and read and any one of them can ask the next person about, and that if a man in a wet coat from this harbor came up that road and wrote four shillings and his own name under it, then the two things on that board would be a figure they could check and a figure they could not, and that the second one would be the one they would talk about, and that he has been the person a page-holder looks at when a page has a gap in it for about two years and he is not going to be the reason the whole of a community stops being able to check anything.

About three of that four said nothing. One of that four said out loud that it was the first sensible thing anybody had said about that board in four days, and nobody said anything else.

The man of about thirty-two went out to the end of that mole after that and worked until five and came in with his hands cold.

---

That yard had the woman of thirty of no office in it from about eleven until about three with her own page, and she had gone out of it a half hour before three. He came into it about half an hour before that and stayed in it until five. No room with the other one and a page in it came up on that day, not one of them wrote on the other's paper, and forty-eight pence was not said by either of them to the other.

From ten until two that bench belonged to the man of about thirty-four of the second lay and not one person asked him anything in that time, and this is the seventy-third day of it.

Eight hands went across three sheets that left that harbor that day, none of the eight being the hand of whoever wrote the thing the hand went against, and the page of days carries eight against this day.

---

About half past five on that Friday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand one hundred and three words, and the ordinary work came first in it, the man of about thirty-two came in the middle of it, and the settlement came at the end of it, and nothing in this reading wants counting.

Two hundred and thirty-sixth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. "**Nothing on that wall has moved. Those two sheets are on their fifty-fourth day a hand's breadth apart at one size and that wrong figure is left showing under the correction and is not struck out, the leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its forty-ninth day, the fifteen-gap sheet is at fifteen, and the wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has been bare since the fourteenth of August. It is three hundred and twenty-one days since a sound was got in the left side of the head of that man of about twenty-nine, no figure is set against that figure and no work is set against it either. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls are lying about two sea miles off that mole head and a thirteenth day has gone by here without a word from anybody in this harbor about any of them.**"

Nobody was thanked for four shillings that stayed in a coat, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty lines on it.

THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Friday the twenty-first day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirteenth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Friday and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that the ordinary work of that said Friday was three boats off that mole before noon and a hatches out of the water by three, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their thirty-third day and had the whole of the day's work off in about an hour and three quarters; that the board at that end stall has been in the weather since Thursday morning and the top of the six hundred and seventy-two has run down the face of it and stopped about a hand's width above the forty-eight, and that the man of about fifty-eight of that market said out loud at about ten that it would go in about a week and that he was not going to chalk it up again; that at about three in that yard in about four people the man of about thirty-two said out loud that a man-day in that yard is sixteen pence and that three of them is forty-eight pence, being four shillings, and that the whole of what is owed to one man four days up that coast road for three days he did not get is four shillings and a bit over; that he said out loud that he could put four shillings in his coat that afternoon and be on that board by Thursday morning and that he is not going to, and that the reason is that a figure four hundred people can walk up to and check is worth more to them than a figure with a name on it from a harbor four days down the road; that about three of that four said nothing and one of that four said out loud that it was the first sensible thing anybody had said about that board in four days; that the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about eleven until about three and was gone out of it a half hour before three, and that the man of about thirty-two was in that yard at three, and that a room with the other one and a page in it did not come up and neither of them signed the other's page; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything, and that it is the seventy-third day of that run of days; that eight hands went onto three sheets that left that harbor on that day and that no one of the eight wrote any of them; that the line worked on the nineteenth settled into the foot of that sheet at about four and the total at that foot was read after the settlement, and that one hundred and twenty-eight thousand five hundred and ninety added to one thousand three hundred and four is one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; and that the two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-fourth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the man of about thirty-two turned down the one thing he could do that week and did not say why in the way people usually say why, and that the reason he gave instead is the one he could stand behind, being that a figure with a name under it in another community is not a figure anybody there can check, and that he knows that because he has spent two years being looked at by people who wanted him to explain a figure he had never seen; that the reason nobody in that four said anything about the forty-eight pence is that four shillings is not a sum that makes a noise, and that a man who says out loud in four people that he is owed four shillings is heard and a man who says it out loud in nine is not, and that this yard has four years of the second thing in it and no way of checking which is which; that the board at that end stall running in the weather is not a mistake and nobody wiped it and nobody will, and that a figure that survives a week of rain is worth more to that lane than a fair copy nailed inside a shed, because a copy cannot be compared with anything by anybody who was not there when it was written; that the man of about fifty-eight of that market said out loud that he is not going to chalk it up again, and that the reason he is not is that if he chalks it up again it becomes his handwriting twice over and a second hand that is his own is not a second hand at all, and that he has thought that out and it took him nine years.*
