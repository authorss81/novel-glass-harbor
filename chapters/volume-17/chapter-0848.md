# Chapter 848

*Tuesday the Fifteenth Day of November, the Thirty-Eighth Day, the Hundred and Seventh Wage Tuesday, the Chest Shut from Ten Until Four and the Column at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moving, the Woman of Thirty of No Office Writing at About Two in About Nine People the Second Line at the Foot of a Page That Has Ever Been Written in This Harbor and the First One Written by Anybody Who Keeps a Page, in Her Own Hand and Naming Herself, With the Man of About Thirty-Two in That Room Not Holding the Sheet and Not Reading It Before It Was Finished and Not Reading It After, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty-One and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Seventy-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Tuesday the fifteenth day of November, with that market open from six and a sky the colour of an old pewter and a wind that came about at ten and stayed in the east, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is a wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe, and the next charge is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wipe is on Sunday the twentieth of November and the wage Tuesday after this one is on the morning of Tuesday the twenty-second day of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-one thousand seven hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-three marks, five shillings and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand one hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-six marks, twelve shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixty-first days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-eighth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and forty-sixth day and nobody has answered it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and forty-eighth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and eighty-sixth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and fifty-second night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventieth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and twenty-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fortieth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and fifty-seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and eighty-eighth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy-nine days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its forty-seventh day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its forty-sixth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-eight days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and forty-sixth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut on that step from ten until four for the seventh wage Tuesday running, and not one person in that yard so much as laid a hand on that lid, and the column on that lid stands where it stood a week ago at this same hour, at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and the hundred and seventh week is owing on every one of those twenty-six lines.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon with that east wind coming on it and a shackle pin driven out and a new one of brass driven in at that bench with a drift on it, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their fifty-eighth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had stopped waiting altogether and were doing the day's work instead, which they said was the same as waiting with better manners twice over.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the keeper said out loud that there is a drawer in the corner of that room over that chandler's store.

He said out loud that it is not opened on any day of this run of days, and that he will say nothing whatever anybody asks him, and that about four of that nine heard him say that and that nobody asked him a second thing about it.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, the woman of thirty, who holds no office, took a page of her own out of her coat and put it on that bench stone in front of her and wrote at the foot of it.

She wrote it herself and she wrote it in her own hand and she asked nobody to hold it and nobody held it. She wrote for about three minutes and about nine people stood round and not one of them asked to see it while she was writing it and not one of them asked to see it after.

The whole of what she wrote at the foot of that page is this. **Four hundred and six pence, the first day of October, in that community's own hand. Read four days up that coast road as what a crossing costs, and the boat was not hired. The woman of thirty, who holds no office, who keeps this page.**

The man of about thirty-two was in that room the whole of those three minutes and stood the width of that bench stone off from her with his hands in the pockets of his coat, and he did not read it before it was finished and he did not read it after, and he has not asked since what it says.

She did not read it back to herself either. She turned it over and put it under the page of her own and went out of that shed and up that lane at about ten past two, and nobody stopped her at the door and nobody said one word about it.

Going up that lane she thought, without saying one word of it out loud, that her own name at the foot of a page is the one thing about that page which cannot be put back into the column it came off, and that this is the whole of what she has wanted since the first day of October and is not a small thing to want, and that on Sunday, in about nine people, he had said that a man whose name goes under a figure is gone by Monday morning, and that she had heard the whole of it and had given him nothing back at the time, and that there was nothing she could have given him then that would not have been the answer to some other matter, and that thirty-eight days of keeping the harder half of it is what she has for an afternoon like this one.

About four of that nine said nothing at all.

---

At about half past three the man of about thirty-two stood in the doorway of that shed for about a minute and then went and sat down at that far end of that bench with the page of routes in front of him and did not read it, and thought, without saying one word of it out loud, that a woman who has just put her own name under a figure in a hand that a stranger can read, and that he is the man in this harbor who said out loud in about nine people on Sunday that a name under a figure is a person gone by Monday morning.

He thought that he said it, and that on Wednesday she said a thing back about two pages not being the same page, and that on Friday in about nine people she was asked the same question four times and said she would not decide it in front of about nine people, and that she has now decided it in front of about nine people, and that he has no standing whatever to say one word about that in either direction.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the ninety-eighth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Eight hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those eight was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries eight against this day.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and fifty words, and the wage Tuesday came first in it, the three minutes at that bench stone came in the middle of it, and the four columns came at the end of it, and the whole of what she wrote is not in this reading because nobody read it out and the keeper does not read out a thing he has not been asked to read out.

Nobody was thanked for a woman naming herself at the foot of her own page with nine people standing round her and not one of them reading it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had sixty-five lines on it.

THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FIRST DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**
