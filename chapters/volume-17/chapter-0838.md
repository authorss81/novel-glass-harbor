# Chapter 838

*Saturday the Fifth Day of November, the Twenty-Eighth Day, an Ordinary Working Saturday, a Man of About Forty-One of That Market Who Does Odd Carrying with a Handcart Saying a Thing Quietly at About Two in the Room Over That Fish Market in About Four People and the Man of About Thirty-Two Not Hearing It and Asking Him to Say It Again, and Nobody Asking Him One Further Question About It and Nobody Putting Him on a Step, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Seventy-One and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Twenty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Twenty-Seven*

Saturday the fifth day of November, with that market open from six and the first of that clean swell still running in under that mole and a lane so full at about eleven that two stalls had to put their boards out sideways, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Saturday, and there is no charge on that cistern in it and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next wipe is on the morning of tomorrow and the next charge is on the morning of Monday and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the eighth of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifty-first days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-eighth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood five hundred and thirty-six days and nobody has answered it. Nobody has put a question to the bellwright of this harbor for five hundred and thirty-eighth days. The two objects on that crown that belong to nobody are into the eight hundred and seventy-sixth day. That bench under that pump stood bare on its six hundred and forty-second night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and sixtieth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and fifteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and thirtieth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventy-eighth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood sixty-nine days together with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-seventh day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirty-sixth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-eight days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-six days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

On that Saturday the ordinary work was four boats off that mole before noon in a big lane and a coil of line measured off against a gauge, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews stood at their forty-eighth day and took the day's work off in about two hours in the middle of all that, and about four of that nine said that a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place was in that yard on Saturday for the fourth day running and had not asked anybody for anything.

---

At about two, in the room over that fish market, in about four people, a man of about forty-one of that market who does odd carrying with a handcart said a thing quietly.

He said that for about nine years he has written figures out of one person's book into another person's book for nothing, because he cannot read the figures and can copy them, and that about four people in this harbor have been doing it for him in that nine years and about four have forgotten they had.

He said that in September he put one figure into the wrong line in one of those books, and that he found out on the twenty-eighth of September, and that he has not said one word about it to anybody in this harbor since and has not slept properly about it since either.

The man of about thirty-two did not hear it. He was on that side of his head and he was looking at the wall of that room at the time and the man of about forty-one said it the way a person says a thing they are ashamed of, which is quietly, and nobody said it twice of their own accord.

He asked him, in the ordinary way, to say that again.

The man of about forty-one said it again. He said the same thing in the same words at the same volume, and he did not say it louder, and he did not explain it any further, and about four of that four heard the whole of it both times.

Nobody in that room asked him one further question about it. Nobody put him on a step and nobody asked him to stand up and nobody asked him his name and nobody asked him where he had learned to copy.

The man of about forty-one went back down to that lane at about a quarter past two and pushed his handcart up it and about four of that four watched him go out of the door of that room without saying one word to each other about it.

No figure of money and no figure of days and no figure of any kind was set against the asking to say that again, and it is not priced and it is not improved in this run of days and it is never to be set against the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two, which is a different thing.

---

At about four the man of about thirty-two was told what had been said in that room at two and by whom, and he said nothing about it and did not do anything about it, and he has not done anything about it since.

At about half past four he sat down at the far end of that bench with the page of routes in front of him and thought, and did not say out loud, that the man of about forty-one is the only person anybody has found in twenty-eight days who has done the thing that was done four days up that coast road, and that he came down that road on his own two feet without being asked and without wanting anything.

He thought about the nine years of it as well, and about the fact that nine years was a figure that had been standing in that room at two with nobody saying one word about it, and that a person can carry a thing that long and go on calling it nothing in particular.

From ten until two that bench was sat at by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay and nobody spoke to him, which makes the eighty-eighth day of that run of days he has not missed.

At about half past four a woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing read one of those eleven sheets over again on that shelf, and nobody asked her which one and nobody asked her why.

The woman of thirty of no office was not in that harbor today and was three days up that coast road, and the man of about thirty-two did not write one word of what was said in that room on anything.

Four hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those four is the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries four against this day.
