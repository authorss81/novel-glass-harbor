# Chapter 860

*Sunday the Twenty-Seventh Day of November, the Fiftieth and Last Day of This Run, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-Fourth Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Thirty-Eight Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One and Not Going Up Whole, Fifty-One Off and Fifty-One Back and One Hundred and Forty the Whole of It, the Seventh Line Settling at About Four on That Fitting-Out Sheet and the Whole of That Sheet Read After the Settlement and Not Before It, the Foot Standing at One Hundred and Thirty-Three Thousand One Hundred and Eighty-Eight Pence Out and One Hundred and Twenty-Two Thousand Five Hundred and Forty-Eight Pence Short, One Sheet Going into the Drawer in the Corner of That Room Over That Chandler's Store at About Half Past Three and the Outside of That Drawer Written on Again in a Keeper's Hand, the Column of the Put Back Taken Once and in Writing at About Six and Not Read Out in That Shed and Not in the Half-Past-Five Slip, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety-Three and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Thirty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November, with that market shut until seven on a Sunday and a dirty sky out of the west-south-west with the flat water of the day before gone back into a chop by eight, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is the fiftieth and last day of this run and is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage, and nothing at all falls after this day inside this run of fifty days, the next wipe, the next charge on that cistern and the next wage Tuesday being all of them past its end. The wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare until a quarter to one and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at fifty-one minutes off and fifty-one back and thirty-eight standing bare, one hundred and forty the whole of it and a minute longer at each end than it ran the time before. That seventh line settled at about four and the whole of that fitting-out sheet was read after that settlement and not before it, and the foot of that sheet stands at one hundred and thirty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and thirty-two marks, five shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and twenty-two thousand five hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and sixty-five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventy-third days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fiftieth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-eighth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixtieth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-eighth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixty-fourth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eighty-second day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fifty-second day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundredth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety-one days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-ninth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-eighth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in fifty days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifty-eighth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and four people stood in front of it from the moment it came off, counting. One of the four was the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, and that is the fourth time he has watched it go and on every one of the four he has kept his mouth shut.

Bare for thirty-eight minutes, and back at a quarter to one with the top course of it left short and one whole face of it showing the mortar behind, and that is the hundred and thirty-fourth time anybody has done it to that wall. Both ends took a minute longer than the two ends of the last one took. Nobody on that step said a word about either end of it and nobody has yet asked.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon in that chop and the last of the winter blocks strapped down and a bucket turned over out of the rain at that bench, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their seventieth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that that was the last day of that run of seventy and that nobody had said out loud what the seventy-first would be for.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine asked the keeper what that sixth column down the side of that leaf comes to.

He said out loud that he is going to add it up once, in writing, at about six this evening, and that he is not going to read it out at that stone standing up and is not going to say the number out loud to anybody this month.

About four of that nine said out loud that they would be back at about six and would read it off the side of that leaf themselves, and that about four of those nine said out loud that they had not told anybody what they thought it came to and were not going to.

Nobody asked him a second thing about it.

---

At about half past three the keeper put one sheet into the drawer in the corner of that room over that chandler's store, and that sheet is the rule with six lines on it, and the fifth of those lines went in there five weeks ago.

He wrote on the outside of that drawer again, in his own hand, what is in it and the day it went in, and he wrote over the outside of it where the old writing on it was, and he said out loud to nobody in particular that the outside of it had been out of date since Wednesday night.

Nobody in that room put one question to him about drawers and nobody asked him anything else either. The wall of that room is bare and has had nothing put on it for ninety-five days.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and tenth day of that run of days he has not missed, and on the fiftieth day of a run of fifty days nobody in that lane noticed it either.

At about four that seventh line settled, and about four of that nine stood round that bench stone while it was being done, and the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place was not in that room and was two miles up that lane by then.

At about four the two people in that harbor who are married to each other went up that lane together from the end of it, and neither of them said anything about any of it, and the four words that were said in that yard on the Thursday of the week before last were not said again and were not written down by either of them, and they are still married.

---

At about a quarter past four the keeper read the whole of the foot of that fitting-out sheet out loud in about nine people, and he read it after that settlement and not before it, as he has read it on every day there has been one since September.

He read out that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and thirty-two marks, five shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and twenty-two thousand five hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and sixty-five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and that the whole of what that sheet has done in fifty days is go up and not come down. He said out loud that a column gets added up once and in writing and that a foot gets read once and out loud, and that the two of those are different and that this shed has been mixing them up since September.

Nobody wrote that figure down out loud in that shed and nobody repeated it after him. About four of that nine had it before he said it and about four of that nine did not.

---

And at about four, on the flat of that bench stone at the end of that mole, while a foot was being read out loud in about nine people in a yard a long way back from it, a man of about thirty-three who is not in any part of this run of days read the honest total off a page lying face up on that stone and read it out loud to nobody, and nobody asked him why he read it.

that the wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare thirty-eight minutes and went back up at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, at fifty-one off and fifty-one back and one hundred and forty the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-fourth time, and that each of the two ends runs a minute longer than it ran the time before and that nobody in that lane said one word about either end of it and nobody asked; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a count of five in fifty days, on a column with ten days in it and seven of them empty, is what this harbor got out of six weeks of finding out that a page cannot say what a figure was for, and that about four of that nine said on Saturday that they would not say the number out loud in a shed and were right not to; that the reason seven of those ten days stand without a figure in them is that settling a line is not a put back, that nobody has ever read a wage off a page, and that a sheet of rules carrying no figure at all puts nothing back onto anybody, and that the days with nothing in them are the days where about four people could see where a figure would have gone and where they were not going to put it; that the reason the outside of that drawer was written on again tonight and not on Wednesday night is that a keeper who puts his own hand on the outside of a drawer in front of about nine people is showing about nine people what is inside it, and that a thing kept in there cannot be shown to about nine people at any hour; that the reason nobody in that harbor knows why that man of about thirty-three was on that mole at about four is that a page carries no name for a reader and fifty days of this run have been spent proving it, and that he is the whole of what a reader is on a page, which is nothing at all, and that he read it out loud.*
