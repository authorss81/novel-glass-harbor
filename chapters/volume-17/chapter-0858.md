# Chapter 858

*Friday the Twenty-Fifth Day of November, the Forty-Eighth Day, the Seventh Line Worked onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eight in the Morning With the Twenty-Seventh Day of November on the Face of It for the Day It Settles, That Day Being the Fiftieth Day of This Run of Fifty Days, Two Days of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Coal and Oil for Two Days and Sixty Yards of Line and a New Glass for the Lamp at That Mole Head and Four Men at Two Days at Sixteen Pence a Man-Day, the Whole of It One Thousand and Four Pence and Nothing Coming into the Foot of That Sheet Until Sunday, One Figure Entered in the Column Down the Side of That Leaf at About Two at the Figure It Was Given In, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety-One and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Twenty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Friday the twenty-fifth day of November, with that market open from six and a bright cold morning with that frost still hard in the shade and the sea going down at that mole head in long slow sets with no white on it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Friday carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe is on tomorrow, being Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November, and no charge and no wage Tuesday falls again inside this run of fifty days. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was settled on it, that foot standing before this morning's work at one hundred and thirty-two thousand one hundred and eighty-four pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-six marks and two shillings, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand five hundred and forty-four pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-nine marks, eight shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and what was worked on it this morning stands on the face of that line and on nothing else in that yard. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventy-first days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-eighth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-sixth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-eighth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-sixth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixty-second night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eightieth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fiftieth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-eighth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-nine days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-seventh day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-sixth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-eight days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifty-sixth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eight this morning four men went out on that seventh line and it went onto that fitting-out sheet with the twenty-seventh day of November written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is the fiftieth day of this run of fifty days.

Its five parts are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence; coal and oil for two days at forty pence; sixty yards of line at three pence the yard at sixty times three for one hundred and eighty pence; a new glass for the lamp at the head of that mole at two marks, being three hundred and twenty pence; and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at four times two times sixteen for one hundred and twenty-eight pence. The whole of that line is one thousand and four pence, being six marks, three shillings and eightpence, and until that yard settles it at about four on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November it is on the face of that line and nowhere else.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-eighth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that a line with Sunday on the face of it is the last thing this yard will write before that glass is any use to anybody, which is a thing they said out loud and nobody argued with.

The glass went into that lamp at about half past ten and the lamp at the head of that mole has been out since the middle of the summer, and about four of that nine said out loud that a lamp being out is the one thing in that harbor nobody had ever had a column for and that it has just gone onto a fitting-out sheet with a Sunday on the face of it.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine said out loud that this is the seventh line in six weeks and that they could not tell anybody what any of the seven of them was for.

The keeper said out loud that they could all be told, one at a time, by whoever worked them, and that nobody was going to be asked, and that a line this yard works says what it is in its own five parts and that is why nobody has to stand in a shed and be told.

Nobody argued that and nobody asked him a second thing about it.

---

At about two the keeper set one figure down on the page of that week in his own hand at the foot of that day's entry, being three hundred and thirty-six pence for two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, and one figure went into the sixth of those columns and into no other, and he said out loud that that one is on the face of this morning's line at the figure it was given in and has been carried four days up that coast road as what a crossing costs.

About four of that nine said out loud that this is the second day running that the sixth column has had anything in it, and that on Thursday and Tuesday of this week it had nothing in it, and that the keeper said he was not adding it up and was not going to be asked again this month.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and eighth day of that run of days he has not missed.

At about half past three the man of about thirty-two turned the page of that week round twice with the corner of his thumb and did not say out loud what he was looking at, and thought, without saying one word of it out loud, that a line with the last day of a run written on the face of it is the only figure in that harbor that knows today what it is going to be on Sunday, and that a page of days does not do that for anything.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those nine was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

That seventh line lay on that bench stone with the twenty-seventh day of November on the face of it until about five, and it is the last line this yard will settle inside this run of days, and the foot of that sheet has two more days to be read after a settlement on it.

---

About half past five on that Friday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about six hundred and seventy-eight words, and the line came first in it and the figure came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the sixth column down the side of that leaf is not in this reading because the keeper is not adding it up this month.

At about two the keeper set one figure down on the page of that week in his own hand, being three hundred and thirty-six pence for two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, and one figure went into the sixth of those columns and into no other.**"

"**From about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and eighth day of that run. At about half past three the man of about thirty-two turned the page of that week round twice with the corner of his thumb and said nothing out loud about what he was looking at. Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings.**"

"**Two hundred and seventy-first days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-eight days and is neither shut nor struck. Eighty-nine days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its eighty-fourth day.**"

"**The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare. It is three hundred and fifty-sixth days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and forty-eight days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for a lamp at the head of a mole getting its glass back off a fitting-out sheet on a Friday, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy-five lines on it.

---

**THE SEVENTH LINE WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT EIGHT WITH THE FIFTIETH DAY OF THIS RUN ON THE FACE OF IT AND IS ONE THOUSAND AND FOUR PENCE AND DOES NOT TOUCH THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET UNTIL SUNDAY THE TWENTY-SEVENTH DAY OF NOVEMBER. ONE FIGURE WENT INTO THE SIXTH COLUMN DOWN THE SIDE OF THAT LEAF AT ABOUT TWO AND NOBODY ADDED THAT COLUMN UP. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIRST DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

that the seventh line went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight with the twenty-seventh day of November on the face of it for the day it settles, and that that day is the fiftieth day of this run of fifty days; that the glass went into that lamp at about half past ten and that lamp has been out since the middle of the summer; that those four new crews were on their sixty-eighth day; that at about one in that shed in about nine people about four of that nine said out loud that this is the seventh line in six weeks and that they could not tell anybody what any of the seven of them was for; that the keeper said out loud that they could all be told one at a time by whoever worked them and that nobody was going to be asked; that at about two the keeper set one figure down on the page of that week in his own hand, being three hundred and thirty-six pence for two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence, and that one figure went into the sixth of those columns down the side of that leaf and into no other, and that he said out loud that it is on the face of that line at the figure it was given in and has been carried four days up that coast road as what a crossing costs; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and eighth day of that run; that at about half past three the man of about thirty-two turned the page of that week round twice and said nothing out loud about what he was looking at; that nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and that the page of days carries nine against this day; and that that seventh line lay on that bench stone with the twenty-seventh day of November on the face of it until about five.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a lamp at the head of a mole has been out since the middle of the summer because this harbor has four kinds of thing it can put on a wall and a light going out is not one of them, and that on Friday it went onto a fitting-out sheet with a Sunday on the face of it at the price of two marks, and that nobody in that shed remarked on the cheapest thing this yard has done in six weeks being a thing with a date on it; that the reason one figure went into that sixth column at about two and three went into it on Saturday is that a boat's hire for one day and a boat's hire for two days are the same figure and twice that figure, and that this harbor has now put one of them back four times in three days and has put nothing else back at all; that the reason nobody asked to be told what any of the seven lines was for is that four of those people could tell any of them and four more could not, and that asking would have found out which of those two they were; that the reason a line carrying the last day of a run on its face matters is that it is the single thing in that harbor that commits itself in advance, and that six weeks of correct figures have been corrected every single time they were found out.*
