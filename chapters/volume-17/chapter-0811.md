# Chapter 811

*Sunday the Ninth Day of October, the First Day, a Wipe and the Hundred and Twenty-Seventh of Them at Forty-Four Off and Thirty-One Standing Bare and Forty-Four Back Up and One Hundred and Nineteen the Whole of It, a Man of About Fifty-Eight of That Market Asking Out Loud at About Eleven at That End Stall in About Nine People Whether Four Hundred and Six Pence Is What a Crossing Costs and Nobody Answering Him and Him Not Asking Again, No Charge on That Cistern and No Wage Movement, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Forty-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Five Hundred and Ninety-Four*

Sunday the ninth day of October, with that market open from six and a fine still morning with no wind in it and the water glassy at eight and the tide making an hour after, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary Sunday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-four marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and twenty-seven marks, ten shillings and twopence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in and nothing else in it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Sunday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and twenty-fourth days without a tin opened in it, the last having been the twenty-ninth of April past.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this first day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and ninth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and eleventh day of nobody asking him anything, and the chest against his wall stays shut. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and forty-ninth day of themselves. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and fifteenth night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirty-third day and no figure of money stands against either of them. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and eighty-eighth day of its standing. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and third day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twentieth day. On this first day of this run the man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-first day of not speaking about six names. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with an empty space under it, his surname is on no page, and he is off the water for the eighth time and nobody has asked him about it. The fourth of those four stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line since before this run of days began and is neither shut nor struck. The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-second day together at the same size and a hand's breadth apart, and the rule in the inside of that door is on its tenth day, and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the ninth day of being on that community's own page four days up a coast road. The nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor says nothing whatever about them. The left side of the head of the man of about twenty-nine has heard nothing for the three hundred and ninth day and he is not on the water and no figure is set against that figure.**

---

The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before eleven and a hatches out of the water by two in the afternoon, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

The wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten, the way it has come off every Sunday morning since the twelfth of January, and it was forty-four minutes off and thirty-one minutes standing bare and forty-four minutes back up, which is a hundred and nineteen minutes the whole of it, which is three minutes more than the last one was and one minute more at each end than the one before that. Nobody said why. Nobody has said why on any Sunday this harbor can account for, and about four people who were standing there know it has been a minute longer each time and have not said so to anybody.

The man of about thirty-two was at the end of that mole from about seven with the four new crews and the book of hours, and they were on their twenty-first day and did the morning's work in about two hours under him, which is slower than the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay, and about four of that nine said so and about four said nothing. He held one end of the plank the wall goes up on and he did not go near the bare face of it, because there is nothing to hold and he is not a man who goes near a thing for the sake of going near it.

---

At about eleven, under the awning of that end stall, in about nine people, a man of about fifty-eight of that market who keeps the stall and a slate stood with his hands behind him and asked a question out loud.

He said that a sheet had gone up that coast road on the first of October, in a community's own hand, carrying four hundred and six pence on it, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, and that it had ten second hands on the face of it besides that hand. He said that he had seen the figure twice since and both times in a market and never on the page itself. He said that he was not asking anybody to take anything down and that he had no quarrel with anybody. He said that what he wanted to know was whether four hundred and six pence is what a crossing costs.

About nine people were standing there and none of them answered him. One of them was the keeper and one of them was the woman of thirty of no office and one of them was a woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing but has a stall two along.

The silence went on long enough that a woman of about thirty-six further along the row said out loud that there was a person behind her, and the man of about fifty-eight said that was all right, and nobody said anything else then either, and about two of that nine looked at the two sheets hanging on that end stall under the awning, which are the same size and a hand's breadth apart and which have been that size for forty-two days.

He did not ask again. He put the slate down on the boards of the stall with its face down, which is not how he keeps it, and he went back to his own two boards and he did not look at it again that day.

---

About two that afternoon the keeper went up the stairs of that fish market to the long room and did not open the drawer, and came down again, and about four people in that market saw him go up and about four did not.

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and from about three until about four with another, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about five with the page of routes in his hand. Both of them were in that yard on the same day and neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page, and neither of them signed the other's page and neither of them said one word to the other about the eleven words they had said at a bench stone four days before.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about one and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-first day of that run of days, and he has been on that bench every day of it.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those ten was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

About half past five on that Sunday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and twenty-four words, and the ordinary work came first in it and the question at that end stall came in the middle of it and the wipe came last in it, and no count of anything came in it.

"**The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before eleven and a hatches out of the water by two in the afternoon, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it. The four new crews were on their twenty-first day and did the morning's work in about two hours under the man of about thirty-two, which is slower than the man of about twenty-seven, and about four of that nine said so and about four said nothing.**"

That is three minutes more than the last one was and one minute longer at each end than the one before it. Nobody said why. Nobody has said why on any Sunday this harbor can account for, and about four people standing there know it has been a minute longer each time and have not said so to anybody.**"

"**And the rest of it. About two that afternoon the keeper went up the stairs of that fish market to the long room and did not open the drawer and came down again, and about four people in that market saw him go up and about four did not. The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and from about three until about four with another, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about five with the page of routes in his hand, and both of them were in that yard on the same day and neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page and neither of them said one word to the other about the eleven words they had said at a bench stone four days before. A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about one and was not asked anything, and it is the sixty-first day of that run.**"

"**That foot stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and eighty-two pence out and one hundred and sixteen thousand four hundred and forty-two pence short, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and twenty-fourth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and nothing has ever gone under it, that boy's surname is on no page, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of those four is at one hundred and twenty-five and is neither shut nor struck. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen. The wall of the room over that chandler's store is bare and has stood bare since the fourteenth of August and nothing went up on it today and nothing came down off it. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor has closed a day without a word about them.**"

"**A man of about twenty-nine has now gone three hundred and nine days without hearing anything in the left side of his head, no figure is set against that figure, he is not on the water, and the hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced and is not improved and is a different thing from that one and is never set against it. Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, every one of them the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against.**"

Nobody was thanked for a question at a stall or for standing in nine people and not answering one, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had twenty-eight lines on it.

---

**THE WIPE WAS THE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-SEVENTH AND TOOK ONE HUNDRED AND NINETEEN MINUTES. FOUR HUNDRED AND SIX PENCE, BEING TWO MARKS, SEVEN SHILLINGS AND TWOPENCE, IS ON THE NINTH DAY OF A COMMUNITY'S OWN PAGE FOUR DAYS UP A COAST ROAD AND IT IS CORRECT. FOUR PEOPLE ASKED AND FOUR PEOPLE SAID NOTHING. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND TWENTY-FOURTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the ninth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the first entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary Sunday and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was a wipe day, and that the wall behind that reeve's bench came off at about half past ten and stood bare thirty-one minutes and went up again at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, and that the whole of it was one hundred and nineteen minutes, being forty-four off and forty-four back up and thirty-one bare, which is one minute longer at each end than the hundred and twenty-sixth time and three minutes longer in all; that the ordinary work of that said Sunday was two boats off that mole before eleven and a hatches out of the water by two in the afternoon, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it; that the four new crews were on their twenty-first day and did the morning's work in about two hours under the man of about thirty-two, which is slower than the man of about twenty-seven of the second lay; that at about eleven under the awning of that end stall, in about nine people, a man of about fifty-eight of that market who keeps the stall and a slate asked out loud whether four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, is what a crossing costs, and that about nine people were standing there and none of them answered him, and that a woman of about thirty-six further along that row said out loud that there was a person behind her and he said that was all right, and that nobody said anything else then either, and that he put the slate down on the boards with its face down, which is not how he keeps it, and that he did not ask again; that about two that afternoon the keeper went up the stairs of that fish market to the long room and did not open the drawer and came down again; that the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one and from about three until about four and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about five, and that neither of them signed the other's page and neither of them said one word to the other about the eleven words they had said at a bench stone four days before; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about one and was not asked anything, and that it is the sixty-first day of that run of days; that ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, every one of them the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and that the page of days carries ten against this day; and that a hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and that under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody in that stall answered the man of about fifty-eight is that every person standing there had a different answer and that three of the four answers were the truth and that saying one of them out loud would have been putting a figure up for something a wall is not for, and that a thing put up for that is a fifth kind of thing, and this harbor has not got one and did not ask for one on the thirtieth of September either; that the reason he put the slate down face down is that a man who keeps a slate for thirty years carries it the way other men carry a hand, and that when a thing he has carried a long while is put down in a place where he cannot see it, that is not an answer to anybody and is only a thing a body does; that the reason nobody has said why that wall takes a minute longer each way is that everybody in that market has counted it and nobody has put the count anywhere it would stick, and that a count kept in about four heads is not a count that anybody can check, and that this yard does not know what to do with a figure it has no place for.*
