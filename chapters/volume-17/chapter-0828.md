# Chapter 828

*Wednesday the Twenty-Sixth Day of October, the Eighteenth Day, an Ordinary Working Wednesday With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and No Wage Movement in It, a Man of About Twenty-Seven of the Second Lay Putting Down a Length of Line at About One and Saying Out Loud One Thing in About Nine People and Nobody Answering It and It Not Going into Any Column and Not Going into the Half-Past-Five Slip, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-One and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Wednesday the twenty-sixth day of October, with that market open from six and low cloud sitting on that water all day with not a breath in it and the swell going up and down without any of it moving, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Wednesday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wipe is on the morning of Sunday the first of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the first of November. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-first days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this eighteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twenty-sixth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-eighth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixty-sixth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and thirty-second night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fiftieth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twentieth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-eighth day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-ninth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-seventh day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-sixth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-six days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon on a flat sea and a length of coil cut off against a gauge by a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay in about a minute and a half without saying one word while he did it, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews arrived at their thirty-eighth day and had the day's work off in about two hours under the man of about thirty-two, and about four of that nine said that they had stopped waiting to be shown things and had told nobody that they had stopped waiting.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the man of about twenty-seven of that second lay put the length of line down on the bench stone where he had cut it and said out loud, to nobody in particular and to all nine, that a page can be corrected and a use cannot be.

He said out loud then that he wanted to know who is owed the difference between what a figure says and what somebody spent it on. He said it once, in the ordinary way, without stopping what he was doing and without waiting for anybody to catch up with him. He did not say it again and he did not say it a second time louder, and he picked the line back up and went on with it.

About nine people heard all of it. Nobody answered it.

About four of that nine went straight on with what they were doing, which is the ordinary thing to do at about one in that yard. One of that four, the woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing, said out loud about an hour afterwards and to nobody in particular that she has wanted to say something like that for about a year and that she is glad somebody else said it and that it makes no difference whatever to anything she has to do in the morning. The man of about twenty-seven of that second lay said nothing back to that and went on cutting line.

Nobody repeated it after him. Nobody wrote it down. The man of about thirty-two was at the end of that mole at one and was not told until about four, and at about four he was told that a man in that shed had said one thing at one o'clock and that nobody had answered it, and he said nothing about it and went back to the page of routes.

At about two, that weather being as flat as it was, the nine buoys off that mole head answered one another the way they do about twice a day, one after another and not all together, and the sound came in over that water and went in under the talk in that shed. About nine of those who were in it heard the whole run of them without counting any. Nobody in that shed has said out loud at any time that those nine sounds make one sound and nobody has ever tried to make them.

And nothing else in this harbor has said one word in eighteen days about nine hulls lying about two sea miles off that mole head, and nobody who goes down that mole every day of the working week has counted them again since the first week of this run of days.

---

The woman of thirty of no office had that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and was in the room over that fish market from three until about four. The man of about thirty-two came into that shed at three and stayed until five. Neither of them was in that shed at one and neither of them heard a word of it and neither of them raised it with the other.

Nobody put a question to that man of about thirty-four of the second lay between ten and two on that Wednesday and he was at that bench for the whole of it, being the seventy-eighth day of that run.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor on that Wednesday, every one of the ten the hand of a person who is not the person who wrote it, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

About half past five on that Wednesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and sixty-one words, and the ordinary work came first in it and the man of about twenty-seven came in the middle of it, and one thing said at about one in that yard is not in this reading and is not going into any column.

"**From about ten until about one the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard with her own page, and she was in the room over that fish market from three until about four, and the man of about thirty-two came into that shed at three and stayed until five, and neither of them was in that shed at one and neither of them heard a word of it.**"

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left buildings in that harbor, and the ten are ten people and those three sheets were written by other people.**"

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and forty-first days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it.**"

"**That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in eighteen days and is neither shut nor struck. Fifty-nine days now of that pair standing where they stand, one size, a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its fifty-fourth day. The fifteen-gap sheet is at fifteen. That wall in the room over that chandler's store has been bare and empty since the fourteenth of August and nothing has gone up on it.**"

"**That man of about twenty-nine is three hundred and twenty-six days into having nothing come out of the left of his head, and nothing whatever is set against that figure, and he has not been on the water at any point in that time. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor is on its eighteenth day of not saying a word about that.**"

Nobody was thanked for saying one thing out loud at about one on a Wednesday, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty-five lines on it.

**A MAN OF ABOUT TWENTY-SEVEN OF THE SECOND LAY, WHO KEEPS NO RECORD AND HOLDS NO OFFICE, SAID ONE THING OUT LOUD AT ABOUT ONE IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE AND WAS ANSWERED BY NOBODY, AND IT IS IN NO COLUMN OF THIS YARD AND IT IS NOT IN THE HALF-PAST-FIVE SLIP AND IT WILL NOT BE PUT ANYWHERE ELSE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-FIRST DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-sixth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the eighteenth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Wednesday and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that the ordinary work of that said Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon and a length of coil cut off against a gauge by a man of about twenty-seven of that second lay, and that no line went onto that fitting-out sheet; that the four new crews were on their thirty-eighth day and had the day's work off in about two hours; that at about one in that shed in about nine people the man of about twenty-seven of that second lay put down the length of line he had cut and said one thing out loud to nobody in particular and to all nine, and that about nine people heard all of it and nobody answered it; that he said it once and did not say it again and did not say it louder and picked the line back up and went on with it; that about four of that nine went straight on with what they were doing; that a woman of about twenty-nine of that market who sells nothing said out loud about an hour afterwards and to nobody in particular that she has wanted to say something of that kind for about a year and that it makes no difference whatever to anything she has to do in the morning, and that the man of about twenty-seven said nothing back to that; that nobody repeated it after him and nobody wrote it down and no column of this yard carries it; that the man of about thirty-two was at the end of that mole at one and was not told until about four and said nothing about it when he was told; that the woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one and was in the room over that fish market from about three until about four, and that the man of about thirty-two came into that shed at about three and stayed until about five, and that neither of them was in that shed at one and neither of them heard a word of it; that a man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything, and that it is the seventy-eighth day of that run of days; that ten hands went onto three sheets that left buildings in that harbor, and that the ten are not the hands that wrote them; that nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on the said day and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; and that the two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-ninth day at the same size and a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure left showing underneath the correction and not struck out.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that one thing was said once in about nine people at about one on that Wednesday and is not written down here in any column and is not in the half-past-five slip, and that the reason for leaving it out of both is that this yard has spent five weeks making a point of the opposite, being that a thing a stranger can read off a page afterwards is worth more than the same thing said once in a shed and then gone, and that carrying this one across and putting it on the page would turn the whole of the last eighteen days into a list; that the reason he was not told until about four is that about four of that nine did not tell him and about four of that nine did not think it was anything, and that a thing which is said once in nine people and not repeated is not news by four o'clock and never becomes news; that what the woman of about twenty-nine who sells nothing was glad about was that somebody else had said it, and that the reason for that gladness is that a person can count herself out of a page for about a year and has no way of putting that count anywhere, and that being told that somebody else sees the shape of it changes nothing about her own Thursday and everything about whether she is standing in the right place.*
