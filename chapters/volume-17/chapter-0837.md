# Chapter 837

*Friday the Fourth Day of November, the Twenty-Seventh Day, an Ordinary Working Friday, a Man of About Forty-Six of the Second Lay Writing at About Two in About Nine People the First Line at the Foot of a Page That Has Ever Been Written in This Harbor with the Man of About Thirty-Two Holding the Sheet, What He Writes Being What the Man of About Thirty-Two Expected, and the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud at About Three in About Four People That Holding a Sheet While a Person Writes on It Is a Way of Telling That Person What the Page Is For, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Seventy and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Twenty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Twenty-Two*

Friday the fourth day of November, with that market open from six and a fine hard clear morning with that confused sea gone by midnight and about two sea miles of clean swell coming in under the mole from the north-west, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Friday, and there is no charge on that cistern in it and no wipe in it and nothing on it that moves on a wage, and the next charge is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe falls on Sunday the sixth of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday the eighth of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fiftieth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood five hundred and thirty-five days and nobody has answered it. Nobody has put a question to the bellwright of this harbor for five hundred and thirty-seventh days. The two objects on that crown that belong to nobody are into the eight hundred and seventy-fifth day. That bench under that pump stood bare on its six hundred and forty-first night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-ninth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and fourteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-ninth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-sixth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventy-seventh day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood sixty-eight days together with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-sixth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirty-fifth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-seven days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-five days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

On that Friday the yard's ordinary work was three boats off that mole before noon in that clear light and a coil of line measured off against a gauge with that door propped on a brick, and neither of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and no sum at all is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their forty-seventh day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said that the man of about forty-six of the second lay had come in at about half past seven and had not said one word to anybody since.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, the man of about forty-six of the second lay took a page of his own out of his coat and asked for the sheet to be held.

He said out loud that he cannot read his own writing back, and that a thing written at the far end of that bench by a man who cannot read his own writing is a thing nobody will ever be able to check, and that whoever holds the sheet is holding it, and that he does not care who that is.

The man of about thirty-two held the sheet. Nobody asked him to and nobody asked the man of about forty-six to let him hold it, and about four of that nine said afterwards that they had not known at the time which of the two men had decided anything.

The man of about forty-six wrote at the foot of that page of his own, in about four minutes, in his own hand, with the stub of a pencil, and the whole of it is this. **Two bell seats cut and faced, the twenty-ninth day of October, at eighteen pence a seat, thirty-six pence. Off a page in this yard. The man of about forty-six of the second lay.**

There is nothing on that page of his own under that line. There is nothing above it either except eleven years of figures taken off other people's pages.

---

At about three, in that shed, in about four people, the man of about thirty-two said three things out loud and they were said in the ordinary voice and not to anybody in particular.

He said out loud that what is written on that page of his own is what the man of about thirty-two expected to find written there, and that he can tell the difference between those two things because he was standing over the sheet with it in his hand while it was written, and that a man holding a sheet is telling the man who is writing on it what the sheet is for.

He said out loud that on Wednesday, when a man of about thirty-one said that a figure was copied twice on its way up a coast road, that was the same telling again in the ordinary way, a page going out with the person holding it, and that the person holding it is the page.

He said out loud that the line that is written there does not say whose page it was and that the not saying it is what the man of about forty-six did when the man of about thirty-two was holding it, and that it is wrong, and that he knew it would be wrong before he took hold of the sheet and he took hold of the sheet, and that nobody in this harbor is going to put that right this month.

About four of that nine heard all three of those sentences. Nobody repeated any of them. Nothing whatever is set against the hearing in the left side of that man and it is not priced and it is not improved by anything said at three o'clock on a Friday.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person asked him a thing, which makes the eighty-seventh day of that run of days he has not missed.

At about half past three one of those four new crews read one of those eleven sheets over again on that shelf, and at about four the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place read one of them over again, and neither of them said which one and neither of them was asked.

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and went up that coast road at one, and she was not in that shed at any point between two and four.

Thirteen hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left that harbor that day, no hand among the thirteen the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries thirteen against this day.

---

THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTIETH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Friday the fourth day of November at about six in the evening in that shed, being the twenty-seventh entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Friday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe falls on Sunday the sixth of November; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a man who cannot read his own writing back asked for a sheet to be held and did not ask who was holding it, and that this is the first time in twenty-seven days that anybody in this harbor has asked another person to take hold of a page, and that the reason is that a line put at the foot of a page is worth nothing at all if the man who wrote it cannot tell what he wrote, and that it took eleven years of copying figures off other people's pages before a man came to that; that the man of about thirty-two took the sheet because he was nearest and that about four of those nine have said since that at the time they did not know which of the two men had decided anything, and that is the ordinary shape of a thing done in a working place without anybody meaning to do it; that the reason the line as written does not say whose page the figure came off is that a man standing over the sheet with it in his hand is telling the man who is writing on it what the sheet is for, and that what the man of about thirty-two expected a page to be for on that Friday was for showing where a figure came from, and that a page of eleven years of other people's figures has no such thing to show; that the man of about thirty-two said it out loud, that the line is wrong, and that he knew it would be wrong before he took hold of the sheet is that a thing said by a page-holder about his own holding of a sheet is the only kind of correction this month that nobody can refuse, and that it will not be repaired, and that nothing in this harbor is going to make it so.*
