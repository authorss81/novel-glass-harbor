# Chapter 817

*Saturday the Fifteenth Day of October, the Seventh Day, a Second Line Worked This Morning With the Seventeenth of October on Its Face for the Day It Settles, Four New Crews on Their Twenty-Seventh Day, the Keeper Saying Out Loud at About One That a Figure in Chalk Four Days up a Coast Road Is Not a Page and Cannot Go on Any of the Four Walls, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Fifty and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Saturday the fifteenth day of October, with that market open from six and a heavy sky with the swell still running and the water the colour of a slate, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Saturday, being a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it and no wage movement in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Monday and the next wipe is on the morning of tomorrow and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of Tuesday. A second line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet today at about eight with the seventeenth of October on its face for the day it settles, and nothing settled into the foot of that sheet today and the foot is not read today, and it stands at one hundred and twenty-seven thousand and seven hundred and sixty-eight pence out, being seven hundred and ninety-eight marks, seven shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and seventeen thousand one hundred and twenty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and thirty-two marks and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten on a Saturday as on any other day and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and thirtieth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and fifteenth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and seventeenth day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and fifty-fifth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and twenty-first night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and thirty-ninth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the three hundred and ninety-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and ninth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and twenty-sixth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and fifty-seventh day of not speaking about six names and did not come down that lane today. The two sheets at that end stall are on their forty-eighth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixteenth day and the honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the fifteenth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifteen days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The second line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight in the morning in the keeper's hand, with the fifteenth on it as the day the work was done and the seventeenth on it as the day it settles, and both are on it because they are not the same day.

The first part is two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at one hundred and sixty-eight pence a day, two times one hundred and sixty-eight, for three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence. The second is coal and oil for two days at forty pence, being three shillings and fourpence. The third is ninety yards of line at three pence the yard, ninety times three, for two hundred and seventy pence, being one mark, nine shillings and twopence. The fourth is four panes of sheet glass at twelve pence a pane, four times twelve, for forty-eight pence, being four shillings. The fifth is four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day, four times two times sixteen, for one hundred and twenty-eight pence, being ten shillings and eightpence.

Three hundred and thirty-six and forty and two hundred and seventy and forty-eight and one hundred and twenty-eight is eight hundred and twenty-two pence, being five marks, one shilling and tenpence. Nothing settles into that foot today and the foot is not read today.

The four new crews were on their twenty-seventh day and did the morning's work in about two hours under the man of about thirty-two and about half an hour of it in rain, and the whole of the twenty-seventh day is the best they have done and about four of that nine said so out loud for the first time.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the keeper said out loud that a figure in chalk four days up a coast road is not a page.

He said that a wall in this harbor takes four kinds of thing, being claims, corrections put up in public, refusals and restricted records, and that all four of them have been used on the wall in the room over that fish market and there is nothing else on it. He said that a figure in chalk on a board four days up a road is a fifth kind of thing and that there is no fifth kind, and that he is not proposing one, and that he said that on purpose and would say it again.

He said that he has been asked twice this week whether that board could be brought down here and written out fair, and that he has said no both times, and that the reason is not pride in this yard and is that a board four days up a road is the only record that community has got and that taking a figure off it and putting it in a drawer here would leave about four hundred people with a figure they cannot check. He said that a figure a stranger can check is worth more than a figure in a better building.

About four of that nine said out loud that that was a poor thing to have found out and about four said nothing and nobody said it was wrong.

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday was four boats off that mole before noon and a hatches out of the water by two in the afternoon, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page and went up that coast road on the one o'clock boat, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about two until about five with the page of routes, and neither of them was in a room with the other one and a page and neither of them said one word to the other about the woman of about thirty-four of the Saltmarch, who is still in that market and is staying another night and has asked for a bed at the back of that shed and has been given the bench at the wall end of it.

A man of about thirty-four of the second lay was on that bench from about ten until about two and was not asked anything by anybody, and it is the sixty-seventh day of that run of days.

Four hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day, and every one of those four was the hand of a person who was not the person who wrote what the hand went against, and the page of days carries four against this day.

---

*First column, entered on Saturday the fifteenth day of October at about six in the evening in that shed, being the seventh entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Saturday and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday and was not a wipe day; that a second line was worked onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight in the keeper's hand with the fifteenth on it as the day the work was done and the seventeenth on it as the day it settles; that the parts of that line are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, ninety yards of line at three pence the yard at ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence, four panes of sheet glass at twelve pence a pane at four times twelve for forty-eight pence, and four men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at four times two times sixteen for one hundred and twenty-eight pence; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the keeper said out loud that there is no fifth kind of thing for a figure in chalk on a board four days up a road, and that the reason for saying it twice over and meaning it both times is that a man who has just said a thing is a man who has been asked for a fifth kind by about four people who meant well, and that the only way to keep a wall a wall is to refuse the extension before it is asked for twice; that the reason he has twice refused to have that board copied into a drawer in this harbor is that a column in this yard is the only kind of record there is that a stranger can come at afterwards and get the same two figures off, and that a figure of four hundred people four days up a road is worth more than the same figure in a better building; that about four of that nine spoke up that afternoon and called it a poor thing to have found out, and that the reason they did is that they had spent the whole of that week hoping for a fifth kind, and that a wall that can be extended by anybody who is angry is not a wall.*
