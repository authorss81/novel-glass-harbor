# Chapter 836

*Thursday the Third Day of November, the Twenty-Sixth Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-Eighth Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Keeper Saying Out Loud at About One in About Nine People That He Is Not Going to Propose a Fifth Kind of Thing for Any Wall in This Harbor, a Man of About Forty-Six of the Second Lay Saying Out Loud at About Two That He Keeps a Page of His Own and Has Written Nothing at the Foot of One in Eleven Years and That He Will Write It Today If Anybody Wants It Written, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Sixty-Nine and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Nineteen and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Fourteen*

Thursday the third day of November, with that market open from six and a wind that came round to the north in the night and left that mole in a confused sea that was doing nothing much in either direction, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday carries a charge on that cistern and carries no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe falls on Sunday the sixth of November and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of next Tuesday. That butt was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-eighth time and it took fourteen trips by hand and not one of them was walked on. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-ninth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has stood five hundred and thirty-four days and nobody has answered it. Nobody has put a question to the bellwright of this harbor for five hundred and thirty-six days. The two objects on that crown that belong to nobody are into the eight hundred and seventy-fourth day. That bench under that pump stood bare on its six hundred and fortieth night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-eighth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and thirteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-fifth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventy-sixth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood sixty-seven days together with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-fifth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirty-fourth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-six days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-four days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about nine that butt was charged for the hundred and ninety-eighth time and it took fourteen trips by hand and nobody walked on any of them, and a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried four of them without being asked.

At about half past twelve that man read one of those eleven sheets over again on the shelf in that room over the sailmaker and told nobody he had been up there.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was one boat off that mole before noon and a bell seat cut and faced at that bench with the shavings going into a bucket and the bucket going into the fire at about three, and that work cost the yard nothing and went onto no sheet of this yard at all.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the keeper said out loud that he is not going to propose a fifth kind of thing for any wall in this harbor.

He said out loud that the four heads on that wall take a claim, a correction, a refusal and a restricted record, and that all four of those have been used in this month, and that what happened on Wednesday is that a thing four days up a coast road was decided on a figure two hands removed from a page in this harbor, and that a wall takes no such thing, and that a use cannot go up anywhere.

He said out loud that he said the same thing in this shed in September about a board in chalk and that about four of that nine remember him saying it, and that he is not going to keep saying it once a week for another twenty-five days, and that on Wednesday he said it in about nine people and was not asked one question about it.

About four of that nine said nothing. Nobody asked him which four kinds of thing a wall takes and nobody asked him what the fifth would have been.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, a man of about forty-six of the second lay said out loud that he keeps a page of his own.

He said out loud that he has kept it about eleven years and that he takes figures off other people's pages onto it every week of his life, and that he has never written one word at the foot of one of those pages saying what he spent it on, and that he has never asked anybody whose page it was he was copying from.

He said out loud that if anybody in that shed wants that written down he will write it today, this afternoon, and that he is not asking anybody to want it, and that he had the thought at about half past twelve and had it again at about one and again at a quarter past one.

The keeper said out loud that nobody has to write anything. Nobody asked the man of about forty-six one question about it and about four of that nine said that they would rather he did not.

From about ten until about two that bench was worked by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay and nobody in that lane asked him one thing, which is the eighty-sixth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Those four new crews stood at their forty-sixth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said that one of them had asked the man of about forty-six whether he could read and that he said he can read slow.

The man of about thirty-one of that place came down that lane again at about four and stood at that door again and nobody stopped him. The woman of thirty of no office was in that yard from about ten until about one with her own page. Eleven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

About half past five on that Thursday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and sixty-one words, and the cistern came first in it, the fifth kind of thing came in the middle of it, and the page of his own came at the end of it, and no count that anybody keeps is in this reading.

Two hundred and forty-ninth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it.**"

"**That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-six days and is neither shut nor struck. Sixty-seven days now of that pair standing at that stall at one size, a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its sixty-second day. The fifteen-gap sheet is at fifteen, and that wall of the room over the chandler's store is bare and has been since the fourteenth of August.**"

"**There has been nothing in the left of that man of about twenty-nine's head for three hundred and thirty-four days, and no figure of money and no day of his life is set against it, and he is not on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor is on its twenty-sixth day of not mentioning them.**"

Nobody was thanked for offering to write something nobody had asked for, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had fifty-three lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHTH CHARGE WAS MADE AND IT WAS FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THE KEEPER SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT ONE THAT HE IS NOT GOING TO PROPOSE A FIFTH KIND OF THING FOR ANY WALL IN THIS HARBOR. A MAN OF ABOUT FORTY-SIX OF THE SECOND LAY OFFERED OUT LOUD TO WRITE AT THE FOOT OF A PAGE OF HIS OWN AND WAS ASKED NOTHING ABOUT IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-NINTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a man offered out loud in about nine people to write at the foot of a page of his own and that nobody in that shed asked him a question about it is the nearest this harbor has come in twenty-six days to somebody offering the thing it wants, and that nobody asked is not an accident but a thing, and that every person in that room who had spoken in the last week had been asked something already, and that being asked a thing makes a person careful about offering the next one; that the reason the keeper said he would not say the thing about the wall a fourth time is that he has said it in September about chalk and in the middle of this month about a chalk board and on Wednesday about a copy of a copy, and that a thing said three times and never asked about becomes a thing said, and that what it needs now is somebody else to say it, and that nobody has; that the reason the man of about forty-six said he can read slow is that a man who reads slow is a man who has been copying figures off other people's pages for eleven years and being careful about them for eleven years, and that this harbor has spent five weeks asking people to be careful on a page and has not once asked one of them what being careful has cost.*
