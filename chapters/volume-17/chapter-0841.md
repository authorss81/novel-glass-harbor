# Chapter 841

*Tuesday the Eighth Day of November, the Thirty-First Day, the Hundred and Sixth Wage Tuesday, the Column on the Lid of That Step Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks With the Hundred and Seventh Week Fallen Due and the Chest Shut from Ten Until Four and the Column Not Moving, the Woman of Thirty of No Office Saying Out Loud at About One in About Nine People That a Page Should Carry What It Was Used For and the Man of About Thirty-Two in the Room Saying Nothing at All, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Seventy-Four and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Forty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Seventy-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Tuesday the eighth day of November, with that market open from six and a low grey sky sitting on that water like the lid of something, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is a wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe, and the next charge is on the morning of Thursday the tenth of November and the next wipe is on Sunday the thirteenth of November and the wage Tuesday after this one is on the morning of Tuesday the fifteenth of November. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fifty-fourth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirty-first day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood five hundred and thirty-nine days and no answer has ever come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and forty-first day of not being asked a question. The pair of objects upon that crown that belongs to nobody is at the eight hundred and seventy-ninth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and forty-fifth night. The two lights lying about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and sixty-third day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and eighteenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and thirty-third day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and fiftieth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and eighty-first day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood seventy-two days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fortieth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its thirty-ninth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing at all under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-one days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-nine days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon under that low grey and a length of line measured off against a gauge with the cold making the hand slow on it, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing at all in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their fifty-first day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that a wage Tuesday in that yard is the one day of the week on which everybody stands about waiting for four o'clock.

---

The chest with the twenty envelopes in it stood shut on that step from ten until four and nobody in that yard put a hand on the lid of it, and the column on that lid stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks with the hundred and seventh week fallen due on every one of those twenty-six, and it did not move today and is not going to move on any of them.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried water at about eleven and did not look at that step and did not ask anybody anything about it, and that is the seventh day he has been in this harbor.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, the woman of thirty, who holds no office, said out loud that a page should carry what it was used for.

She said it the way she says a thing to a room and not to anybody in it. She said out loud that she was three days up that coast road in the middle of last week and stood in a room with a slate in it and a boy of about nineteen in it who cannot read, and that the figure on that slate is a figure out of a page of her own and that the figure is right, and that the whole of what has happened to it since the first of October is that it has been used for a thing the page does not say.

She said out loud that a page carrying a figure and nothing else cannot be asked what the figure was for, and that a page which carried the use as well could have gone up that road on the top of the figure and would have answered the only question anybody up there had to ask, and that nobody carried it because there was nothing on it to answer with.

The man of about thirty-two was in that room when she said it and he said nothing at all. About four of that nine said nothing at all. Nobody repeated any of it out loud afterwards and nobody wrote one word of it down.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the ninety-first day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from ten until about one, and after one she was in that shed and did not go up that coast road at one the way she goes on most days of the week.

Seven hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those seven was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries seven against this day.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and one words, and the wage Tuesday came first in it, the thing she said at one came in the middle of it, and the four columns came at the end of it, and nothing that anybody counts is in this reading.

Two hundred and fifty-fourth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it.**"

"**That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-one days and is neither shut nor struck. Seventy-two days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its sixty-seventh day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare.**"

"**It is three hundred and thirty-nine days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and thirty-one days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for a woman saying out loud in about nine people what she had gone four days to hear said by somebody else, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had fifty-eight lines on it.

**A PAGE SHOULD CARRY WHAT IT WAS USED FOR, AND THAT WAS SAID OUT LOUD AT ABOUT ONE IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE BY THE WOMAN OF THIRTY, WHO HOLDS NO OFFICE, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO WAS IN THAT ROOM AND SAID NOTHING. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE AND THE CHEST WAS SHUT FROM TEN UNTIL FOUR. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FOURTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a woman who keeps a page put a case for that page out loud in a working place on a wage Tuesday is a thing this harbor has not done in thirty-one days, and that the day it was done on is the one day of the week on which nobody can leave before four, which is why about four of that nine said nothing and let it stand; that the reason she said it today and not in the middle of last week is that she had only come back down that road on Sunday and had not been in that shed with the whole of it in front of her since the twenty-ninth of October; that the reason he said nothing whatever when it was said is that a man who has spent ten days asking to be handed somebody to put a wrong figure right to will not be handed the other half of that question in about nine people, and that the other half of that question has been standing in that yard since the first of October and he has not said a word about it; that the reason nobody wrote any of it down is that this harbor wrote a sheet of its own on Monday with a count on it and has no practice yet at putting a thing anybody said in there next to a figure, and that the keeper said out loud on Friday that a name against a sheet means the person was asked and gave an answer, and that a sentence said out loud in about nine people and written down afterwards says almost the same thing about a person who was not asked.*
