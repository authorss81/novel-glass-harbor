# Chapter 856

*Wednesday the Twenty-Third Day of November, the Forty-Sixth Day, a Sixth Line Added to the Five in Public and Out Loud in About Nine People at About Two, It Saying on Its Own Face That It Was Made Under Pressure by People With No Stake in It and That Anybody Who Has to Work Under It Is Free to Repair It, and Its Whole Content Being That a Page Records Who Wrote It and Who Put a Second Hand on It and Does Not Record Who Read It, That Where a Figure Has Been Used for Something a Page Does Not Say the Person Who Used It Is Not a Person the Page Can Reach, and That Anybody Who Worked from a Page Is Owed the Figure as It Stands and Nothing Else, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Eighty-Nine and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Nineteen and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Eighty-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Fourteen*

Wednesday the twenty-third day of November, with that market open from six and a thin blue morning with the cloud gone off that lane in the night and the first frost of it standing in the shade of that mole, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of tomorrow, being Thursday the twenty-fourth day of November, and the next wipe is on Sunday the twenty-seventh day of November, and no wage Tuesday falls again inside this run of fifty days. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-two thousand one hundred and eighty-four pence out, being eight hundred and twenty-six marks and two shillings, and one hundred and twenty-one thousand five hundred and forty-four pence short, being seven hundred and fifty-nine marks, eight shillings and eightpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and sixty-ninth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this forty-sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-fourth day and nobody has answered it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and fifty-sixth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-fourth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixtieth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and seventy-eighth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and forty-eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and sixty-fifth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the four hundred and ninety-sixth day of not speaking about six names. That pair at that end stall has stood eighty-seven days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its fifty-fifth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-fourth day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-six days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and fifty-fourth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was four boats off that mole before noon in that thin blue light and the shackle that was ground back on Friday set into its clevis at last and found to be a half inch short, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews reached their sixty-sixth day and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had heard at about half past one that there was going to be a rule said out loud this afternoon and had stopped work early to hear it.

---

At about two, in that shed, in about nine people, the keeper said out loud the whole of a sixth line and about four of that nine agreed to each part of it as he said it, before he wrote any of it.

He said out loud that a page records who wrote it and who put a second hand onto it and does not record one person who read it, and that about four of that nine said yes to that in the ordinary voice. He said out loud that where a figure has been used for something a page does not say, the person who used it is not a person that page can reach, and about four of that nine said yes to that too.

He said out loud that anybody who worked from a page is owed the figure as it stands and nothing else. About four of that nine said out loud that they were not going to say that that is what a person is owed, and that they were going to agree to the words on the face of the thing and to nothing else, and that the whole of what was on the face of the thing was the words.

Then he said out loud that it was made under pressure by people with no stake in it, and that anybody who has to work under it is free to repair it, and he wrote that on the face of it before he wrote anything else on it, in his own hand, and then wrote the rest of it under that.

The whole of it on the face of that sheet is this. **A page records who wrote it and who put a second hand on it and does not record who read it. Where a figure has been used for something a page does not say, the person who used it is not a person that page can reach. Anybody who worked from a page is owed the figure as it stands and nothing else. Made under pressure in about nine people by people with no stake in it. Anybody who has to work under this is free to repair it.**

---

It is not a fifth kind of thing on any wall and it is not on a wall at all.

It is on a sheet, and it went into the inside of that door at about half past two the way the other five went in, and about four of that nine said out loud that it is not read out at that stone standing up and does not go up over that fish market with the claims and the corrections and the refusals and the restricted records.

About four of that nine said out loud that they had agreed to six lines with no stake in any of it and that the reason the keeper put the last part on the face of it in the first place is that six lines agreed to by people with nothing in it is a thing that has to be repairable or it is nothing at all.

Nobody asked what anybody has to do under it. Nobody has yet.

---

At about half past four the keeper put that sheet into the drawer in the corner of that room over that chandler's store, and nobody in that room said one word about what a drawer is for, and the outside of that drawer still says what was in it when it went in forty-six days ago.

The wall of that room is bare and has had nothing put on it for one hundred and one days.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and sixth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The man of about thirty-two stood in that room from one until five and said nothing out loud at any point in it, and about four of that nine looked at him while the sixth line was being said out loud and about four of that nine did not.

At about half past three he thought, without saying one word of it out loud, that a rule is the first thing this harbor has made in six weeks that a stranger could be told about in one sentence, and that he has no standing whatever to be the man who tells a stranger, and that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place is the only person in that lane who will ever have to work under it if anybody does.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of four sheets that left that harbor that day and not one of those twelve was the hand that wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

At about five in that afternoon about four of that nine said out loud that a rule which names no figure cannot put a figure back onto anything at all, and that the sixth column down the side of that leaf stands empty against this day for that reason and not because nothing went wrong.

---

About half past five on that Wednesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about seven hundred and sixty-five words, and the ordinary work came first in it and the six lines came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the sixth line is in this reading because he said it out loud in about nine people, and the sixth column down the side of that leaf is not in this reading because a column is not read out at that stone standing up.

"**A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus eight thousand four hundred less eight thousand four hundred is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is still under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Two hundred and sixty-ninth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. "**The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its eighty-second day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen. It is three hundred and fifty-fourth days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head and not one day of his life is set against that, and he has not been on the water. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and forty-six days have gone by in this harbor without a word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for six lines said out loud and agreed to by people with no stake in any of it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy-three lines on it.

---

**A SIXTH LINE WAS ADDED TO THE FIVE AT ABOUT TWO TODAY IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, MADE OUT LOUD, AND IT SAYS ON ITS OWN FACE THAT IT WAS MADE UNDER PRESSURE BY PEOPLE WITH NO STAKE IN IT AND THAT ANYBODY WHO HAS TO WORK UNDER IT IS FREE TO REPAIR IT. NOBODY SAID THAT PAYING THE FIGURE AS IT STANDS IS WHAT A PERSON IS OWED. NO FIGURE WENT INTO THE SIXTH COLUMN AGAINST THIS DAY. THE COLUMN DID NOT MOVE. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-NINTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a sixth line agreed to out loud by about nine people with nothing in it is the only thing this harbor has made since the first of October that nobody has asked to be made, and that a rule nobody has asked for is either the best thing in a place or a waste of an afternoon, and that nobody in that shed knows which yet and that is what the last part on the face of it is for; that the reason nobody said that paying the figure as it stands is what a person is owed is that if that is said out loud in about nine people then it is said to a page and not to a person, and about four of that nine know what this harbor does to a person whose name goes under a figure; that the reason he wrote the pressure and the repair on the face of it before he wrote the rest of it is that a rule put at the bottom reads as an afterthought and a rule put at the top reads as the reason for the rest, and that this harbor made five lines in a shed on the thirtieth of September and has found them hard to argue with ever since; that the reason the drawer was not written on again tonight is that the outside of that drawer says what was in it forty-six days ago and that it is now out of date, and that a keeper who writes on a drawer at half past four on a Wednesday writes on it when he is not in a room with anybody.*
