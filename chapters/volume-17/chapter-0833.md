# Chapter 833

*Monday the Thirty-First Day of October, the Twenty-Third Day, a Rota Day and the Hundred and Ninety-Seventh Charge on That Cistern at About Nine and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Line Worked on Saturday Settling at About Four and the Total at the Foot of That Sheet Read After the Settlement and Not Before It, the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud at About Two in About Nine People That He Is Not Going to Ask the Copyist One Thing, Nobody Reading One of Those Eleven Sheets and a Day With No Figure on It Not Being a Zero, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns at Five Hundred and Sixty-Six and Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Seven Hundred and Two*

Monday the thirty-first day of October, with that market open from six and a flat grey morning that did not decide anything and a hard frost still standing in the shade of that quay wall at about nine, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**A Monday, and a charge falls on that cistern while there is no wipe in it and nothing on that page moves on a wage, and the next wipe falls on Sunday the sixth and the next wage Tuesday is on the morning of tomorrow. That butt was charged at about nine and it was the hundred and ninety-seventh time and it was fourteen trips by hand and not one of them was walked on. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet until about four and at about four the line worked on Saturday settled there and the total at that foot was read after the settlement and not before it, standing at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and sixteen marks, two shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and forty-nine marks and nine shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and forty-sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-third day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now stood five hundred and thirty-one days. Nobody has put a question to the bellwright of this harbor for five hundred and thirty-three days. The two objects on that crown that belong to nobody are into the eight hundred and seventy-first day. That bench under that pump stood bare on its six hundred and thirty-seventh night. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and fifty-fifth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and tenth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and twenty-fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and forty-second day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and seventy-third day of not speaking about six names. Sixty-four days now of that pair at that end stall standing together with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its thirty-second day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the thirty-first day of being on that community's own page. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-three days. Nine hulls lie about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and thirty-one days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about nine that butt was filled for the hundred and ninety-seventh time and the last of it from Thursday had gone flat at the bottom and had a skin on it and nobody drank it and nobody said anything about that either.

The ordinary work of that Monday was two boats off that mole before noon and a coil of line measured off against a gauge by a man of about twenty-four of that market, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and no money at all is set against either half of it.

Those four new crews stood at their forty-third day and took the day's work off in about an hour and three quarters, and about four of that nine said that one of them had been told on Saturday by a man of about thirty-one of that market that this harbor was a hard place to learn in and that he was still here.

---

At about two, in that shed, with about nine people standing round it and nobody sitting down, the man of about thirty-two said out loud that there is a man in this market who keeps a copying book and charges a penny a sheet for copying anything into it.

He said out loud that he was not going to ask that man one thing this month. He said out loud that the reason is that in September that man was made to stand up in the room over that fish market in front of about twenty people and say six sentences once, and that nobody in this harbor has sent for him since and is not going to send for him twice over a thing this harbor has already admitted once that it got wrong.

The keeper said, in the ordinary voice he keeps for things he agrees with, that that is the right answer and that he would have given the same one, and that a man who is not asked is owed the not being asked, and that this harbor is not going to take that back off him in order to get a name out of the room.

The other five of that nine said nothing at all. Nobody repeated either of those two sentences after either man.

---

At about four the line worked on Saturday went into the foot of that sheet and the total at that foot was read after that and not before it, at one hundred and thirty thousand five hundred and eighty-eight out and one hundred and nineteen thousand nine hundred and forty-eight short.

The keeper said out loud that he reads the foot of that sheet after the settlement on every day there is a settlement in it and never before, and that a man who reads a total and then settles it has read two different numbers and has written one of them down somewhere in his head.

At about half past four that afternoon the keeper went up that lane to the room over that sailmaker, stood in front of the shelf with the eleven sheets lying on it, read none of them, and came back down. Nobody else went up that lane that day.

A day with no figure on it is not a zero, and he did not write a zero on anything, and nobody in that shed at half past five asked him what the day had come to.

---

The woman of thirty of no office had that yard from about ten until about two with her own page and went up that coast road at two. Between two and five the man of about thirty-two was at the far bench with the page of routes and about four of that nine noticed he had come in from the lane by the long way and said nothing about it.

From about ten until about two nobody asked that man one thing in that lane, being the eighty-third day of that run of days he has not missed.

Seven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and every one of the seven belonged to somebody other than the person who wrote what it went against, and the page of days carries seven against this day.

---

About half past five on that Monday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and twenty words, and the cistern came first in it, the settlement came in the middle of it, and the shelf with nothing read off it came at the end of it, and no count that anybody keeps is in this reading.

Two hundred and forty-sixth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it.**"

"**That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-three days and is neither shut nor struck. That pair at that stall has stood sixty-four days where it stands, one size, a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its fifty-ninth day. The fifteen-gap sheet stands at fifteen, and the wall of that room over the chandler's store is bare and empty.**"

"**It is three hundred and thirty-one days and still nothing in the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, and nothing whatever is set against that figure, and he has not been on the water at any point in that time. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and this harbor is on its twenty-third day without one word about them.**"

Nobody was thanked for not asking a man one question on a Monday, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had fifty lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVENTH CHARGE WAS MADE AND IT WAS FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. THE LINE WORKED ON SATURDAY SETTLED AT ABOUT FOUR AND THE TOTAL AT THAT FOOT WAS READ AFTER THE SETTLEMENT AND NOT BEFORE IT, AT ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY THOUSAND FIVE HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-EIGHT OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND NINETEEN THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND FORTY-EIGHT SHORT. NOBODY READ ONE OF THOSE ELEVEN SHEETS TODAY AND A DAY WITH NO FIGURE ON IT IS NOT A ZERO. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND FORTY-SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that nobody read one of those eleven sheets on that Monday, and that this is not the same thing as nothing having happened, and that a day with no figure on it is not a zero and this harbor has spent five weeks learning that a page cannot carry a blank as though a blank were an answer; that the man of about thirty-two was put on that bench at about ten and came off it at about two and went up to that room over the sailmaker and read nothing, and the reason is that a count which is taken by a person who decides on the day which of the eleven sheets is worth reading is not a count of readings and is a count of that person; that the reason the keeper went up that lane and stood in front of the shelf and read none of them is that he had read one of them on Sunday and on Saturday and did not want his own habit in this harbor to be the reason a figure exists on a day it would not otherwise exist; that the reason a man who is not asked is owed the not being asked is that this harbor made a man say six sentences once in September in front of twenty people and has not given that back, and that asking him a second set of questions about a different thing would take the first set back without saying so, and that nobody in that shed at half past five said the word owed out loud.*
