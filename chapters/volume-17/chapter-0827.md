# Chapter 827

*Tuesday the Twenty-Fifth Day of October, the Seventeenth Day, the Hundred and Fourth Wage Tuesday and the Column on the Lid of That Step Standing at Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks and Not Moving and the Hundred and Fifth Week Fallen Due With No Envelope Against It, the Fortnight of That Bell Touched Once at About One at That End Stall in About Four People and Settled Nowhere, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the First Column at Five Hundred and Sixty and the Second at Two Thousand Six Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Tuesday the twenty-fifth day of October, with that market open from six and a fog that came off that water about ten and stayed until about one and left a flat grey afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**An ordinary working Tuesday, being a wage Tuesday and a day with no charge on that cistern and no wipe in it, and the next charge on that cistern is on the morning of Thursday and the next wipe is on the morning of tomorrow. The column on the lid of that step stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks and it did not move today and it does not move on any wage Tuesday in this run, and a further week stands fallen due at its close, being the hundred and fifth, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it was shut from ten until four. Nothing settled into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and twenty-nine thousand eight hundred and ninety-four pence out, being eight hundred and eleven marks, eleven shillings and twopence, and one hundred and nineteen thousand two hundred and fifty-four pence short, being seven hundred and forty-five marks, four shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and fortieth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this seventeenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is on its five hundred and twenty-fifth day. The bellwright of this harbor is on the five hundred and twenty-seventh day of nobody asking him anything. Those two objects belonging to nobody on that crown are into the eight hundred and sixty-fifth day. That bench under that pump is on the six hundred and thirty-first night bare. Two lights about a mile off that point are on their four hundred and forty-ninth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is on the four hundred and fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is on the four hundred and nineteenth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is on the four hundred and thirty-sixth day. The man of about fifty-one is on the four hundred and sixty-seventh day of not speaking about six names. The two sheets at that end stall are on their fifty-eighth day together and the wrong figure is still left showing underneath the correction and is not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its twenty-sixth day. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on the twenty-fifth day of being on that community's own page. The third of those four columns is headed by a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line. Those nine hulls are about two sea miles off that mole head. Three hundred and twenty-five days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The keeper read the column on the lid of that step at about ten and it stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, and it is the same figure it was on the hundred and third wage Tuesday and on the hundred and second and on every one of those before it, and a further week stands fallen due at its close and the hundred and fifth of those weeks has now fallen due with no envelope against it.

The chest was shut from ten until four and the keeper keeps that key in his own pocket and no copy of it exists that this yard knows of, and he shut it at four and nobody asked him anything about it, because nobody asked him anything about it last Tuesday either.

---

At about one, at that end stall, in about four people, the fortnight of that bell came up.

It came up because the leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its fifty-third day a step to the left of that refusal, and the two of them carry a count of a season a step apart, being two hundred and thirty-eight days on one and two hundred and thirty-one days on the other, and about four people have now walked up to that board and read both.

A woman of about forty-four of that market who sells line said out loud that a fortnight of a bell out of service is fourteen days and that a season belongs to the place the fortnight is in and not to a stranger with a sheet, and a man of about forty-five of that market said out loud, at the same time and in the ordinary way, that a fortnight of that bell is three weeks where the season is counted the other way and that the number on the leaf came first and the number on the foot came after and that both of them were written by people who were not in that room.

Two of that four wanted it put down at fourteen days. Two of that four wanted it put down at twenty-one. Not one of the four said out loud that the other two had it wrong. They said it the way two people say a thing they have both said before, one across the other, and then all four of them stood there for about a minute and a half and said nothing at all, and about two of them looked at the two sheets a step apart and about two of them looked at the water.

Nobody wrote anything down. Nobody in that four said which of the two places was right about its own season, and about four of the nine people in that shed at half past five have heard about that conversation and none of them has said anything about it since.

---

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon in that fog and a hatches out of the water by half past three, and no line went onto that fitting-out sheet and no figure of money is set against either half of it.

On their thirty-seventh day those four new crews had the day's work off in about two hours of it with the man of about thirty-two over them, and about four of that nine did it in that fog with no lamp because the lamp at that mole head does not come on until about four.

The woman of thirty of no office had that yard from about ten until about two with her own page and did not go up that coast road. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed with the page of routes between two and five. Neither of them was in that lane at about one and twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks was not said by either of them to the other.

That bench was worked by a man of about thirty-four of the second lay between about ten and about two, unasked and unanswered, and it is the seventy-seventh day of that run of days.

Three hands went onto two sheets that left buildings in that harbor that day and none of the three belonged to the person who wrote what each of them went against, and the page of days carries three against this day.

---

About two sea miles out from that mole head nine of them are lying, and seventeen days have now gone by in a harbor that has not mentioned them once.**"

Nobody was thanked for two people saying one thing and two people saying another, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had forty-four lines on it.

THE FORTNIGHT OF THAT BELL WAS TOUCHED ONCE AT ABOUT ONE IN ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE AND TWO OF THEM WANTED FOURTEEN AND TWO OF THEM WANTED TWENTY-ONE AND IT IS SETTLED NOWHERE.
