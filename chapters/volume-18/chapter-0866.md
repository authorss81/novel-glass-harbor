# Chapter 866

*Saturday the Third Day of December, the Sixth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, Line Two onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eight With the Eighth of December Written on the Face of It for the Day It Settles, Two Days of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Coal and Oil for Two Days and a New Glass for the Lamp at the Head of That Mole at Two Marks and Sixty Yards of Line and Six Men at Two Days at Sixteen Pence a Man-Day, the Whole of That Line One Thousand and Sixty-Eight Pence, Six Men Out at About Eight and a Man of About Twenty-Seven Saying Out Loud at About One That That Wall Was Quicker Last Year, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety-Nine and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Sixty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety-Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Sixty-Four*

Saturday the third day of December, with that market shut until seven on a Saturday and an east wind making up short of that mole head by noon and the cold coming off the water in a way that put a coat on everybody in that yard before nine, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Saturday is an ordinary working Saturday and carries no charge on that cistern, no wipe, and nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next charge is on Monday the fifth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the sixth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was settled on it, and what was worked on it this morning stands on the face of that line and on nothing else in that yard, that foot standing before this morning's work at one hundred and thirty-three thousand nine hundred and eighty-two pence out, being eight hundred and thirty-seven marks, five shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-three thousand three hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and seventy marks, eleven shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventy-ninth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and sixty-fourth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixty-sixth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and fourth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventieth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eighty-eighth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and forty-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fifty-eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventy-fifth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and sixth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its ninety-second day. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety-seven days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixty-fifth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its sixty-fourth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and eleven days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in six days. Three hundred and sixty-fourth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Six men went out on that second line at about eight in that east wind and came in at half past two, and six is the whole of the labour on it and there is no seventh man in it.

That second line went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight with the eighth day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is Monday, and nothing of it comes into the foot of that sheet before Monday afternoon.

Its five parts are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence; coal and oil for two days at forty pence, being three shillings and fourpence; a new glass for the lamp at the head of that mole at two marks, being three hundred and twenty pence; sixty yards of line at three pence the yard at sixty times three for one hundred and eighty pence, being one mark, one shilling and eightpence; and six men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at six times two times sixteen for one hundred and ninety-two pence, being one mark, two shillings and eightpence.

The whole of that line is one thousand and sixty-eight pence, being six marks and nine shillings.

---

The glass for the lamp at the head of that mole went up in a hand at about ten and cost two marks, being three hundred and twenty pence, and came out of that yard's own working money and not out of that bag, and it is the second of those glasses this quarter.

Nobody in that yard was asked whether that light is wanted by anybody using it.

The ordinary work of that Saturday was two boats off that mole before noon with the wind on their quarter and a hand-cart of coal up from the store, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and no man in that harbor is set against either half of it.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay put down a length of coil he had cut and said out loud that that wall was quicker last year.

About four of that nine agreed with him out loud in about four different ways, and about four of those nine said out loud that they had not counted that wall themselves and were going on believing him, and the man of about twenty-seven said out loud that nobody has to, and picked the coil back up.

Not one of those four has a figure for last year and not one of those four ever had one.

One of about four of those nine then asked him, in the ordinary way and to nobody in particular, by how much it was quicker last year, and he said out loud that he did not know and did not measure it and was not going to, and he put the coil back down and went on with it and did not say it twice and did not say it louder.

Nobody asked him a second thing about it and nobody agreed with him out loud a second time, and that is the whole of what was said about that wall in that shed on that Saturday, and it was said by five men and not by the wall.

The man of about thirty-two heard all of it and said nothing then, and said something about it on the Monday, and nobody in that shed knew at the time that he had a figure of it and nobody has asked him for one.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and sixteenth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Nine hands went onto the faces of two sheets and a part of a third that left that harbor that day, and not one of those nine was the hand that wrote what any of them went against, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

**LINE TWO WENT ONTO THAT SHEET AT ABOUT EIGHT WITH THE EIGHTH DAY OF DECEMBER ON THE FACE OF IT AND IS ONE THOUSAND AND SIXTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING SIX MARKS AND NINE SHILLINGS, AND DOES NOT TOUCH THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET UNTIL MONDAY. ONE QUESTION WENT INTO THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED ON THIS DAY, BEING BY HOW MUCH THAT WALL WAS QUICKER LAST YEAR, AND IT WAS ANSWERED THAT THE MAN DID NOT KNOW AND DID NOT MEASURE IT. THE COLUMN WAS NOT ADDED UP. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-NINTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the third day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the sixth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Saturday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the sixth day of December; that six men went out on that second line at about eight and came in at about half past two, and that six is the whole of the labour on that line; that line two went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight with the eighth day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is Monday; that its five parts are two days of a boat at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, a new glass for the lamp at the head of that mole at two marks being three hundred and twenty pence, sixty yards of line at three pence the yard at sixty times three, and six men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at six times two times sixteen; that the whole of that line is one thousand and sixty-eight pence, being six marks and nine shillings; that that glass went up in a hand at about ten and came out of that yard's own working money and not out of that bag, and that nobody in that yard was asked whether that light is wanted by anybody who uses it; that at about one in that shed in about nine people a man of about twenty-seven of the second lay put down a length of coil he had cut and said out loud that that wall was quicker last year; that about four of that nine agreed with him out loud in about four different ways and about four of those nine said out loud that they had not counted that wall themselves; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason about four people agreed out loud with a thing nobody could put a figure to is that agreeing with a man about last year costs nothing and a figure costs whatever it costs, and that this harbor has spent six weeks learning what a figure costs and has consequently got very good at not making one; that the reason the man of about twenty-seven said out loud that he did not measure it is that a man who has not measured a thing can be wrong in either direction and a man who has measured it can only be wrong in one, and that a working man holding a coil of line prefers the first of those two; that the reason a new glass went into a lamp at the head of a mole at two marks and nobody was asked whether anybody wanted the light is that a lamp is not a claim and not a correction and not a refusal, and that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put on a wall and a light going out is not one of them; that the reason a man heard a true thing about a wall said out loud and said nothing back is that the man had a figure for it and the four who agreed had not, and that this is the first time in six weeks that the only person in a room who could have answered a figure was the one person in the room who did not open his mouth.
