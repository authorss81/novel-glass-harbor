# Chapter 906

*Thursday the Twelfth Day of January, the Forty-Sixth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Eighteenth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen, an Ordinary Working Thursday With No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, That Piece of Wood Twenty Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirty-Nine and Three Thousand and Sixty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirty-Eight and the Second at Three Thousand and Sixty-Four*

Thursday the twelfth day of January, with that market open from six and a flat tide lying beyond that mole before six with no wind on it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside that run is on Sunday the fifteenth day of January and the next charge is on Monday the sixteenth day of January and there is no wage Tuesday after it inside that run. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and eighteenth time and it was walked on by none of it. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, is not read at any hour of that Thursday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and nineteenth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine figures passed through that forty-sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is read at its six hundred and fourth day. That bellwright is found into the six hundred and sixth day of not being asked anything by anybody. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are read at the nine hundred and forty-fourth day. That bench beneath that pump was left bare again on its seven hundred and tenth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are marked at their five hundred and twenty-eighth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are counted at the four hundred and eighty-third day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is read at its four hundred and ninety-eighth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is read at its five hundred and fifteenth day. The man of about fifty-one is kept at the five hundred and forty-sixth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is kept on its hundred and thirty-second day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and thirty-seventh days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is set on its hundred and fifth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in that harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, has been on that community's own page for a hundred and four days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fifty-first days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-six days. Four hundred and fourth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That tide lay flat beyond that mole before six and showed no line on it, and about four of that nine said out loud that a flat tide before six means the boats will go off without waiting for anybody.

---

That cistern was filled for the two hundred and eighteenth time at about ten past ten with two men on the handles, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody walked on one single trip of it.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and did not once put it down in the middle, and not one figure of any kind stands in any page in that harbor against that man or against anything that he does.

About four of that nine said out loud that fourteen trips on a flat morning goes quicker than on a windy one, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Thursday, and he worked beside that bench for most of it without once putting a hand on that piece of wood.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for twenty days and nobody has touched it at any hour of that Thursday, and the man of about thirty-four sat at that same bench from about ten until about two with his coat near it.

What he was thinking about, at that bench, in a working shed, with that piece of wood face up beside another man's coat and nobody asking him anything at all, was that leaving a thing where it lies does not make it belong there, and that watching other men count without him has not made him stop counting in his own head, and that going home to a room he shares will not make either of those things any easier to carry.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the two of them are still married, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other in that yard about any part of any of it.

---

In the room they have, after work, with the door shut against that flat evening, the woman of thirty of no office asked him in the ordinary way and to nobody in particular whether he ever wrote any of it down on anything, and he said out loud that he did not.

She said one sentence and it is not written down on any page in that harbor and it is not in any column and it is not put on any page in that room.

Neither of the two of them gave up a foot of professional authority and neither signed the other's page, and the disagreement between them is not settled and they are still married.

---

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor seventy-two days by that Thursday and stood at the back of that shed for about two hours of it and said nothing and asked nobody for anything.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell has been on that board for its hundred and thirty-second day, and about four of that nine said out loud that a leaf nobody turns still gathers dust on its face.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was two boats off that mole before noon on that flat sea and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fifty-sixth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

At about noon the keeper chalked the rota figure on the back of that board and left it there, and about four of that nine said out loud that a chalk figure on the back of a board is not a figure on a page.

About four of that nine said out loud at about two that a flat sea makes for quick work off that mole, and three boats went off before noon and came back with full holds and nobody wrote one word about them.

That room over that chandler's store stood shut all that Thursday with its bare wall showing nothing, and about four of that nine went up to it at about three with a lamp and looked at nothing and came down again.

---

About half past five on that Thursday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and seventy words, and that cistern came first in it and the bench came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench within a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood and there is no figure of it on any page in that harbor.

That cistern was filled for the two hundred and eighteenth time at about ten past ten with two men on the handles, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody walked on one single trip of it in that yard. About four of that nine said out loud that fourteen trips on a flat morning goes quicker than on a windy one in that yard, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that at any hour. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Thursday, and he worked beside that bench for most of it without once putting a hand on that piece of wood in that shed. The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the two of them are still married, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other in that yard about any part of any of it. The ordinary work of that Thursday was two boats off that mole before noon on that flat sea and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store in that yard. From about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and fifty-sixth day of that run. Eleven hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day in that shed. That bell in that shed rang at about four and nobody in that shed stopped work for it at any hour, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bell at four means the covers go on and the working day is done in that yard. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Three hundred and nineteenth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-six days and is neither shut nor struck. One hundred and thirty-seventh days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and thirty-second day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and that wall of the room over the chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fifty-first days. It is four hundred and fourth days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, he has not been on the water, and no figure at all is set against any part of it. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. At about noon the keeper chalked the rota figure on the back of that board and left it there, and about four of that nine said out loud that a chalk figure on the back of a board is not a figure on a page in that harbor. About four of that nine said out loud at about two that a flat sea makes for quick work off that mole, and three boats went off before noon and came back with full holds and nobody wrote one word about them in that yard. That room over that chandler's store stood shut all that Thursday with its bare wall showing nothing, and about four of that nine went up to it at about three with a lamp and looked at nothing and came down again in that store. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head at first light yet still now here still.**"

Nobody was thanked for a Thursday on which that cistern was filled for the two hundred and eighteenth time and a piece of wood lay twenty days on a bench and two married people spoke one sentence at home and settled nothing, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and twenty-three lines on it.

*First column, entered on Thursday the twelfth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the forty-sixth entry of that run of fifty days: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday; that that cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and eighteenth time and the old man of about sixty-eight went up ten; that that piece of wood has been face up for twenty days; that the woman of thirty worked that yard over her own page and neither said one word to the other in that yard; that eleven hands went onto two sheets; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for one thing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason fourteen trips went quicker on a flat morning is that a barrow runs lighter when the air does not push against it, and that the covers went on at about eleven all the same; that the reason nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for one thing is that a bench sat at for a hundred and fifty-six days from about ten until about two is a bench nobody questions; that the reason that piece of wood lay twenty days untouched is that a thing nobody owns gathers no hands, and that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and a piece of wood with mornings on it is none of them.*
