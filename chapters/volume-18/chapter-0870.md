# Chapter 870

*Wednesday the Seventh Day of December, the Tenth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, Line Three onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eight With the Twelfth of December Written on the Face of It for the Day It Settles, Three Days of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Coal and Oil for Three Days and a Hundred and Twenty Yards of Line and Two Bell Seats Cut and Faced and Five Men at Three Days at Sixteen Pence a Man-Day, the Whole of That Line One Thousand Two Hundred Pence and Nothing of It into the Foot of That Sheet Until Friday, a Man of About Fifty-Eight of That Market Asking at About One in About Nine People Whether That Wall Is a Figure on a Page Anywhere in This Harbor and a Second Question About Who Puts It Up That Nobody Answers, the Column of the Asked Added Up Once and in Writing at About Six and Not Read Out in That Shed and Not in This Reading, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Three and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Eighty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Two and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Wednesday the seventh day of December, with that market open from six and a clear morning with the cold coming back after noon and the water off that mole head going out long and slow with no white on it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday is an ordinary working Wednesday and carries no charge on that cistern, no wipe, and nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eleventh day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the eighth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the thirteenth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was settled on it, and what was worked on it this morning stands on the face of that line and on nothing else in that yard, that foot standing before this morning's work at one hundred and thirty-five thousand and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and forty-four marks and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-four thousand four hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and seventy-seven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighty-third days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this tenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and sixty-eighth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventieth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and eighth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventy-fourth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninety-second day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and forty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixty-second day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventy-ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and tenth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its ninety-sixth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and one days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixty-ninth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its sixty-eighth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fifteen days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in ten days. Three hundred and sixty-eighth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Five men went out on that third line at about eight and came in at half past three, and five is the whole of the labour on that line and there is not a sixth of them.

That third line went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight with the twelfth day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is Friday, and not one penny of it comes into the foot of that sheet before that Friday afternoon.

Its five parts are three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at three times one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four pence, being three marks and two shillings; coal and oil for three days at sixty pence, being five shillings; a hundred and twenty yards of line at three pence the yard at one hundred and twenty times three for three hundred and sixty pence, being two marks, three shillings and fourpence; two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence for thirty-six pence, being three shillings; and five men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at five times three times sixteen for two hundred and forty pence, being one mark, six shillings and eightpence.

The whole of that line is one thousand two hundred pence, being seven marks, six shillings and eightpence, and the keeper read that total back to nobody and did not add it to the foot of that sheet, and said out loud why it was not, being that a line is in the foot of that sheet on the day that yard settles it and not on the day it was worked.

Those two bell seats were cut and faced at that bench between eleven and one, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bell seat is the only thing on that sheet this month that a stranger could tell the use of by looking at it.

About four of that nine said out loud that three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk is the biggest part of that line, and that no figure anywhere in that yard says what a boat's day ought to be, and that the figure in the chalk is written by somebody who is not in that yard.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, a man of about fifty-eight of that market asked, in the ordinary way and to nobody in particular, whether that wall is a figure on a page anywhere in this harbor.

He was told out loud that it is not, and that the man telling him knows every figure on every page in that room and that this one is on none of them.

About four of that nine said out loud that a figure nobody wrote down is not a record, and one of that nine said out loud that a figure somebody has written down on a piece of wood in his own coat is not a record either, and nobody in that shed said out loud which of those two is right.

About four of those nine then asked, in the ordinary way and to nobody in particular, who it is that puts that wall up, and about four of that nine said out loud that they did not know, and the man of about thirty-two said nothing at all to that and nobody asked a second man the same thing.

Neither question of that day was answered with a name and neither of them was put to the man who puts that wall up, who was not in that shed and is not named anywhere in this harbor.

About four people in that shed said out loud that a wall does not get counted and a bench does, and nobody answered that.

---

The wooden slate was in the coat of the man of about thirty-two and stayed in that coat and nobody knew it was there, and about four of that nine did not know of it, and one of that nine knew.

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon with that cold coming back, and a hand-cart of that line at three pence the yard taken out to the third lay and brought back a quarter full, and neither of those came near that fitting-out sheet. The man of about thirty-two read that line from the other side of that bench stone at about half past three and did not say anything about it, and about four of that nine did not know he had read it.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twentieth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about two, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed at about one and was in it with her for an hour and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about any of it.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and about four of those nine could not read a word of what was on any of them and were not asked to, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

At about six that evening the keeper took a column and added it up once, in writing, at the back of that leaf, and said out loud that he was not going to say the number out loud to anybody this month and was not going to read it at that stone standing up.

About four of that nine said out loud that they would come back and read it off the side of that leaf themselves, and about four of those nine said out loud that they would rather not know.

Nobody in that shed was given the figure and nobody in that yard heard it and it is not in this reading.

---

THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED WAS ADDED UP ONCE, IN WRITING, AT ABOUT SIX THIS EVENING, AND WAS NOT READ OUT IN THAT SHED AND IS NOT IN THIS READING AND IS IN NO MOUTH. *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the answer given to a man of about fifty-eight was that it is on no page in that room is that a page of days in this harbor carries nine columns and a leaf carries four and a fitting-out sheet carries one, and that the figures of that wall have never been in any of the fourteen of those places, and that the man who said it knows because he keeps all fourteen; that the reason about four of that nine said out loud that a wall does not get counted, a bench does, is that a bench is a thing a man may be given and a wall is not, and that this harbor has been counting benches and tables and columns and cisterns for six weeks and has not once had to decide what to do with a body; that the reason nobody in that shed said which of the two things about that wall was right is that the two of them were said by two different people about eleven minutes apart and neither of them knew the other had said it, and that a harbor that wants an answer to a thing like that has to have the two people in the same conversation, and that is a thing this harbor has not arranged in six weeks; that the reason the count of that column was taken once, in writing, at about six, and not said out loud to anybody, is that a count of questions is a count of what a harbor asked itself, and that a harbor which says its own questions out loud at a stone on a mole head begins to pick the ones it likes.
