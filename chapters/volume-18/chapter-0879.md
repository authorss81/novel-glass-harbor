# Chapter 879

*Friday the Sixteenth Day of December, the Nineteenth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Friday Carrying No Charge and No Wipe and Nothing on a Wage, a Sentence Begun at About Two in About Four People and Stopped After Four Words and Replaced Out Loud by the Wind Getting Up Off That Point, the Slate Not Out of the Coat at Any Hour of This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twelve and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Thirty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Eleven and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Twenty-Nine*

Friday the sixteenth day of December, with that market open from six and a grey morning with that sleet coming on in strips off that water and a wind getting up behind it by about two, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Friday is an ordinary working Friday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighteenth day of December and the next charge is on Monday the nineteenth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twentieth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninety-second days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this nineteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventy-seventh day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventy-ninth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and seventeenth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and eighty-third night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and first day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fifty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and seventy-first day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-eighth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and nineteenth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and fifth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and tenth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-eighth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-seventh day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-four days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in nineteen days. Three hundred and seventy-seventh days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Two men came into that shed at about two that Friday and one of them was the man of about thirty-two, and he had a sentence ready and had had it ready since Wednesday morning.

He got four words out of it and stopped, and about four of that nine looked at him, and he said out loud that there was a wind getting up off that point and went back to what he had been holding.

That is what he said out loud on that Friday, and the four words he had got out before that were not a figure and were not a name and about four of those nine were still talking about a tarpaulin.

---

He carried that sentence about that yard for two days like a thing he had cut and not needed, and it was still in his mouth at about five that evening when that shed stood empty with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on.

Nobody in that yard knew on that Friday that he had had a sentence ready, and about four of those four who looked at him at about two have not mentioned it since and will not.

He did not say it on that Friday, and the next day is a Saturday and that market does not open until seven.
---

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor forty-five days by that Friday and had asked nobody for anything on any of the forty-five, and about four of that nine said out loud that a man who stands in a place for forty-five days and asks for nothing is either working or waiting, and nobody in that shed has worked out which.

---

Three hundred and seventy-seven days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he has not been on the water, and about four of that nine said out loud that not one minute of that man's life has been set against that on any page in this harbor and that nobody ever will.

---

The drawer in the corner of that room over the chandler's store has not been opened since the day it was written on, and its outside carries what is in it and the day it went in in a keeper's hand, and about four of that nine said out loud that a thing which says on its own face what is inside it is not a shut thing.

---

Forty marks first after food is owed to a person of no office and has been owed since September past, and three marks are owed to a man eleven miles up that coast road and have gone unsent for just as long, and about four of that nine said out loud that two figures standing in the same place for three months are the only figures nobody has looked at twice.

---

That sleet came across that mole head sideways all afternoon and about four of that nine said out loud this is the fourth day this winter to come in sideways, and that every one of them has cost somebody a wet coat and nobody has ever written a figure against a wet coat anywhere.

---

The ordinary work of that Friday was three boats off that mole before noon in that sleet and a hand-cart of forty yards of line out to the second lay and back with a quarter gone, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head in that weather and answered one another at two hours a day, and about four of that nine said out loud they can be heard further in that than in anything else, and nobody out there has ever tried to make them into one note.

---

The slate was not out of that coat at any hour of that Friday, and about four of that nine did not know that wood was in that room and one of that nine knew.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about one, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed at about two and at about four and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about anything at all.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twenty-ninth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO CARRIED THAT SENTENCE ABOUT THAT YARD FOR TWO DAYS AND DID NOT SAY IT ON THIS DAY. THE SLATE WAS NOT OUT OF THAT COAT AT ANY HOUR OF THIS DAY AND NO FIGURE OF THAT THING BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH IS ON ANY PAGE IN THIS HARBOR. *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man stops four words into a sentence he has been carrying two days is that the four words are the four that make it true and the rest of it is the four that make it cost something, and that a room of four people is a room that hears the first four; that the reason he said out loud that it was windy is that a man will say anything at once rather than stand in a room and be looked at, and that this is the same instinct as picking a line back up, and that this harbor is full of men doing it quietly; that the reason nobody in that yard knew on that Friday that he had had a sentence ready is that a sentence a man does not finish is not a thing anybody can carry away, and that this is the only kind of keeping in that yard that costs the keeper nothing and that a room of nine people has watched a man do it for nineteen days; that the reason he did not say it on that Friday is that he had worked out on the Thursday that a thing said in about four people on a Friday is a thing four people will carry to their own beds, and that a Saturday morning is the first hour of this harbor in which about four people are not carrying anything.
