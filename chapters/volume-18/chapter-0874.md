# Chapter 874

*Sunday the Eleventh Day of December, the Fourteenth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-Sixth Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Forty Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One and Not Going Up Whole, Fifty-Three Off and Fifty-Three Back and One Hundred and Forty-Six the Whole of It, a Minute Longer at Each End Than It Ran the Sunday Before and Nobody in That Lane Saying Why, About Four People in Front of It Counting and Not One of the Four Saying Out Loud That He Has a Figure, the Slate in the Hand of the Man of About Thirty-Two From About a Quarter to Eleven Until About Eleven and Back in That Coat Before One, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Seven and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Six and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Four*

Sunday the eleventh day of December, with that market shut until seven on a Sunday and a grey sky sitting low on that point all day and the water off that mole head going out long and oily with no white on it at any hour, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighteenth day of December and the next charge is on Monday the twelfth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the thirteenth day of December. The wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare forty minutes and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at fifty-three minutes off and fifty-three back, one hundred and forty-six the whole of it, and that is the hundred and thirty-sixth time anybody has done it to it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was settled on it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-six thousand two hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-one marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and twenty-five thousand six hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-five marks and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighty-seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fourteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventy-second day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventy-fourth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and twelfth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventy-eighth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninety-sixth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fifty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixty-sixth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-third day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and fourteenth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundredth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and five days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-third day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-second day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and nineteen days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in fourteen days. Three hundred and seventy-second days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about half past ten the mortar showed on the face of that wall and about four people stepped up to it and began to count, and that wall came off at fifty-three minutes and stood bare forty minutes and went back up at a quarter to one with the top course of it left short, one hundred and forty-six the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-sixth time.

Both ends of it ran a minute longer than the two ends of the last one ran, and about four of that nine had both of those figures in their heads by a quarter to one, and nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it.

One of those four was the man of about thirty-two and one of those four was a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, and that is the sixth Sunday that man has stood in that lane, and on every one of the six he has kept his mouth shut.

---

Not one of those four said out loud that he had a figure, and not one of those four said out loud where his figures came from, and that is the first Sunday of the six on which none of the four of them has said it.

They compared the two ends of it in their own heads and about four of those four had got the same two figures and about four of those four had got two figures that were a minute apart and nobody in that lane knew which of those two was standing where.

A man came down that lane at about ten and was in it for the half hour it takes him and went away again before a quarter to one, and he did not speak to any of those four and none of those four spoke to him, and about four people in that lane would not be able this afternoon to describe him.

---

The man of about thirty-two had that wooden slate out of his coat in his own hand from about a quarter to eleven until about eleven, and stood looking down the column of figures on it while that wall was off, and put it back inside that coat at eleven and had both hands in his pockets after that.

About three of those four did not see him do that and one of those four saw him do it and said nothing about it then and has said nothing about it since.

That is the whole of what that lane did on that Sunday, and one of the four of them knew where every figure on that wood came from, and about four of that nine did not know there was any wood in that lane at all.

---

At a quarter to one he had both ends of it in his head and out loud he said nothing at all, where on the Sunday before he had said out loud that it was a minute each end again.

About four of that nine noticed that he had said nothing, and about four of that nine did not notice.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor forty days by that Sunday and had asked nobody for anything on any of those forty, and about four of that nine said out loud they had stopped trying to work out what he was here for.

---

Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head and answered one another at two hours a day and the answer did not run together into one note.

Nine hulls lay about two sea miles off that mole head and did not move.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon and a bucket turned over out of the rain at that bench, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay came through that lane at about eleven and sat at that bench for an hour, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twenty-fourth day of that run of days he has not missed.

About four of those four counted it in front of him for the whole of that hour and said nothing to him and nothing about him, and he has never once been asked by anybody in this harbor what he is there for.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a Sunday on which four people stood in front of a wall for an hour and not one of the four of them said where his figures came from, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had ninety-one lines on it.

ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE STOOD IN FRONT OF IT AND NOT ONE OF THE FOUR SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE HAD A FIGURE, AND THE FIGURES ARE ON A PIECE OF WOOD IN ONE MAN'S OWN COAT. THE SLATE WAS IN THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO'S OWN HAND FROM ABOUT A QUARTER TO ELEVEN UNTIL ABOUT ELEVEN AND WAS BACK IN THAT COAT BEFORE ONE. that that wall came off at about half past ten and stood bare forty minutes and went back up at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, at fifty-three minutes off and fifty-three back and forty standing bare, one hundred and forty-six the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-sixth time; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason not one of those four said out loud that he had a figure is that a figure said out loud in a lane becomes a figure other people may be told is wrong by, and that four men who have never once been asked where their figures come from have never had to find out; that the reason the man of about thirty-two said nothing at a quarter to one on that Sunday is that he had worked out on the Saturday that the first thing anybody would do with a figure said out loud is ask which morning it was taken, and that there is no morning on that wood, and that a figure with no morning behind it cannot survive being said out loud; that the reason one man of those four saw him put the wood back in that coat and said nothing is that the same four people have been in that lane on six Sundays and have said nothing on all six, and that saying nothing is the whole of the practice they have in common and nobody taught it to any of them; that the reason about four of those four had got two figures that were a minute apart is that four pairs of eyes and two clocks will not agree, and that a disagreement of a minute is not a mistake and is not settled by anybody, because there is nobody in that lane with the standing to settle one.
