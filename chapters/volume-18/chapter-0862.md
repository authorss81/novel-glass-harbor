# Chapter 862

*Tuesday the Twenty-Ninth Day of November, the Hundred and Ninth Wage Tuesday, a Chest on That Step From About Ten Until About Four and Twenty Envelopes in It and None of Them Opened, Twenty-Six Lines at One Hundred and Four Marks Standing Unmoved and the Hundred and Tenth Week Fallen Due at the Close of It, Nothing Put to Anybody in a Question at Any Hour of This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety-Five and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Forty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Forty-Four*

Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of November, with that market open from six and that rime gone off everything by about nine and the ground wet under foot until the wind got up at about eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is the hundred and ninth wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the first day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and thirty-two marks, five shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and twenty-two thousand five hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and sixty-five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventy-fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this second day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and sixtieth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixty-second day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundredth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixty-sixth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eighty-fourth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fifty-fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventy-first day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and second day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its eighty-eighth day. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety-three days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixty-first day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its sixtieth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and seven days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in two days. Three hundred and sixtieth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The chest stood on that step from about ten until about four on that Tuesday, and twenty envelopes were in it, and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody.

Twenty-six lines went on that column in September and not one line has been added to it since, and the whole of it stands at one hundred and four marks, being sixteen thousand six hundred and forty pence, and one mark is one hundred and sixty pence and there is no half of anything in that figure.

At about eleven the keeper wrote the number of this week in the column at the head of that sheet, and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and neither figure was read out to anybody.

Nobody in that yard has ever drawn a mark out of that column, and nobody in that harbor has ever asked what a line on it is for, and the whole of the hundred and ninth Tuesday is the length of a Tuesday and the two numbers of two weeks.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was two boats off that mole before noon on that wet ground and a hand-cart of chain out of the store and back in again, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and nobody in this harbor has set anything against either half of it.

---

A man of about thirty-one of that market stood on that step at about one for a while and said out loud that this is the ninth Tuesday running, and that nobody in this harbor has ever asked what the ninth one is for.

About four of that nine heard all of it, and nobody asked him, and he did not say it a second time, and he went back to the market without waiting for an answer.

---

At about half past one in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine said out loud that they had stopped keeping count of those wage Tuesdays in their own heads and were not going to start again.

They said it in about four different words, and the four words were that a man who counts a thing in his own head is doing the keeper's work badly for nothing, and that the hundred and ninth is a bigger number than the hundred and eighth and that neither of them is any use to anybody standing in a wet lane.

The keeper said out loud that he was not going to add that up in front of people, and that he would put it in a column or he would not put it anywhere at all.

Nobody put any of that to him as a question, and so there is nothing whatever against this day in the column of the asked, and a day with no figure in it is not a day with nothing in it and is not written as though it had nothing in it.

That market was busy at about one and quiet at about three, and a man of about fifty-seven came through that shed with a copying book under his arm and was not asked for anything by anybody in it.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twelfth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The man of about thirty-two came into that shed at about one and stayed until about three and said nothing out loud at all, and about four of that nine did not look at him while they were saying the thing about the counting they had stopped doing.

Seven hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and not one of those seven was the hand that wrote what either sheet went against, and the page of days carries seven against this day.

---

That shed stood empty at about five with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and the chest went off that step at about four in a locked state, and the hundred and tenth week stood fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of that Tuesday.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and ninety-four words, and that chest came first in it and the thing about the counting in their own heads came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the column of the asked carries nothing at all on this day and is not written as carrying nothing at all.

The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and two days of this run have gone by without a word.**"

Nobody was thanked for a Tuesday on which nothing at all was put to anybody, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy-nine lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND NINTH WAGE TUESDAY WENT PAST WITH TWENTY ENVELOPES IN A LOCKED CHEST AND NO LINE OF TWENTY-SIX MOVED A MARK AND THE HUNDRED AND TENTH WEEK FELLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF IT. NOTHING AT ALL WAS PUT TO ANYBODY IN A QUESTION ON THIS DAY, AND A DAY WITH NO FIGURE IN THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED IS NOT ENTERED AS THOUGH IT WERE EMPTY AND IS NOT CALLED EMPTY. THE WAGE COLUMN STANDS AT TWENTY-SIX LINES AND ONE HUNDRED AND FOUR MARKS, BEING SIXTEEN THOUSAND SIX HUNDRED AND FORTY PENCE, AND HAS TAKED NO LINE IN TWO DAYS. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FIFTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-ninth day of November at about six in the evening in that shed, being the second entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was the hundred and ninth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the first day of December; that the chest stood on that step from about ten until about four with twenty envelopes in it and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody; that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, being sixteen thousand six hundred and forty pence, and has taken no line since September; that at about eleven the keeper wrote the number of this week in the column at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and neither figure was read out to anybody; that a man of about thirty-one of that market stood on that step at about one and said out loud that this is the ninth Tuesday running and that nobody in this harbor has ever asked what the ninth one is for, and nobody asked him and he did not say it a second time; that at about half past one in that shed in about nine people about four of that nine said out loud that they had stopped keeping count of those wage Tuesdays in their own heads and were not going to start again; that the keeper said out loud that he was not going to add that up in front of people and that he would put it in a column or he would not put it anywhere at all; that nothing whatever was put to anybody in a question on the said day; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and twelfth day of that run; that the man of about thirty-two came into that shed at about one and stayed until about three and said nothing out loud at all; that the hundred and tenth week stood fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of the said Tuesday.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody asked a man of about thirty-one of that market what the ninth Tuesday is for is that a question put to the keeper about a wage he is not paying is a question about somebody else's twenty envelopes, and this harbor has worked out that a thing owed to a man is safer in a locked chest than in a conversation; that the reason four people in that shed gave up counting those Tuesdays in their own heads is that a number a man keeps in his own head is a number he cannot show anybody, and that after a hundred and eighth Tuesday a number nobody can show anybody is the only kind of figure that is worth carrying; that the reason that figure is not written down as a number is that a day with nothing in that column is a day nobody has anything to be asked about, and that a harbor which has a column of questions in it can turn any ordinary Tuesday into a day somebody is to blame for; that the reason twenty envelopes stayed shut from ten until four is that the chest is not a promise and is a place, and that the difference between a promise and a place is that a place can be opened by anybody with a key and a promise can be opened by nobody at all.
