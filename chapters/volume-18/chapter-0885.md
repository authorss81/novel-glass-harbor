# Chapter 885

*Thursday the Twenty-Second Day of December, the Twenty-Fifth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Twelfth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen, at About One in About Nine People with the Door Open and the Yard Going On the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud That Nobody Asked Him to Keep That Count and That He Cannot Name the Man He Has Counted a Hundred and Thirty-Seven Times and That His Own Good Manners Have Been a Tax on Somebody Else's Body for a Hundred and Thirty-Three Weeks, the Slate Still in That Coat and Not Out of It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Eighteen and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Sixty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Seventeen and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty-Nine*

Thursday the twenty-second day of December, with that market open from six and hail coming off that point in a slant from about eleven until about half past one and the ground white with it at two, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and settles nothing on a fitting-out line, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December and the next charge is on Monday the twenty-sixth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of December. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and twelfth time and it was walked on by none of it. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, is not read at any hour of that Thursday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninety-eighth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this twenty-fifth day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and eighty-third day and no answer has come back off it. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and eighty-fifth day of not being asked a question. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody stand at the nine hundred and twenty-third day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and eighty-ninth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and seventh day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and sixty-second day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and seventy-seventh day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its four hundred and ninety-fourth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twenty-fifth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and eleventh day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and sixteenth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eighty-fourth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for eighty-three days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and thirty days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-five days. Three hundred and eighty-third days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Nobody put a stick into that water at any hour of that Thursday, and that butt was filled for the two hundred and twelfth time at about ten past ten with two men on the handles, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and put it down against that door at about one for a rest and picked it up again, and not one figure of any kind stands in any page in this harbor against that man or against anything that he does.

That is his figure on that job every Monday and Thursday since the middle of August and about four of that nine did not know it, and one of that nine did and has not said anything about it in twenty-five days.

---

At about one, in about nine people, with that door open and that yard going on outside it, the man of about thirty-two said out loud that nobody had ever asked him to keep that count, and that he had spent three days looking for the person who had, and that he could not find anybody.

Then he said out loud that he could not name that man and has never known the name, and that he has counted that man's labour a hundred and thirty-seven times, and about four of those nine stopped what they were doing and about four of those nine did not.

Then he said out loud that the half hour it takes to put that thing back up is the whole of the time he has ever stood in that lane with that man in it, and that it is the same half hour every Sunday, and that he has never once been on the far side of it with him.

---

Then he said out loud that he began counting because somebody else was counting, and that he has never in three days been able to bring back who that was or what they had said or what they had said the figure was.

Then he said out loud that wanting to be able to say a number is not the same as being asked to keep one, and that nobody in that yard had ever asked him for a number about anything at any hour.

Then he said out loud that the count has been his own good manners, and that he has carried that wood in the inside of one coat for a hundred and thirty-three weeks without anybody asking him to carry it.

Then he said out loud that his own good manners have been a tax on somebody else's body for a hundred and thirty-three weeks, and about four of that nine heard that sentence and about four of that nine did not hear it because of the noise from that yard.

He stood with his hands at his sides after that and did not put them in his pockets, and the hail had stopped by then and the yard outside was louder than it had been in the morning, and about four of that nine looked at the boards of that shed floor and about four of that nine looked at the open door of it, and not one of them looked at him.

That is the first time in this run that anybody in that yard has said out loud that they had put a cost on somebody else and had not known they were doing it.

---

Nobody in that shed said one word back to any of that.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that it had not been known in that yard that any of it was about a man's body at all, and then stopped, and said nothing else, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that either.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a figure a man keeps about his own work is one thing and a figure about somebody else is another thing, and that this harbor has a great deal of the first of them and has never had one of the second on any page. And it was given in these words. "Nobody in this harbor has ever put a man on a page. Not one of them."

Not one of that nine said anything to him about going on with it and not one of that nine said anything to him about stopping, and one of them said out loud that they were not going to be the one to tell him either, and gave it in these words. "Not from me. He carries it or he puts it down, and either of those two is his own."

---

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about one, and she went inside at one and did not come out again until about two, and he said all of that in that shed between those two hours and did not say one word of it to her at any hour of that Thursday.

The man of about twenty-seven of that second lay was in that shed at about one and heard about half of that and said nothing at any hour of that day.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was two boats off that mole before noon in that hail and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store with a rope over it and half that rope gone before the cart was in, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirty-fifth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-one days and has stood in that lane on seven Sundays and said nothing on any of the seven.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

**AT ABOUT ONE ON THIS THURSDAY, IN ABOUT NINE PEOPLE, WITH THAT DOOR OPEN, THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO SAID OUT LOUD THAT NOBODY HAD EVER ASKED HIM TO KEEP THAT COUNT, THAT HE COULD NOT NAME THAT MAN AND HAS NEVER KNOWN THE NAME, THAT HE HAS COUNTED THAT MAN'S LABOUR A HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVEN TIMES, AND THAT HIS OWN GOOD MANNERS HAVE BEEN A TAX ON SOMEBODY ELSE'S BODY FOR A HUNDRED AND THIRTY-THREE WEEKS. THE SLATE WAS NOT OUT OF THAT COAT AT ANY HOUR OF THIS DAY. NOT ONE OF THAT NINE SAID ONE WORD BACK TO ANY OF IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-EIGHTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody in that shed said one word back is that a sentence about somebody else's body is a claim, and this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and a man is not one of them, and that a room of nine people has no shape for what to do with one; that the reason about four of that nine did not hear the last sentence is that he had chosen the moment the yard outside was loudest, and that a man who has been keeping a count in his own head for a hundred and thirty-seven weeks has had six weeks to work out which hour of the working day carries the most noise in it; that the reason the man of about twenty-seven heard about half and said nothing is that he has said one thing out loud about that lane in six weeks and that thing was about last year and nobody ever gave him a figure back, and that a man who has been left without a figure once does not interrupt a man who has been left without one for a hundred and thirty-seven; that the reason nothing said to him about going on and nothing said to him about stopping is that the standing to do either of those things does not exist in this harbor and has not existed since the fourteenth of August, and that twenty-five days of not asking him a question has not created one.*
