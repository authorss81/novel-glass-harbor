# Chapter 864

*Thursday the First Day of December, the Fourth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Sixth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten, Two Men and a Barrow and Fourteen Trips and Walked On by None of It, a Figure for the Hundredth Time That Cistern Was Charged and a Figure for the Two Hundredth and Nothing at All for What the Two Hundred and Sixth Is For, the Covers On at About Eleven and Off at About Four, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety-Seven and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety-Six and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Thursday the first day of December, with that market open from six and a raw close morning with a haar coming off that point at about eleven and gone again by one, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the sixth day of December. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and sixth time and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and thirty-two marks, five shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and twenty-two thousand five hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and sixty-five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventy-seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and sixty-second day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixty-fourth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and second day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixty-eighth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eighty-sixth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and forty-first day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fifty-sixth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventy-third day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and fourth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its ninetieth day. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety-five days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixty-third day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its sixty-second day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and nine days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in four days. Three hundred and sixty-second days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That fourteenth trip went at about ten past ten with two men and a barrow, and the other man on it is an old man of about sixty-eight and has carried ten of those fourteen trips on every Monday and Thursday since the middle of August without once being asked to carry fewer.

The covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and the water stood where it stood, and nothing was measured and nobody measured it.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was two boats off that mole before noon with that haar sitting flat on the water, and a coil of small stuff taken off a hook and put back on it, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and there is no column anywhere in this harbor that either half of it belongs in.

A man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had come down that lane with two pails on the Monday and again on that Thursday, and both times he carried them up again himself, and both times there was water running off him onto the flags of that yard by the time he got to the top.

About four fathoms of water is what that mole carries on that flat morning, and two boats went off it before noon and came back at about two, and about four of that nine said out loud that a thing under four fathoms is a thing a man can see the bottom of.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine said out loud that there is a figure for the hundredth time that cistern was charged and a figure for the two hundredth time, and nothing at all for what the two hundred and sixth time is for.

The keeper said out loud that a cistern is a cistern, and that he charges it on the days it comes, and that the two hundred and sixth of them is a Thursday and he does not see any harm in a Thursday being a Thursday.

About four of that nine said out loud that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been thirty days in that harbor and had asked nobody for anything on any of the thirty, and that he carried two of those pails himself on the Monday, and that nobody in that shed had the least idea what he was doing here.

Nobody said out loud what they thought that man was here for, and nobody put it to him as a question.

---

Those nine counters went up one each at the close of that Thursday, and the hearing of the man of about twenty-nine went to three hundred and sixty-two days, and no figure of any kind was set against that hearing then or afterwards.

---

A man of about fifty-eight of that market stood at the back of that end stall with his slate under his arm for about an hour that afternoon and said nothing to anybody, and about four of that nine said out loud that a figure put on a board cannot be taken off again, which is a rule of his and not a rule of this harbor's.

At about four in the afternoon a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place went up that lane and stopped at that reeve's bench for a moment and then went on up past it.

That wall was up behind that bench with the mortar showing on one whole face of it, and it has shown on one face of it on every one of the Sundays this harbor can remember, and there is no day on any page in this harbor on which it began showing, and nobody in that lane can name one, and nobody in that lane asked.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fourteenth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and not one of those nine was the hand that wrote what any sheet went against, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

That market shut at about four and the flags outside that stall were running. That shed stood empty at about five with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had eighty-one lines on it, and nothing whatever was put to anybody in a question at any hour of that Thursday, and a day with no figure in the column of the asked is not a day written as having nothing in it.

---

The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced and is not improved. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and four days of this run have gone by without one single word about any of them.**"

Nobody was thanked for a cistern filled on a Thursday by two men and an old man, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had eighty-one lines on it.

**THE TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTH CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN AT ABOUT TEN PAST TEN AND TOOK FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, AND THERE IS A FIGURE FOR THE HUNDREDTH OF THESE AND A FIGURE FOR THE TWO HUNDREDTH AND NOTHING AT ALL FOR WHAT THAT TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTH IS FOR. NOTHING AT ALL WAS PUT TO ANYBODY IN A QUESTION ON THIS DAY, AND A DAY WITH NO FIGURE IN THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED IS NOT ENTERED AS THOUGH IT WERE EMPTY AND IS NOT CALLED EMPTY. NOTHING CAME INTO THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET AND NO LINE WAS WORKED ON IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Thursday the first day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the fourth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the sixth day of December; that that cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten with two men and a barrow and it was the two hundred and sixth time and it was walked on by none of it, and that the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four and nothing was measured; that at about one in that shed in about nine people about four of that nine said out loud that there is a figure for the hundredth time that cistern was charged and a figure for the two hundredth time and nothing at all for what the two hundred and sixth time is for; that the keeper said out loud that a cistern is a cistern and that he charges it on the days it comes; that about four of that nine said out loud that a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been thirty days in that harbor and had asked nobody for anything on any of the thirty; that those nine counters went up one each at the close of the said day and the hearing of the man of about twenty-nine went to three hundred and sixty-two days, and no figure of any kind was set against that hearing; that at about four in the afternoon a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place went up that lane and stopped at that reeve's bench for a moment and then went on up past it; that that wall was up behind that bench with the mortar showing on one whole face of it, and that it has shown on one face of it on every one of the Sundays this harbor can remember, and that there is no day on any page in this harbor on which it began showing and nobody in that lane can name one and nobody in that lane asked; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and fourteenth day of that run; that nothing whatever was put to anybody in a question on the said day.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the keeper would not give the two hundred and sixth of those charges a figure is that the hundredth and the two hundredth are round numbers a man can say in a shed without stopping, and that a figure is a thing a person can hold in the mouth, and that the two hundred and sixth is not round and is not a thing anybody can hold in the mouth; that the reason nobody in that lane asked on which day the mortar began showing on one face of that wall is that a wall which is put up and taken down in the same half hour cannot be looked at properly by anybody, and that a question put into that half hour is a question nobody can answer until it is too late to matter; that the reason a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been thirty days in that harbor and has asked nobody for anything is that a person who came from four days up a coast road and wants nothing is the hardest kind of person in a yard to work out, and that six weeks of figures have not given this harbor one way of working out anybody who is not asking; that the reason nothing whatever was put to anybody in a question on that Thursday is that a Thursday with fourteen trips in it and two boats off the mole is a day a yard does not have the breath left on, and that a day nobody has breath on is a day the questions do not get asked on, and that is not the same as a day with nothing to ask.
