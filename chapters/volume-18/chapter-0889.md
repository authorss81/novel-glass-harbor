# Chapter 889

*Monday the Twenty-Sixth Day of December, the Twenty-Ninth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Thirteenth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen, Line Six Settled at About Four at Four Hundred and Sixty-Four Pence, Being Two Marks and Twelve Shillings, the Whole of That Foot Read Out Loud After That Settlement and Not Before It at About a Quarter Past Four, at One Hundred and Thirty-Eight Thousand Nine Hundred and Six Pence Out and One Hundred and Twenty-Eight Thousand Two Hundred and Sixty-Six Pence Short, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-Two and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Eighty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-One and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Monday the twenty-sixth day of December, with that market open from six and a bright hard morning that put a white rim on every rail in that yard before nine and then stood still all day without a cloud coming over that point, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles one line on that fitting-out sheet and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-ninth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of December. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and thirteenth time and it was walked on by none of it. That sixth line settled at about four at four hundred and sixty-four pence, being two marks and twelve shillings, and that foot is read after that settlement and on no other day of this run before the last day of it. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and second days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this twenty-ninth day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and eighty-seventh day and no answer has come back off it. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and eighty-ninth day of not being asked a question. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody stand at the nine hundred and twenty-seventh day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and ninety-third night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and eleventh day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and sixty-sixth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-first day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its four hundred and ninety-eighth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twenty-ninth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and fifteenth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and twentieth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eighty-eighth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for eighty-seven days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and thirty-fourth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-nine days. Three hundred and eighty-seventh days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The two hundred and thirteenth charge went onto that cistern at about ten past ten, and it was the two hundred and thirteenth time, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody walked on any of it.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and about four of that nine said out loud that he will go up ten of those fourteen again on Thursday and about four of that nine said nothing about it.

Not one figure of any kind stands in any page in this harbor against that man or against anything that he does, and that has been the position since the middle of August and is not going to be argued about by anybody in this harbor in this run.

---

That sixth line settled at about four at four hundred and sixty-four pence, being two marks and twelve shillings, and about four of that nine stood round that bench stone at the end of that mole while it was done.

At about a quarter past four the keeper read the whole of that foot out loud in about nine people, after that settlement and not before it, and that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence.

The second figure of that foot was said out loud at the same moment by the keeper and by two men in that shed, and all three of them had it right, and no page in this harbor has ever said that any figure could be got right three times over.

---

One of about four of that nine said out loud that four people stood in that lane yesterday morning and counted a thing that four people have been counting since the middle of August, and that nobody in that shed has told those four to go on and nobody has told them to stop.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that he does not know who told them and he does not know where any of them came from, and that he is not going to go up there on Sunday morning and tell them to stop, and the keeper said nothing back to that. The words he gave that in were these. "I do not know one of the four of them."

Nobody in that shed said out loud that those four ought to go on either, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had thought about saying it and had not, and nothing whatever was settled in that yard on that Monday about the four of them.

Nobody in that shed asked after those four again that afternoon, and that yard went on at that end of it with what that yard goes on with, and about four of that nine carried the four of them about with them and about four of that nine did not.

---

That piece of wood was lying face up on that bench at about half past five and has not been taken off that bench by anybody since about ten past four on Friday, and about four of that nine have walked past it every day this week and not one of them has said anything about it.

The keeper said out loud that a thing lying on a bench is not a figure in that yard and is not a record and is not a page, and about four of that nine said out loud that it is the only thing in this harbor that is none of those three and is still the truest thing anybody has got, and he said nothing back to that.

---

The ordinary work of that Monday was three boats off that mole before noon in flat water and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store with a tarpaulin over it and that tarpaulin gone entirely before the cart was in the yard, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about half past two, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about eleven until about five, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirty-ninth day of that run of days he has not missed.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-five days by that Monday and has asked nobody for anything on any of the fifty-five.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

LINE SIX SETTLED AT ABOUT FOUR AT FOUR HUNDRED AND SIXTY-FOUR PENCE AND THE FOOT WAS READ AFTER THAT SETTLEMENT AND NOT BEFORE IT, AT ONE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHT THOUSAND NINE HUNDRED AND SIX PENCE OUT AND ONE HUNDRED AND TWENTY-EIGHT THOUSAND TWO HUNDRED AND SIXTY-SIX PENCE SHORT. *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody said out loud that those four ought to go on is that saying that is the same act as standing in that lane on Sunday morning with a figure of one's own, and that about four people in this yard have just watched a man give that act up and none of them wants to be the second one; that the reason one man said out loud that he is not going to go up there and tell them to stop is that a man who has been in that lane on a Sunday does not know who he would be speaking to at the top of it, and that this harbor has four people standing there tomorrow who have not been asked anything by anybody; that the reason the keeper said nothing back to that is that a keeper who has read a foot out loud after a settlement for six weeks has found that saying nothing is the only way a room of nine people keeps anything straight, and that he is not going to spend that on a Sunday morning; that the reason nobody has taken that piece of wood off that bench in three days is that there is nowhere in this harbor a thing like it is supposed to go, and that this is the first time in six weeks that a figure has been put down in this harbor and had no place to be taken to, and that about four of that nine worked that out on Friday and have not been able to do anything about it since.*
