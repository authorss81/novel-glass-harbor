# Chapter 895

*Sunday the First Day of January, the Thirty-Fifth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-Ninth Time, That Wall Off the Face of Itself at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Forty-Three Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One and Not Going Up Whole, Fifty-Six Off and Fifty-Six Back and One Hundred and Fifty-Five the Whole of It, Both Ends a Minute Longer Than the Two Ends of the Sunday Before Ran and Nobody in That Lane Saying Why and Nobody Asking, About Four People in Front of It, Three of Whom Walked Into That Shed on Saturday the Thirty-First Day of December, the Man of About Thirty-One of That Lowfen Reed's Own Place on His Ninth Sunday in That Lane Saying Nothing, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-Eight and Three Thousand and Fourteen and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-Seven and the Second at Three Thousand and Nine*

Sunday the first day of January, with that market shut until seven on a Sunday and a blue morning off that point with the wind coming round at that quay in a way it had not come round in a fortnight, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage and settles nothing on a fitting-out line, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Monday the second day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January. That wall behind that reeve's bench came off the face of itself at about half past ten and stood bare forty-three minutes and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at fifty-six minutes off and fifty-six back, one hundred and fifty-five the whole of it, and that is the hundred and thirty-ninth time anybody has done it to it. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, is not read at any hour of that Sunday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and eighth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this thirty-fifth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and ninety-third day. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninety-fifth day of not being asked anything by anybody. The two things upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirty-third day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and ninety-ninth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and seventeenth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are at the four hundred and seventy-second day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-seventh day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and fourth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-fifth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and twenty-first day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and twenty-sixth days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-fourth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety-three days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fortieth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place has been in that harbor sixty-one days and has asked nobody for anything on any of them. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-five days. Three hundred and ninety-third days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

That market was shut until seven on that Sunday and that whole quay was empty in a way it is empty eleven days out of every four hundred, and about four of that nine said out loud that the only reason anybody stands in that lane on a Sunday morning is that the rest of that quay has nothing on it.

The sheet with the fifteen gaps has stood at fifteen since the twenty-fifth day of September and about four of that nine said out loud that fifteen is the one figure in this harbor nobody has ever been able to account for, and that it is not written in anybody's hand and never has been.

---

At about half past ten the top course came off that wall and about four people stepped up to it and began to count, and it stood bare forty-three minutes and went back up at a quarter to one with the top course of it left short, one hundred and fifty-five the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-ninth time.

Both ends of it ran a minute longer than the two ends of the Sunday before ran, and nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it.

For the third Sunday running nobody in that lane has said why and nobody in that lane has asked.

---

Three of those four were the three men who walked into that shed at about four on Saturday, and the fourth was a man of about twenty-six who has not been in that yard at any hour of any day that this run has had.

They said their own figures out loud to one another, and about two of those four figures were a minute apart, and nobody in that lane told any of them to do that and nobody in that lane told any of them to stop.

The man of about forty-one said his figure twice out loud to make sure of it and said nothing else at any hour of that Sunday.

---

A man came down that lane at about ten and was in it for the half hour it takes him and went away again before a quarter to one, and he did not speak to any of those four and none of those four spoke to him.

About four of that nine said out loud that a man of about forty-one who says his figure twice to be sure of it is doing in that lane what about four people in this shed have been doing in that yard for nine days without saying so, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

A man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place stood in that lane from about half past ten until about a quarter to one and watched four people count and said nothing at any hour of it, and that is the ninth Sunday he has stood in that lane and he has not said one word on any of the nine. He had been in that harbor sixty-one days by that Sunday and has asked nobody for anything on any of the sixty-one.

The man of about thirty-two was not in that lane at any hour of that Sunday and did not stand in front of that thing, and he was in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

---

The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon in that wind and a hand-cart of coke left standing in the open because there was nowhere dry to put it, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for nine days and nobody has touched it at any hour of that Sunday at all.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page until about two and did not come into that shed at any hour of that Sunday, and the man of about thirty-two did not tell her one word about anything.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-fifth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

**THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-NINTH TIME THAT WALL CAME DOWN WENT DOWN BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND STOOD BARE FORTY-THREE MINUTES AND WENT BACK UP AT A QUARTER TO ONE AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, FIFTY-SIX AND FORTY-THREE AND FIFTY-SIX AND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-FIVE THE WHOLE OF IT, AND BOTH ENDS RAN A MINUTE LONGER THAN THE TIME BEFORE AND NOBODY SAID WHY AND NOBODY ASKED. ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE STOOD IN FRONT OF IT AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO WAS NOT ONE OF THEM. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS THREE HUNDRED AND EIGHTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the first day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirty-fifth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was a wipe day and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Monday the second day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January; that that wall came off the face of itself at about half past ten and stood bare forty-three minutes and went back up at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, at fifty-six minutes off and fifty-six back, one hundred and fifty-five the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-ninth time; that both ends of it ran a minute longer than the two ends of the Sunday before ran and that nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it, and that this is the third Sunday running that nobody in that lane has said why and nobody has asked; that about four people stood in front of it, and that three of those four were the three men who came into that shed at about four on Saturday, and that the fourth was a man of about twenty-six who has not been in that yard at any hour; that the four of them said their own figures out loud to one another and about two of those four figures were a minute apart; that nobody in that lane told any of them to do that and nobody in that lane told any of them to stop; that the man of about forty-one said his figure twice out loud to make sure of it and said nothing else at any hour of that Sunday; that a man came down that lane at about ten and was in it for the half hour it takes him, and that about four people in that lane would not be able to describe him that afternoon; that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place stood in that lane from about half past ten until about a quarter to one and said nothing, being the ninth Sunday he has stood in that lane; that the man of about thirty-two was not in that lane at any hour of that Sunday and did not stand in front of that thing; that that piece of wood has been face up on that bench for nine days and nobody has touched it; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and forty-fifth day of that run; and that twelve hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason no question has been put in that lane on any Sunday of the last three is that nobody in that lane has the standing to ask anybody anything and has not had one since the fourteenth of August, and that a lane with nobody in it who may ask a question is a lane where four men can stand and count a thing for a hundred and thirty-eight weeks without one of them ever being asked what he is doing there; that the reason the man of about forty-one said his own figure twice out loud is that he had been one minute out with another man that morning and had to know which of the two of them was wrong, and that a man who has to know which of two figures of his own is the wrong one is a man who has begun to need other people; that the reason nobody told any of those four to go and nobody told any of them to stop is that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and a thing that happens at half past ten on a Sunday in a lane is not any of those four; that the reason the man of about thirty-two was in that shed at half past five and not in that lane is not given out loud by anybody in this harbor and is not a thing he has told anybody, and that a man who put a piece of wood down in the rain on a Friday and stopped counting has not said out loud that he will not stand in a lane again, and that what a man does not say is not the same as what a man has promised.*
