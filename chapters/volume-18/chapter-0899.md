# Chapter 899

*Thursday the Fifth Day of January, the Thirty-Ninth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Sixteenth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen and Putting That Barrow Down at About One for About Four Minutes and Picking It Up Again After That, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirty-Two and Three Thousand and Thirty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirty-One and the Second at Three Thousand and Twenty-Nine*

Thursday the fifth day of January, with that market open from six and a grey morning that came off that point without any wind in it and gave that quay a flat listless sort of cold all day, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Monday the ninth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and sixteenth time and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Thursday, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Thursday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and twelfth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this thirty-ninth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at its five hundred and ninety-seventh day. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninety-ninth day of not being asked anything. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirty-seventh day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its seven hundred and third night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and twenty-first day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are at the four hundred and seventy-sixth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and ninety-first day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and eighth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-ninth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and twenty-fifth day. That correction and that refusal have stood at that end stall one hundred and thirtieth days at one size with the wrong figure still showing underneath and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-eighth day and it has six lines on it and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety-seven days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-fourth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of those columns stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-nine days. Three hundred and ninety-seventh days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The two hundred and sixteenth charge went onto that cistern at about ten past ten, and it took fourteen trips by hand with two men on the handles, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody put a stick into that water at any hour of that Thursday.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder, and at about one he put it down against that door and sat on the step beside it for about four minutes, and did not pick it up again at once.

About four of that nine watched him do that and wrote nothing down about it.

Not one figure of any kind stands in any page in this harbor against that man or against anything that he does, and that has been the position since the middle of August.

The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-eighth day and it has six lines on it and it says on its own face that it was made under pressure and can be repaired, and about four of that nine said out loud that it is the only writing in this harbor anybody is allowed to take back.

About four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that shed has ever watched that man before and that nobody there is going to start, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

About four of that nine said out loud that four minutes on a step is the first time any of that nine has seen him not be in any hurry, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that either.

---

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for thirteen days and nobody has touched it.

He looked down at that wood once that Thursday at about half past four on his way out of that shed and went out of the door, and about four of that nine noticed that and about four of that nine did not.

And one of about four of that nine said out loud that a thing thirteen days old on a bench in a working yard has stopped being a thing anybody looks at and become a fixture, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Thursday.

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty-five days by that Thursday and stood at the back of that shed for about two hours of it and said nothing and asked nobody for anything.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four and then carried it inside, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

The ordinary work of that Thursday was two boats off that mole before noon in that flat cold and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store with a tarpaulin over it and that tarpaulin gone entirely before the cart was in the yard, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-ninth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

Nobody was thanked for a Thursday on which an old man sat on a step for about four minutes and about four people watched him do it and wrote nothing down, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and sixteen lines on it.

*First column, entered on Thursday the fifth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirty-ninth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Monday the ninth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January; that that cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and sixteenth time and it was walked on by none of it, and that the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four; that the old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen and at about one put that barrow down against that door and sat on the step beside it for about four minutes and did not pick it up again at once; that about four of that nine watched him do that and wrote nothing down about it; that not one figure of any kind stands against that man or against anything he does; that about four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that shed has ever watched that man before and that nobody there is going to start; that that piece of wood has been face up on that bench for thirteen days and that nobody has touched it; that the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing; that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty-five days and stood at the back of that shed for about two hours and said nothing; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and forty-ninth day of that run; and that ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason about four people in that shed watched an old man sit down on a step for about four minutes is that six weeks of saying figures out loud in that yard has made four of them watch anything anybody in that yard does that takes longer than a moment, and that this is not about that man and is about what four people have become; that the reason nobody wrote it down is that a figure about a man's back is a figure about a man, and that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and a man is not going to be one of them, and that the four of them worked that out in about half a second and about four of the nine said nothing back; that the reason a piece of wood on a bench has become a fixture in that yard is that a yard cannot have two things in it that nobody will take away, and that this yard has had one of those since about ten past four on the twenty-third day of December; that the reason the man of about thirty-one has been in that harbor sixty-five days without once saying what he is here for is that nobody in this harbor has the standing to require an answer of anybody, and that a man who is owed an answer by nobody may go on standing at the back of a shed for as long as the market stands there.*
