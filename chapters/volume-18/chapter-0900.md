# Chapter 900

*Friday the Sixth Day of January, the Fortieth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Friday Carrying No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, That Piece of Wood Fourteen Days Face Up on That Bench and Nobody Going Near It, None of Those Four in That Shed at Any Hour of That Friday and Nobody in That Yard Saying Out Loud Where Any of Them Are, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirty-Three and Three Thousand and Thirty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirty-Two and the Second at Three Thousand and Thirty-Four*

Friday the sixth day of January, with that market open from six and a soft morning that came up that quay out of a low sky with no rain in it and no wind either, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Friday is an ordinary working Friday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Monday the ninth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Friday and nothing is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Friday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and thirteenth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this fortieth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and ninety-eighth day. That bellwright goes into the six hundredth day of not being asked a question. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirty-eighth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its seven hundred and fourth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and twenty-second day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are at the four hundred and seventy-seventh day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and ninety-second day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and fortieth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and twenty-sixth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and thirty-first days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-ninth day and it has six lines on it and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety-eight days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-fifth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty days. Three hundred and ninety-eighth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Not one of those four was in that shed at any hour of that Friday, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that yard has any way of finding out where any of the four of them are on a Friday.

Nobody in that shed said out loud that those four will be up that lane in the morning, and nobody in that shed said out loud that they will not be, and about four of that nine said nothing at all about it.

The man of about twenty-seven of that second lay was in that shed from about eleven until about one and said nothing at any hour of that Friday, and about four of that nine noticed that and about four of that nine did not.

---

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for fourteen days, and about four of that nine said out loud at about four that a fortnight is the first figure anybody in that yard has had for anything in this run that was not on a page, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that at all.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a piece of wood that has lain on a bench for a fortnight is not a thing anybody in that yard can be said to have done anything about, and that this is the same as it being a fixture and is not the same as it being a record.

About four of that nine said out loud that three of those four walked into that shed on Saturday the thirty-first day of December and stood in a doorway for about ten minutes, and that about four of that nine have thought since then about a Sunday morning coming and about four of that nine have not yet.

Nobody in that shed picked it up and nobody in that shed gave it to anybody and nobody wrote one figure on it at any hour of that Friday.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Friday, and about four of that nine said out loud that a man who has said almost nothing for ten days is not a man who is thinking of nothing.

That is not a question and nobody in that shed put it to anybody.

About four of that nine said out loud that on the Tuesday of the week before last he carried a stove pipe up to that second lay on his own back, and that since then he has not put his hand on anything in that yard that belongs to another person, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell has been on that board for its hundred and twenty-sixth day, and about four of that nine said out loud that it is the only object in this harbor that nobody here can hold up and read the other side of, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the two of them are still married, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it, and she does not know that he has been counting anything.

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty-six days by that Friday and stood at the back of that shed for about three hours of it and said nothing and asked nobody for anything.

About four of that nine said out loud at about half past three on that Friday that sixty-six days is the longest that anybody in that yard remembers anybody standing at the back of that shed without once being asked for anything, and about four of that nine said out loud that this harbor does not have anybody in it to ask anybody, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

The ordinary work of that Friday was three boats off that mole before noon on a flat sea and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store with a rope over it and half that rope gone before the cart was in, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fiftieth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason not one of those four was in that shed on that Friday is that a man who stands in a lane at half past ten on a Sunday has a Friday to be anywhere else in and that this harbor has been their whole week since the thirty-first day of December; that the reason nobody in that shed said out loud that those four will be up that lane in the morning is that a prediction about four people is a claim about four people and this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and a claim is one of them; that the reason a fortnight is the first figure anybody in that yard has had for anything in this run that was not on a page is that fourteen days is what a thing takes before it stops being a thing that happened on one afternoon and becomes a condition of that yard, and that about four people in that shed noticed the same number at the same moment on the same afternoon and said out loud that it was a figure and not one of them wrote it down; that the reason nobody in that shed said one word to the other about any part of any of it is that a thing said in a room with two people in it is a thing that gets settled in a room with two people in it, and that this one is not.*
