# Chapter 872

*Friday the Ninth Day of December, the Twelfth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, Line Three Settled at About Four at One Thousand Two Hundred Pence, Being Seven Marks, Six Shillings and Eightpence, and the Whole of That Foot Read Out Loud After That Settlement at About a Quarter Past Four and Not Before It, the Foot Standing at One Hundred and Thirty-Six Thousand Two Hundred and Fifty Pence Out and One Hundred and Twenty-Five Thousand Six Hundred and Ten Pence Short, the Slate Left in the Coat of the Man of About Thirty-Two All Day and Not Taken Out, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Five and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Ninety-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Four and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Ninety-Four*

Friday the ninth day of December, with that market open from six and a clear thin morning with no rime on anything and the first of that frost gone off that mole head by ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Friday is an ordinary working Friday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe, and it moves two figures on that sheet and nothing else, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eleventh day of December and the next charge is on Monday the twelfth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the thirteenth day of December. That third line settled at about four at one thousand two hundred pence, being seven marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that foot standing after this afternoon's settlement at one hundred and thirty-six thousand two hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-one marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and twenty-five thousand six hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-five marks and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighty-fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twelfth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventieth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventy-second day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and tenth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventy-sixth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninety-fourth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and forty-ninth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixty-fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-first day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twelfth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its ninety-eighth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and three days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-first day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventieth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and seventeen days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twelve days. Three hundred and seventieth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That third line settled at about four at one thousand two hundred pence, being seven marks, six shillings and eightpence, and about four of that nine stood round that bench stone at the end of that mole while the keeper did it.

The keeper wrote the settlement against that line and against the twelve days of December written at the head of it, and against no other line, and a settlement moves two figures at the foot of that sheet and does not touch a third.

At about a quarter past four he read the whole of that foot out loud in about nine people, after that settlement and not before it.

That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-six thousand two hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-one marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and twenty-five thousand six hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-five marks and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.

About four of that nine had that figure before he said it and about four of that nine did not, and nobody wrote it down out loud and nobody repeated it after him.

---

The keeper read the five parts of that third line back out loud in about four people before he settled it, and they were three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at three times one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four pence, being three marks and two shillings; coal and oil for three days at sixty pence, being five shillings; a hundred and twenty yards of line at three pence the yard at one hundred and twenty times three for three hundred and sixty pence, being two marks, three shillings and fourpence; two bell seats cut and faced at eighteen pence for thirty-six pence, being three shillings; and five men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at five times three times sixteen for two hundred and forty pence, being one mark, six shillings and eightpence.

Those two bell seats were still lying on that stone, and about four of that nine said out loud that a stranger could tell what those two things were for by looking at them and could not tell what anything else on that sheet was.

About four of that nine said out loud that the twelve days of December had been written at the head of that line on the Wednesday it was worked, and that it is the seventh day this harbor has had written on anything in advance.


---

The keeper said out loud that a settlement is the thing that makes the two figures at the foot of that sheet one figure instead of two, and that a number taken out of that foot before a settlement is a number about what somebody meant to do.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that that is the first thing anybody in that yard has said this month that is not about money, and about four of those nine said out loud that it was about money.

---

The man of about thirty-two stood at that stone and heard both of those numbers read out on that Friday afternoon, and neither one of them told him anything about the wood in his coat.

That was the whole of what he wanted off that foot, and it was that he has never once known which of two readings any of those figures was taken at, and that no page in that room carries either of those two readings at any hour, so that nobody alive could tell him which one his own column belongs to.

A figure written on a piece of wood is the same figure at four in the afternoon as at six in the morning, because nothing in this harbor stands an hour against it, and that is not a fault in the wood.

---

That Friday he did not take the slate out of that coat.

He had wanted a day off it on Wednesday and had wanted a day off it on Thursday and had not found one, and about four of that nine did not know the wood was in that room and one of that nine knew.

The ordinary work of that Friday was three boats off that mole before noon and a hand-cart of that case and those nails going up to the second lay and back again with the box open, and the box came back lighter than it went that morning, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

---

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, was at that bench stone from about ten until about eleven with her own page under her arm and read the face of that third line off the stone with her own eyes and did not touch it, and the man of about thirty-two stood on the other side of the same stone from about eleven until about half past eleven and looked at the same line and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twenty-second day of that run of days he has not missed.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and about four of those eleven could not read a word of what was on any of them and were not asked to, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO FOUND THAT NO PAGE IN THAT ROOM CARRIES EITHER READING OF THAT FOOT AT ANY HOUR, SO THAT HE CANNOT FIND OUT WHICH ONE HIS OWN COLUMN BELONGS TO. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-FIFTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Friday the ninth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the twelfth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Friday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eleventh day of December and the next charge is on Monday the twelfth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the thirteenth day of December; that that third line settled at about four at one thousand two hundred pence, being seven marks, six shillings and eightpence, and that the keeper wrote the settlement against that line and against the twelve days of December written at the head of it and against no other line; that at about a quarter past four the keeper read the whole of that foot out loud after that settlement and not before it, standing at one hundred and thirty-six thousand two hundred and fifty pence out and one hundred and twenty-five thousand six hundred and ten pence short against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in; that the man of about thirty-two stood at that stone and heard both of those numbers read out and neither one of them told him anything about the wood in his coat; that he has never once known which of two readings any of those figures was taken at, and that there is no page in that room that carries either of those two readings at any hour; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a settlement is what makes two figures one figure is that before it the two of them are two intentions and after it they are one thing that happened, and that a harbor has spent six weeks learning that a figure has an hour on it and has never once learned that a piece of wood has none; that the reason nobody in that shed could tell that man which reading his own column belonged to is that the question requires a page, and that a page is the only thing in this harbor that can hold an hour, and that there has never been one for that wood and there is not going to be one; that the reason he left the slate in the coat on that Friday after taking it out the day before is that a man who opens a thing twice running and finds nothing in it stops opening it, and that stopping is not the same as giving it up and is a different thing from handing it over; that the reason the keeper said that a number taken before a settlement is a number about what somebody meant to do is that this yard writes the day it settles on the face of every line, and that a line with two days on it is the only kind of writing in that harbor that admits what it was before it was a fact.
