# Chapter 875

*Monday the Twelfth Day of December, the Fifteenth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Ninth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, Line Four Settled at About Four at Seven Hundred and Fifty Pence, Being Four Marks, Nine Shillings and Twopence, and the Whole of That Foot Read Out Loud After That Settlement at About a Quarter Past Four, the Foot Standing at One Hundred and Thirty-Seven Thousand Pence Out and One Hundred and Twenty-Six Thousand Three Hundred and Sixty Pence Short, Two Settlements Inside Three Days and Four Lines Through on That Sheet Since the Second of December, the Slate Not Out of the Coat at Any Hour of This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Eight and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Fourteen and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Seven and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Nine*

Monday the twelfth day of December, with that market open from six and a dirty sky from the south-west with that rain coming sideways off that quay all morning and stopping at about two, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe, and it moves two figures on that sheet and nothing else, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighteenth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the fifteenth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the thirteenth day of December. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and ninth time and it was walked on by none of it. That fourth line settled at about four at seven hundred and fifty pence, being four marks, nine shillings and twopence, and that foot standing after this afternoon's settlement at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighty-eighth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this fifteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventy-third day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventy-fifth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirteenth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventy-ninth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninety-seventh day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fifty-second day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixty-seventh day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-fourth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and fifteenth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and first day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and six days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-fourth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-third day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in fifteen days. Three hundred and seventy-third days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The fourteenth load of that Monday went up at about ten past ten with two men on the handles, and it was the two hundred and ninth time, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody put a stick into that water at any hour.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with the barrow, and that is his figure on that job every Monday and Thursday since the middle of August, and about four of that nine did not know it.

---

That fourth line settled at about four at seven hundred and fifty pence, being four marks, nine shillings and twopence, and about four of that nine stood round that bench stone at the end of that mole while it was done.

At about a quarter past four the keeper read the whole of that foot out loud in about nine people, after that settlement and not before it, and that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.

About four of that nine had that figure before he said it and about four of that nine did not, and nobody wrote it down out loud and nobody repeated it after him.

---

The keeper said out loud that that yard has put four lines through since the second of December, and that an ordinary week in it settles one line or two and does nothing on the other days.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a sheet which settles twice in three days has had an unusual week, and about four of that nine agreed with that, and nobody in that shed went on to say what made the week unusual.

The only thing that had happened this week that no page in this harbor carries is a pair of figures on a piece of wood in the coat of one man standing in that shed, and about four of that nine did not know that, and he said nothing about it.
---

That Tuesday was the hundred and eleventh wage Tuesday and it was tomorrow, and the chest with the twenty envelopes in it comes out on that step at about ten and goes off it at about four, and about four of that nine said out loud that a chest which is out tomorrow and shut today is the most reliable thing in that yard.

---

On the wall in the room over that fish market there are four kinds of thing and no fifth kind of thing, being a claim and a public correction and a refusal and one other kind, and where a correction stands beside a refusal they are the same size and a hand's breadth apart, and about four of that nine said out loud that those four have not been added to in five weeks and that nobody knows what a fifth kind of thing would look like.

---

About four of that nine said out loud that a week with two settlements in it is a week somebody will remember, and that nobody in that shed would be able to say in a month which of those two they had been thinking of.

---

The ordinary work of that Monday was three boats off that mole before noon in that rain and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store with a tarpaulin over it and half that tarpaulin gone in a hundred yards, and neither of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and no man in this harbor is set against either half of it.

A man of about fifty-seven of that market came through that shed at about two with a copying book under his arm and was not asked for anything in it.

---

The slate was not out of that coat at any hour of that Monday, and about four of that nine did not know that wood was in that room and one knew and did not say.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about half past two, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about three until about five, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about any part of any of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twenty-fifth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---
