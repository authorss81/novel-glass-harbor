# Chapter 909

*Sunday the Fifteenth Day of January, the Forty-Ninth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Wipe and the Hundred and Forty-First Time, That Thing off the Face of Itself at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Forty-Five Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One and Not Going Up Whole, Fifty-Eight Off and Fifty-Eight Back and One Hundred and Sixty-One the Whole of It, Both Ends a Minute Longer Than the Two Ends of the Sunday Before Ran, About Four People in Front of It, Seven Figures Put Up on That Bare Wall of That Room Over That Store at One Size in a Keeper's Hand, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Forty-Two and Three Thousand and Eighty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Forty-One and the Second at Three Thousand and Seventy-Nine*

Sunday the fifteenth day of January, with that market shut until seven on a Sunday and a room above that store standing cold before six with a pale light in it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage and settles nothing on a fitting-out line, and there is no wipe after it inside that run and the next charge is on Monday the sixteenth day of January and there is no wage Tuesday after it inside that run. That thing behind that reeve's bench came off the face of itself at about half past ten and stood bare forty-five minutes and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at fifty-eight minutes off and fifty-eight back, one hundred and sixty-one the whole of it, and that is the hundred and forty-first time anybody has done it to it, being a hundred and forty weeks from the first time anybody did it to it. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, is not read at any hour of that Sunday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and twenty-second days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine tallies closed through that forty-ninth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is placed at its six hundred and seventh day. That bellwright is put into the six hundred and ninth day of not being asked anything by anybody. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are placed at the nine hundred and forty-seventh day. That bench beneath that pump was set bare again on its seven hundred and thirteenth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are marked at their five hundred and thirty-first day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are set at the four hundred and eighty-sixth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is found at its five hundred and first day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is kept at its five hundred and eighteenth day. The man of about fifty-one is found at the five hundred and forty-ninth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is found on its hundred and thirty-fifth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and fortieth days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is kept on its hundred and eighth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in that harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, has been on that community's own page for a hundred and seven days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store had stood bare for one hundred and fifty-fourth days until that Sunday and now carries seven figures at one size in a keeper's hand. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-nine days. Four hundred and seventh days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

That room above that store stood cold before six and showed nothing on its bare wall, and about four of that nine said out loud at about ten that a bare wall on a Sunday morning is a wall nobody has written on for a long time.

---

At about half past ten the top course came off that thing and about four people stepped up to it and began to count, and it stood bare forty-five minutes and went back up at a quarter to one with the top course of it left short, one hundred and sixty-one the whole of it, being the hundred and forty-first time.

Both ends of it ran a minute longer than the two ends of the Sunday before ran, and nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it.

For the fifth Sunday running nobody in that lane has said why and nobody in that lane has asked.

---

Those four are a man of about forty-one and a man of about thirty-three and a man of about fifty and a man of about twenty-six, and none of the four was in that shed at any hour of the six days before it, and none of the four is asked anything by anybody.

They said their own figures out loud to one another in that lane, and about two of those four figures were a minute apart, and nobody in that lane told any of them to do that and nobody in that lane told any of them to stop.

Not one figure of any kind stands against the counting or against the figures of any of the four on any page in that harbor.

---

A man came down that lane at about ten and was in it for the half hour it takes him and went away again before a quarter to one, and he did not speak to any of those four and none of those four spoke to him, and about four people in that lane would not be able to describe him that afternoon.

A man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place stood in that lane from about half past ten until about a quarter to one and watched four people count and said nothing at any hour of it, and he had been in that harbor seventy-five days by that Sunday and has asked nobody for anything on any of them but the one thing said on Saturday.

The man of about thirty-two was not in that lane at any hour of that Sunday and did not stand in front of that thing, and he was in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

---

At about three about four people went up to that room over that store with the keeper and stood round while he put seven figures up on that bare wall of it at one size in his own hand, being the seven figures of the seven times that thing came down in that run of fifty days, being one hundred and forty-three and one hundred and forty-six and one hundred and forty-nine and one hundred and fifty-two and one hundred and fifty-five and one hundred and fifty-eight and one hundred and sixty-one.

Those seven are not a claim and not a correction put up in public and not a refusal and not the fourth kind of thing that harbor may put up in a room, and they are not a fifth kind of thing on any wall, and they do not go on that wall in that room over that fish market at any hour, and nothing else goes onto that bare wall on any other day of the fifty.

About four of those four standing in that room read those seven and said out loud that the figures are on that wall and not in that drawer, and that this is the first thing that has gone on a wall in that room for one hundred and fifty-four days.

That wall had stood bare for one hundred and fifty-fourth days until that Sunday, and a child was told the practice and not the contents and was not told what the figures are.

---

The ordinary work of that Sunday was one boat off that mole before noon in that pale light and a hand-cart of coal left standing in the open, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for twenty-three days and nobody has touched it at any hour of that Sunday at all.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page until about two and did not come into that shed at any hour of that Sunday, and the man of about thirty-two did not tell her one word about anything.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fifty-ninth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

*First column, entered on Sunday the fifteenth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the forty-ninth entry of that run of fifty days: that the said day was a wipe day and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday; that that thing came off the face of itself at about half past ten and stood bare forty-five minutes and went back up at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, at fifty-eight minutes off and fifty-eight back, one hundred and sixty-one the whole of it, being the hundred and forty-first time; that both ends ran a minute longer and nobody said why and nobody asked; that about four people stood in front of it; that at about three seven figures went up on that bare wall of that room over that store at one size in a keeper's hand, being the seven of the seven times; that nothing else goes onto that wall on any other day of the fifty.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason seven figures went onto that bare wall is that a wall that has stood bare for one hundred and fifty-four days is a wall a stranger can find, and that seven figures of seven times are figures about a wall and not figures against a man; that the reason those seven are not a claim and not a correction and not a refusal and not the fourth kind is that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and figures about a wall are none of them; that the reason nothing else goes onto that wall is that one wall in one room is enough for what there is to give.*
