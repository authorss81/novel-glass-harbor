# Chapter 878

*Thursday the Fifteenth Day of December, the Eighteenth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Tenth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen and Not One Figure of Any Kind Standing Against That Man or Against Anything He Does, the Slate Not Out of the Coat at Any Hour of This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Eleven and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Twenty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Ten and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Twenty-Four*

Thursday the fifteenth day of December, with that market open from six and a sharp clear morning with that rime standing on every rail in that yard until the sun came over that roof at about half past nine, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighteenth day of December and the next charge is on Monday the nineteenth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twentieth day of December. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and tenth time and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninety-first days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this eighteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventy-sixth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventy-eighth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and sixteenth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and eighty-second night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundredth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fifty-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and seventieth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and eighteenth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and fourth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and ninth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-seventh day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-sixth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-three days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in eighteen days. Three hundred and seventy-sixth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That butt was filled for the two hundred and tenth time at ten past ten with two men on the handles, and it was walked on by none of it, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at four.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and did not once put it down in the middle, and not one figure of any kind stands in any page in this harbor against that man or against anything that he does.

He has never been paid a figure, a half figure or a penny of anything for ten trips on a Monday and ten trips on a Thursday, and he has never once said out loud that he would like anything at all for them.

---

About four of that nine said out loud that a cistern gets charged on a Monday and on a Thursday and has got charged on both of those days every week since the middle of August, and that a cistern does not know what day of the week it is and neither does a barrow.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that the rota says a Monday and a Thursday on it and that the rota is a piece of board, and about four of those nine said out loud that the piece of board is the only thing in that yard that keeps the two of them together.

---

The man of about thirty-two counted fourteen trips in his own head at about eleven, standing in the doorway with his hands in his pockets, and got to fourteen and stopped there, and he had not one single figure of any kind about the man who made ten of those fourteen.

That was what was new about that Thursday and about four of that nine did not know it was new and one of that nine did, and it was that a figure about a thing a man has done and a figure about the body of a man doing it are two columns of the same shape, and that he has one of those two columns and has never wanted the other one at all.

---

The ordinary work of that Thursday was two boats off that mole before noon and a hand-cart of that coke up from the store with the tarpaulin tied at four corners that time and not two, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

The slate was not out of that coat at any hour of that Thursday, and about four of that nine did not know that wood was in that room and one of that nine knew.
---

No tin has been opened in that shed since the twenty-ninth day of April past, and about four of that nine said out loud that a yard which has gone two hundred and ninety-one days without a tin has decided something about itself without ever putting it down anywhere.

---

That correction and that refusal have stood at that end stall at one size and a hand's breadth apart for one hundred and ninth days with the wrong figure still left showing underneath the correction and not struck out, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody in this harbor has said out loud which of those two is right and that neither of them has been taken down.

About four of that nine said out loud that the pair of those two standing side by side is the only argument this harbor has made in a year and that it is made of plaster and paint and nothing else.

---

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about one, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about eleven until about three, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about any of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twenty-eighth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a Thursday on which a man found out that he had one column of figures and had never wanted the other one, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had ninety-five lines on it.

THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO FOUND ON THIS DAY THAT HE HAS ONE COLUMN OF FIGURES AND HAS NEVER WANTED THE OTHER ONE. THE SLATE WAS NOT OUT OF THAT COAT AT ANY HOUR OF THIS DAY. *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason not one figure of any kind stands against that man is that no page in this harbor has ever had a place to put one, and that a thing with no place to be put is a thing nobody has ever thought of putting anywhere, and that this is the same absence this harbor found on that piece of wood and it is not the same absence; that the reason the man of about thirty-two counted fourteen trips in his own head is that a figure about a cistern is the easiest kind there is, being a thing that is either filled or not filled, and that his head had a free column standing there and used it; that the reason he had never wanted a figure about the body of the man doing it is that a body is not a thing anybody in this harbor has a figure for, and that this harbor has spent six weeks learning what happens when one is put there, and that he is the only man in that shed who has learned it and has said nothing out loud about it; that the reason nobody in that yard has ever wanted a figure against that man is that a figure against a man is a claim, and that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up and none of them has ever been a man, and that a man is not a fifth kind of thing and is not going to become one.
