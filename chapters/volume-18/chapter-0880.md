# Chapter 880

*Saturday the Seventeenth Day of December, the Twentieth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Saturday Carrying No Charge and No Wipe and Nothing on a Wage, at About One in About Four People the Man of About Thirty-Two Saying Out Loud That He Has Been Keeping the Count Himself and That Nobody Asked Him To and That It Is on Nothing, the Slate Not Out of That Coat and Not Put Down on Anything at Any Hour of This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirteen and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Thirty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twelve and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Thirty-Four*

Saturday the seventeenth day of December, with that market shut until seven on a Saturday and a hard white morning off that point that had that rime out on every rail in that yard by nine and a flat sea with no white on it at any hour, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Saturday is an ordinary working Saturday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighteenth day of December and the next charge is on Monday the nineteenth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twentieth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninety-third days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twentieth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventy-eighth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and eightieth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and eighteenth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and eighty-fourth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and second day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fifty-seventh day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and seventy-second day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twentieth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and sixth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and eleventh days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-ninth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-eighth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-five days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty days. Three hundred and seventy-eighth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about one he said out loud that he had been keeping the count of that thing behind that reeve's bench himself, in about four people, in that shed, with the door shut against the weather.

Then he said out loud that nobody had ever asked him to keep it, and about four of those four said nothing at all to that, and he went on standing exactly where he had been standing.

Then he said out loud that it was on nothing, and there is no page in this harbor and no board and no drawer in any room in this harbor that has one figure of it on it, and that has been so for as long as there have been figures.

---

He did not take the slate out of that coat at any hour of that Saturday and he did not put it down on anything and he did not give it to anybody, and about four of that nine did not know at about four where that wood was.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that it is a strange thing to keep a count of a thing that is not on a page, and he said out loud that it is, and that he was not going to argue about it.

About four of that nine said nothing whatever about the counting at all, and not one of them said anything to him about going on with it and not one about stopping.

---

He did not say who the man was, and he does not know, and he did not say when he began, and there is no day in this harbor that has it on it.

He said those three sentences and nothing else at any hour that Saturday, and about four of that four did not want a fourth one and about four of that nine have not thought about one since.

He stood where he had been standing for the half hour after that with his hands in the pockets of that coat, and what he had said was in no mouth but his own.

One of those four went to that door and put a hand on the latch and did not open it, and the weather came back on that quay at about half past four and four of that nine went out into it one after another.

---

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked at the far end of that yard over her own page from about ten until about two, and she was not in that shed at about one and was not within a wall's thickness of it at that hour either.

She came into that shed at about half past four for her own book and he was at the end of that yard and did not come in, and at about five he said nothing to her about any part of it.

He and the woman of thirty are still married and neither of them said one word to the other one about anything at all on that Saturday or on any of the twenty days before it.
---

That sheet with the fifteen gaps at that stall stands at fifteen and has stood at fifteen since the twenty-fifth day of September, and about four of that nine said out loud that fifteen is the one figure at that stall everybody agrees about and that nobody there can say how that came to be fifteen.

---

The records that keep that work in this harbor are cited and disputed and corrected every week, and they are inconvenient this month for one reason only, which is that they do not have one figure of that thing in them.

---

The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twentieth day of not speaking about six names, and about four of that nine said out loud that six names is a small number for a man to keep in his mouth that length of time and a large number for him to have stopped at.

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday was two boats off that mole before noon in flat water and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store with a rope over it and no tarpaulin, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor forty-six days by that Saturday and has now stood in that lane on six Sundays and said nothing on any of the six, and nobody has asked him for anything on any of his forty-six days.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirtieth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

THE SLATE WAS NOT OUT OF THAT COAT AND WAS NOT PUT DOWN ON ANYTHING AT ANY HOUR OF THIS DAY. NO FIGURE OF THAT THING BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH IS ON ANY PAGE IN THIS HARBOR. *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason three sentences is the whole of what was said out loud in that shed on that Saturday is that the fourth sentence would have had a person in it, and that this harbor has spent six weeks finding out what a person in a sentence costs, and that he stopped at three; that the reason nobody in that room said anything to him about going on with it or about stopping is that four people in a shed are not an office and cannot make anybody do a thing or leave off doing it, and that there is nobody in this harbor with the standing to tell anybody either, and that these twenty days have not created one; that the reason the woman of thirty was at the far end of that yard at about one is that she works over her own page and that page is at that end of that yard, and that neither of the two of them arranged anything at all about that hour; that the reason he put the wood back in that coat and did not hand it over is that a count handed over is a different thing from a count, and that he knows that, and that he is not going to argue about it either, and that the twenty-sixth day of this run is not the day for it.
