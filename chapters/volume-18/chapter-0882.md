# Chapter 882

*Monday the Nineteenth Day of December, the Twenty-Second Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Eleventh Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen and Not One Figure of Any Kind Standing Against That Man or Against Anything He Does, That Thing Behind That Reeve's Bench Not Out of That Coat Before About One and Not Out of It After Two, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Fifteen and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Forty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Fourteen and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Forty-Four*

Monday the nineteenth day of December, with that market open from six and a clear cold morning that came off that point and put a skin on every rail in that yard before nine and then gave about ten degrees of it back by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-second day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twentieth day of December. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and eleventh time and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninety-fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried across this twenty-second day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past has now been out five hundred and eightieth days and no reply has come down that road to anybody. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and eighty-second day of not being asked anything by anybody in this harbor. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody stand at the nine hundred and twentieth day of belonging to nobody. That bench beneath that pump was bare once more on its six hundred and eighty-sixth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and fourth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fifty-ninth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and seventy-fourth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its four hundred and ninety-first day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twenty-second day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and eighth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and thirteenth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under that correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eighty-first day, it has six lines on it, and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for eighty days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-seven days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-two days. Three hundred and eightieth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Fourteen trips went up that cistern at about ten past ten with two men on the handles and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody put a stick into that water at any hour.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and did not once put it down in the middle, and not one figure of any kind stands in any page in this harbor against that man or against anything that he does.

He has been paid nothing for those trips, not a figure and not a half figure and not a penny of anything, and he has never once said out loud that he would like anything at all for them, and that is not a thing anybody in that yard has thought about in twenty-one days.

---

At about one a man of about thirty-one wrote the hour the covers came off that cistern on the back of that rota board with a stub of chalk, and that is the first thing that has ever been written on the back of it.

About four of that nine said out loud that the rota is a piece of board and that the back of it had nothing on it until about one that afternoon, and one of about four of that nine said out loud that a figure written on the back of a piece of board is a figure nobody can check, because anybody may rub it out with a sleeve.

The keeper said out loud that a figure a man keeps in his own head has the one thing that board does not have, which is that nobody can rub it out, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

The man of about thirty-two had that wood out of the inside of his coat in his own two hands at about half past eleven, standing in the doorway of that shed with the light behind him, and he was not in that lane on that Monday at any hour and did not go up it.

What he did with it that Monday was to come at it from the other end, which is the end a man starts from when he has already tried the top of it and could not get there.

He wanted the first time he ever heard a number in that lane. He could not bring up a face, and he could not bring up a day, and what he could bring up after about ten minutes was that he wanted to be able to say one.

That wanting is the whole of what he can find at the bottom of it, and there is nobody in that lane, in that shed or in this harbor who ever asked him for a number in his life.

---

The ordinary work of that Monday was three boats off that mole before noon and a barrow of sand run up to the second lay and left standing in the yard in a heap, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about one and then took it inside, and he was in that shed from about half past eleven until about two, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a man who wants to be able to say a number is doing something ordinary and not something wrong, and that about four of those nine have wanted to be able to say a number about a thing they had no business having a number about.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirty-second day of that run of days he has not missed.

Ten hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a Monday on which a man went at a piece of wood from the other end and found nothing under it but the wish to have said a number, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had ninety-nine lines on it.

**THE TWO HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN AT ABOUT TEN PAST TEN AND TOOK FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT, AND THE OLD MAN OF ABOUT SIXTY-EIGHT WENT UP TEN OF THOSE FOURTEEN AND NO FIGURE OF ANY KIND STANDS AGAINST HIM. AT ABOUT ONE A MAN WROTE THE HOUR THE COVERS CAME OFF ON THE BACK OF THAT ROTA BOARD, WHICH HAD NOTHING ON IT UNTIL THAT AFTERNOON. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO WENT AT THAT PIECE OF WOOD FROM THE OTHER END THAT DAY AND FOUND THAT THE ONLY THING UNDER IT WAS THAT HE WANTED TO BE ABLE TO SAY A NUMBER. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-FIFTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Monday the nineteenth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the twenty-second entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-second day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twentieth day of December; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man who wants to be able to say a number finds nothing under it when he goes looking is that wanting is not a reason and does not leave one behind it, and that a harbor which keeps figures about work has no column at all for what a man wanted; that the reason nobody rubbed out the hour on the back of that rota board at about one is that nobody had ever looked at the back of it, and that a thing nobody has looked at for a hundred and thirty weeks gets one line written on it without anybody treating it as an event; that the reason about four of that nine said nothing back to the keeper is that a head is the only ledger in this harbor that cannot be checked and cannot be rubbed out, and that about four people have worked out in the last three weeks that neither of those two things is an advantage; that the reason he did not go up that lane on that Monday is that a man who cannot find the first of his own figures cannot usefully stand at the far end of the hundred and thirty-seventh, and that this is not the same as saying that he will not go up it again and is not going to be said out loud by anybody.*
