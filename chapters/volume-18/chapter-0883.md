# Chapter 883

*Tuesday the Twentieth Day of December, the Twenty-Third Day of a Run of Fifty Days, the Hundred and Twelfth Wage Tuesday, a Chest on That Step From About Ten Until About Four and Twenty Envelopes in It and None of Them Opened at Any Hour, Twenty-Six Lines at One Hundred and Four Marks Standing Unmoved and the Hundred and Thirteenth Week Fallen Due at the Close of It, the Man of About Thirty-One of the Lowfen Reed's Own Place on His Forty-Ninth Day in That Harbor and on His Seventh Sunday in That Lane With His Mouth Shut, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Sixteen and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Fifteen and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Tuesday the twentieth day of December, with that market open from six and a grey morning with the fog lying on the water off that mole head until about eleven and the roofs of that yard wet under it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is the hundred and twelfth wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a fitting-out line, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-second day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of December. That chest came out onto that step at about ten and stayed on it until about four with twenty envelopes in it and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninety-sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this twenty-third day and every one of them went on by one and none of them went back. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and eighty-first day and no answer has come back off it. That bellwright of this harbor goes into the five hundred and eighty-third day of going unasked. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody stand at the nine hundred and twenty-first day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and eighty-seventh night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and fifth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and sixtieth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and seventy-fifth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its four hundred and ninety-second day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twenty-third day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and ninth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and fourteenth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eighty-second day, it has six lines on it, and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for eighty-one days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-eight days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-three days. Three hundred and eighty-first days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That chest was carried out onto that step at about ten by two men and taken off it at about four by the same two men, and there were twenty envelopes in it the whole of that time, and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that Tuesday by anybody.

The whole of that column stands at one hundred and four marks, being sixteen thousand six hundred and forty pence, and one mark is one hundred and sixty pence and there is no half of anything in that figure.

At about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and neither of those two figures was read out to anybody, and the hundred and thirteenth week stood fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of it.

Nothing has ever been drawn off that column by anybody in this harbor and nobody has ever written a reason for not drawing it off, and that is the fourth week of a run that began before this one.

---

The man of about thirty-two had that wood out of the inside of his coat at about half past eleven for about two minutes and put it back before anybody came round that corner, and about four of that nine did not know he had had it in his hand and one of that nine did.

What he did with those two minutes was to hold that piece of wood next to what the keeper had just written on that sheet about a yard away, and the two of them are the same shape.

That sheet has a day at the head of every figure on it and every one of those weeks is a week somebody worked, and his wood has no day at the top of it and no morning that he can find at the bottom of it, and both of those columns are a run of figures that nobody has drawn one single mark off.

---

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a column with a week at the head of it is a column anybody can be wrong about, and that a piece of wood with a minute at the top of it and nothing under it is a column nobody can be wrong about at all because nobody else can read it.

The man of about thirty-two said out loud that he could stand in that shed and say what that wage column was for without stopping to think about it, and that he could not say what his own figures were for, and that he had tried twice that week to say it out loud to nobody and had got as far as that thing behind that reeve's bench both times.

About four of that nine said nothing whatever back to that, and the keeper wrote nothing down, and about four of those nine had heard both of those sentences and have not spoken about them since.

---

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was two boats off that mole before noon and a hand-cart of coal taken up to the second lay in that wet and brought back with the straps cut and not mended, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about two, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about half past eleven until about three, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirty-third day of that run of days he has not missed.

Nine hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and forty-seven words, and that chest came first in it and the two columns of the same shape came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench inside a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood in one man's own coat and it is not in that column and it is on no page.

Nobody was thanked for a Tuesday on which a man carried a piece of wood up to a column and found the two of them the same shape and could not say what either of them was for, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND TWELFTH WAGE TUESDAY WENT PAST WITH TWENTY ENVELOPES IN A LOCKED CHEST AND NO LINE OF TWENTY-SIX MOVED A MARK AND THE HUNDRED AND THIRTEENTH WEEK FELLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF IT. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO CARRIED THAT PIECE OF WOOD UP TO THAT SHEET AND FOUND THE TWO OF THEM THE SAME SHAPE AND SAID OUT LOUD THAT HE COULD NOT SAY WHAT HIS OWN FIGURES WERE FOR AND HAD GOT AS FAR AS THAT THING BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH TWICE THAT WEEK. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twentieth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the twenty-third entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was the hundred and twelfth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-second day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of December; that that chest stood on that step from about ten until about four with twenty envelopes in it and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody; that the wage column stands at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks, being sixteen thousand six hundred and forty pence, and has taken no line since September; that at about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and neither figure was read out to anybody; that the hundred and thirteenth week stood fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of it and did not move a mark; that nothing has ever been drawn off that column and no reason has ever been written on it for not drawing it off; that the man of about thirty-two had the wooden slate out of that coat at about half past eleven for about two minutes and put it back before anybody came round that corner of that shed, and that about four of that nine did not know and one of that nine did; that he carried that wood next to what the keeper had just written and found the two of them the same shape, in that a day stands at the head of every figure on that wage sheet and no day stands at the head of any figure on that wood; that one of about four of that nine said out loud that a column with a week at the head of it is a column anybody can be wrong about, and that a column nobody else can read is a column nobody can be wrong about; that the man of about thirty-two said out loud that he could say what that wage column was for without stopping to think about it and could not say what his own figures were for, and that he had got as far as that thing behind that reeve's bench twice that week in trying to say it to nobody; that about four of that nine said nothing back to that and the keeper wrote nothing down; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about two and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and thirty-third day of that run; that nine hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and the page of days carries nine against this day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason two columns of the same shape can be one readable and one not is that a column with a week at the head of it has been asked what it is for by somebody, and that a column with a minute at the head of it has been asked by nobody at all and has answered itself out of a man's own habit; that the reason he got as far as that thing behind that reeve's bench twice in one week is that there is no shorter word in this harbor for what that thing is called, and that a man who cannot finish a sentence about his own figures has usually found the edge of what he has been counting rather than the beginning of it; that the reason about four of those nine said nothing back to that is that a sentence about that thing behind that reeve's bench that has just been got as far to and not finished is a thing a room of nine people has no practice with, and that this harbor has spent six weeks not answering and is now living inside that; that the reason nobody has ever drawn a mark off that wage column is not a reason anybody could put in writing and is the same silence that has kept his wood in one coat for a hundred and thirty-seven mornings, and those two are not the same silence and this harbor has not been told so.*
