# Chapter 876

*Tuesday the Thirteenth Day of December, the Sixteenth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, the Hundred and Eleventh Wage Tuesday, a Chest on That Step From About Ten Until About Four and Twenty Envelopes in It and None of Them Opened, Twenty-Six Lines at One Hundred and Four Marks Standing Unmoved and the Hundred and Twelfth Week Fallen Due at the Close of It, the Man of About Thirty-Two Counting the Weeks on That Column in His Own Head at About Eleven Because Nobody in That Yard Was Going to Say the Figure Out Loud, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Nine and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Nineteen and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Eight and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Fourteen*

Tuesday the thirteenth day of December, with that market open from six and a low yellow morning with that frost still in the shadow side of everything and the sun not on that mole head until nearly ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is the hundred and eleventh wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighteenth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the fifteenth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighty-ninth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this sixteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventy-fourth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventy-sixth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and fourteenth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and eightieth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninety-eighth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fifty-third day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixty-eighth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-fifth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and sixteenth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and second day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and seven days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-fifth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-fourth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-one days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in sixteen days. Three hundred and seventy-fourth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about ten a locked chest came out onto that step and stayed there until four, and there were twenty envelopes in it, and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody.

Twenty-six lines went on that column in September and not one line has been added to it since, and the whole of it stands at one hundred and four marks, being sixteen thousand six hundred and forty pence, and one mark is one hundred and sixty pence and there is no half of anything in that figure.

At about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week in the column at the head of that sheet, and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and neither figure was read out to anybody.

The hundred and twelfth week stood fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of it, and it did not move a mark, and nothing has ever been drawn off that column by anybody here.

---

The man of about thirty-two counted those weeks in his own head at about eleven, going down the column the keeper had just written, and got to a hundred and twelve and stopped there, and did not say that figure out loud, because nobody in that yard was going to say it and about four of that nine had gone back to what they were doing.

That was the first figure his head had made on its own in four days, and he had not asked it to and could not have stopped it, and about four of that nine did not know that a man six feet off was doing that behind his own face.

---

One of about four of that nine said out loud that the two of them are the same shape, being a number written down on a thing that stands in a room and a number kept in a man's head about a thing that does not stand in any room, and about four of those nine said out loud that the first of those two is read out loud twice a month and the second has never been read out loud to anybody in this harbor.

The keeper said out loud that a column that is read out loud is a column anybody can be wrong about in public, and that is the whole of the difference, and nobody said anything back.
---

The rule in the inside of that door was made at a working place out loud in about nine people on the thirtieth day of September, and it has six lines on it, and it says on its own face that it was made under pressure and that it can be repaired, and about four of that nine said out loud that it has been in the inside of that door for seventy-five days and nobody has printed it and nobody has changed one word of it.

---

Over the middle of those four columns there has been a boy's hand of twenty-two since September and nothing under it, and his surname is on no page, and about four of that nine said out loud that a column with a hand at the top and nothing under it is the only thing in that yard that gets emptier the longer it stands.

---

Those twenty-six lines went on that column in September for work done by twenty-six men, and not one of them has had a penny of it, and not one of them has said out loud he is expecting it, and nobody has ever drawn a mark off that column and nobody has ever written a reason on it.

---

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was two boats off that mole before noon and a hand-cart of copper template taken up to the second lay and brought back with the straps cut, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

That market was busy at about one and quiet at about three, and a man of about fifty-seven came through that shed at about two with a copying book and was not asked for anything in it.

---

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about one, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed at about half past eleven and at about eleven as well, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about the hundred and twelfth week or anything else at all.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twenty-sixth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Eight hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and not one of those eight was the hand that wrote what either sheet went against, and the page of days carries eight against this day.

---

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a man counts a column nobody is going to read is that the column is in the room and a figure he can reach, and that a head will go on doing that arithmetic whether anybody has asked it to or not, and that this is the first week of his life in which a figure about a thing that is not in the room has come out by itself; that the reason about four of that nine said out loud that the two of them are the same shape is that a shape is the only thing in this harbor that anybody can compare without owning anything, and that six weeks of figures has taught them to look at shapes; that the reason the keeper said out loud that a column read out loud is a column anybody can be wrong about in public is that being wrong in public is the only protection this yard has found, and that a figure nobody hears cannot be checked and cannot be disputed and therefore cannot be corrected, and that is a thing a room of nine people can say out loud about a thing none of them owns; that the reason about four of that nine did not know that a man standing six feet off was counting in his own head is that a head is the one place in this harbor with no keeper in it, and that this harbor has found that out this month and has not yet found out what to do with it.
