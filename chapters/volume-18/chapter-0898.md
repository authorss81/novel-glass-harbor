# Chapter 898

*Wednesday the Fourth Day of January, the Thirty-Eighth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Wednesday Carrying No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, Two of Those Four Up That Lane Coming into That Shed at About Eleven in the Middle of an Ordinary Morning and Nobody Asking Either of Them Anything, That Piece of Wood Twelve Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirty-One and Three Thousand and Twenty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirty and the Second at Three Thousand and Twenty-Four*

Wednesday the fourth day of January, with that market open from six and a fine bright cold morning with the frost gone out of that yard by nine and the wind round at that quay all day, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday is an ordinary working Wednesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the fifth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Wednesday and nothing is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Wednesday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and eleventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried across this thirty-eighth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and ninety-sixth day. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninety-eighth day of not being asked a question. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirty-sixth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its seven hundred and second night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and twentieth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are at the four hundred and seventy-fifth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and ninetieth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-eighth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and twenty-fourth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and twenty-ninth days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-seventh day and it has six lines on it and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety-six days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-third days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-eight days. Three hundred and ninety-sixth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about eleven two of those four came into that shed in the middle of an ordinary morning, being the man of about thirty-three and the man of about fifty, and neither of them was asked anything by anybody in that yard at any hour of that Wednesday.

The man of about forty-one was not in that shed at any hour of that Wednesday and the man of about twenty-six was not in it either, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that yard has any way of finding out where any of those four are on a Wednesday.

Nobody in that shed asked either of those two men one word about a Sunday morning, and neither of those two men said out loud one word about a Sunday morning, and about four of that nine said out loud that this is the ordinary way of a thing that has never been written down and is not going to be.

---

About four of that nine said out loud that there is no page in this harbor that says one word to any of those four about any of it, and that there is no board and there is no drawer in any room in this harbor that carries one figure of any of it.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a thing four people do on a Sunday morning with nobody standing over it is not a practice and is not a rule and has no name in this harbor, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

About four of that nine said out loud that the man who answered the question on Monday has not been in that shed since about two that afternoon and that nobody has sent for him and that nobody is going to.

---

The man of about thirty-two spent about an hour of that Wednesday at the far end of that yard with a coil of new line in his own two hands, untwisting the twist out of it so that it would lie flat in a coil the next time it was wanted, and about four of that nine said out loud that a man untwisting line for an hour on a Wednesday is a man who does not know what else to do with an hour, and nobody said anything back.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for twelve days and nobody has touched it, and about four of that nine walked past it that Wednesday and about four of that nine did not look down at it at all.

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about eleven until about half past four and said almost nothing at any hour of that Wednesday.

Those four columns take one entry a day and five a day and no entry at all, and the fourth of them has taken no line in thirty-eight days, and about four of that nine said out loud that a column nobody has closed is a column anybody may still put a line in, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

That leaf from the Cinder Bell came into that market on the Monday boat and has been on its hundred and twenty-fourth day, and about four of that nine said out loud that about four people have read it and about four of that nine have not.

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty-four days by that Wednesday and stood at the back of that shed from about one until about three and said nothing and asked nobody for anything.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about half past two, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was three boats off that mole before noon on a flat sea and a barrow of sand run up to the second lay and left standing in that yard in a heap, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-eighth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a Wednesday on which two of four people nobody has the standing to stop came into a working shed in the middle of the morning and were asked nothing at all, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and fifteen lines on it.

THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO SAID ALMOST NOTHING. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS THREE HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the fourth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirty-eighth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Wednesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the fifth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January; that nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that day and that nothing is owed to it and that the keeper did not read that foot at any hour of that Wednesday; that at about eleven the man of about thirty-three and the man of about fifty came into that shed and that neither of them was asked anything by anybody in that yard at any hour of that day; that the man of about forty-one and the man of about twenty-six were not in that shed at any hour of that day; that nobody in that shed asked either of those two men one word about a Sunday morning and that neither of them said out loud one word about a Sunday morning; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason two of those four walked into a working shed in the middle of an ordinary morning is that a man who has stood in a lane for two Sundays has nowhere to stand on a Wednesday and no other hour of that day is his own; that the reason nobody asked either of them anything is that the standing to ask anybody in this harbor was taken off it on the fourteenth of August and has not come back since, and that a man may therefore come into a shed and be asked nothing and be told nothing and that this is not a courtesy and is not unfriendliness and is not anybody's decision; that the reason nobody has sent for the man who answered the question on Monday is that a man who says a thing out loud in about nine people in a working place has not done anything that anybody can charge him for, and that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and none of those four was done at about one on Monday; that the reason about four of that nine said out loud that there is no page that says one word to any of those four is that about four people worked that out over six days and have not found a way of saying it to anybody else, and that a page is the only thing this harbor has ever used to keep a thing from being decided by whoever gets to it first.*
