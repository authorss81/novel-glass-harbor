# Chapter 868

*Monday the Fifth Day of December, the Eighth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Seventh Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand, Line Two Settled at About Four at One Thousand and Sixty-Eight Pence and the Whole of That Foot Read After That Settlement and Not Before It, the Foot Standing at One Hundred and Thirty-Five Thousand and Fifty Pence Out and One Hundred and Twenty-Four Thousand Four Hundred and Ten Pence Short, One Question About Whether That Two Hundred and Seventh Charge Is Written Down Anywhere, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and One and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Monday the fifth day of December, with that market open from six and that grey holding all day with no sun in it at all and the water off that mole head going out in a long even set, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eleventh day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on tomorrow, being the sixth day of December, and the hundred and eleventh week will stand fallen due at the close of that one. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and seventh time and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet before this afternoon and one line settled on it at about four, that foot standing after this afternoon's settlement at one hundred and thirty-five thousand and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and forty-four marks and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-four thousand four hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and seventy-seven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighty-first days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this eighth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and sixty-sixth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixty-eighth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and sixth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventy-second night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninetieth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and forty-fifth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixtieth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventy-seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and eighth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its ninety-fourth day. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety-nine days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixty-seventh day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its sixty-sixth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and thirteen days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in eight days. Three hundred and sixty-sixth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The old man of about sixty-eight did ten of those fourteen trips again with the barrow, and the fourteenth went up at ten past ten, and its covers were on it at about eleven and off it at about four, and nobody walked on the water after it at any hour at all.

The old man of about sixty-eight put his barrow down against that door and nobody in that shed asked him a thing.

A little before two in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine asked whether that two hundred and seventh charge is written down anywhere.

The keeper said out loud that it is on a rota, and that a rota is a rota and not a page, and that a rota is a piece of board with two days on it and fourteen trips beside each of them and nothing else on it at all.

About four of that nine said out loud that they had not known that, and did not say what they had thought a rota was for, and one of those four said out loud that he had thought a rota was a thing somebody came round and looked at.

---

The ordinary work of that Monday was two boats off that mole before noon and a stack of that copper template put under cover at the back of that shed, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and there is nothing on any page in this harbor to set against it.

---

That second line settled at about four at one thousand and sixty-eight pence, being six marks and nine shillings, and the keeper wrote the settlement against that line and against no other line.

The nine counters went up one each at the close of that Monday and every one of them was the figure of the Sunday with one added.

At about a quarter past four he read the whole of the foot of that fitting-out sheet out loud in about nine people, after that settlement and not before it.

He read out that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-five thousand and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and forty-four marks and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-four thousand four hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and seventy-seven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in.

About four of that nine had that figure before he said it and about four did not, and nobody wrote it down out loud and nobody repeated it after him.

The keeper said out loud that a column gets added up once and in writing and a foot gets read once and out loud, and that the two of those are different, and that this shed has been mixing them up since September.

---

About four of that nine said out loud, in the ordinary way and to nobody in particular, that a foot read after a settlement and a foot read before a settlement are two different numbers, and that nobody in that lane has ever known which one the figures on that wall were counted against.

A man of about thirty-one of that market said out loud that he did not know which of those two the figures on that wall were, and nobody answered him, and he did not ask again, and that was a statement and not a question and is not counted.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and eighteenth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets and a copy of a fourth that left that harbor that day, and seven of those ten were on two sheets that left this harbor before October, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

Nobody was thanked for a rota being explained out loud in a shed on a Monday, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had eighty-five lines on it.
