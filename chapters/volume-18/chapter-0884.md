# Chapter 884

*Wednesday the Twenty-First Day of December, the Twenty-Fourth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, Line Five onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eight With the Twenty-Third Day of December Written on the Face of It for the Day It Settles on a Friday, Six Men Out at About Eight and In Wet at About Half Past Two, Three Days of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Coal and Oil and a New Mole-Head Lamp Glass and Ninety Yards of Line and Six Men at Three Days, the Whole of That Line One Thousand Four Hundred and Forty-Two Pence, Being Nine Marks and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Seventeen and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Sixteen and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Fifty-Four*

Wednesday the twenty-first day of December, with that market open from six and a low wet morning that turned to rain about half past two and went on with it until that yard emptied, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday is an ordinary working Wednesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it puts one line onto that fitting-out sheet and settles nothing on it, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-second day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of December. That fifth line went onto that sheet at about eight with the twenty-third day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is a Friday. That foot is not read at any hour of that Wednesday and is not read out loud before that Friday afternoon at any hour. That foot stands this morning at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninety-seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this twenty-fourth day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and eighty-second day and no answer has come back off it. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and eighty-fourth day of not being asked a question by anybody in this harbor. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody stand at the nine hundred and twenty-second day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and eighty-eighth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and sixth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and sixty-first day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and seventy-sixth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its four hundred and ninety-third day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twenty-fourth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and tenth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and fifteenth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eighty-third day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for eighty-two days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-nine days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-four days. Three hundred and eighty-second days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Six men went out on that fifth line at about eight and came in wet at about half past two, and six is the whole of the labour on it and there is not a seventh man in it.

Six men at three days has not been worked on a line in this run before, and about four of that nine noticed the figure and about four of that nine had not.

That fifth line went onto that sheet at about eight with the twenty-third day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is a Friday, and not one penny of it comes into the foot of that sheet before that Friday afternoon.

Its five parts are three days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at three times one hundred and sixty-eight for five hundred and four pence, being three marks and two shillings; coal and oil for three days at sixty pence, being five shillings; a new mole-head lamp glass at two marks, being three hundred and twenty pence; ninety yards of line at three pence the yard at ninety times three for two hundred and seventy pence, being one mark, nine shillings and twopence; and six men at three days at sixteen pence a man-day at six times three times sixteen for two hundred and eighty-eight pence, being one mark, ten shillings and eightpence.

The whole of that line is one thousand four hundred and forty-two pence, being nine marks and twopence, and the keeper read the total back to nobody.

---

The keeper wrote the twenty-third day of December on the face of that line at about a quarter to eight, before a man had picked up a barrow that morning, and no line before the fifth has ever had its day written on it in that order.

He carried that mole-head lamp glass up from the store himself at about half past seven and put it down against that door with the other two, and about four of that nine did not know he had carried anything onto that sheet in his life and one of that nine did.

At about four the keeper put a pencil into his hand without saying a word about it and went back to what he was doing, and about four of that nine saw that happen and about four of that nine did not, and the pencil is still in that coat.

---

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a line has two days on the face of it, being the day the work was done and the day it settles, and that a line with two days on it can be asked about by anybody in this harbor at any hour from now on.

About four of that nine said out loud that nobody in this harbor has written any of those six men down in a column with their names on it.

He said out loud that a piece of wood with a hundred and thirty-seven mornings on it has got neither of those two days on it and cannot be asked about by anybody including him, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that and one of that nine went on with what she was doing.

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was three boats off that mole before noon in that rain and a barrow of ninety yards of that line run out to the second lay and brought back by nine, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about one, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed with her for about twenty minutes of that morning, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

A man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place came through that yard at about ten and went up that lane and came back down it at about eleven and had been in that harbor fifty days and had asked nobody for anything on any of the fifty.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirty-fourth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Thirteen hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries thirteen against this day.

---

About half past five on that Wednesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and fifty-three words, and that fifth line came first in it and the two days on the face of it came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench inside a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood in one man's own coat and it is not in that column and it is on no page.

THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the keeper wrote the day it settles on the face of a line before the work is done is that a line used to have the day written on it afterwards and a line written on afterwards is a line that cannot be looked up until it is finished, and that a man who has been counting mornings for a hundred and thirty-seven weeks would find a yard that writes the day first before he found one that writes it last; that the reason a pencil was put into that man's hand at about four with nothing said about it is that a keeper who has had his figures corrected by about four people for six weeks has begun handing out the tools of the trade to whoever is standing there, and that this is the first time in this run that anything in that yard has been given to anybody; that the reason nobody said anything back to the sentence about a piece of wood that cannot be asked about is that it is true, and that a room of nine people has twice now agreed with a true sentence and not gone on to say what ought to be done about it; that the reason a man of about thirty-one went up that lane at about ten and came back down it at about eleven with nothing said is that he has now done it on seven Sundays and once on a Wednesday, and that about four of that nine have stopped putting those two kinds of day together in their heads and have not admitted to doing it.*
