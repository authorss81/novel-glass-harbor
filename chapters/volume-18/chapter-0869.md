# Chapter 869

*Tuesday the Sixth Day of December, the Ninth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, the Hundred and Tenth Wage Tuesday, the Chest on That Step From About Ten Until About Four and Twenty Envelopes in It and None of Them Opened, Twenty-Six Lines at One Hundred and Four Marks Standing Unmoved and the Hundred and Eleventh Week Fallen Due at the Close of It, One Question About How Many Wage Tuesdays There Have Been Since the Wages Stopped and a Column Added Up Once in Writing at About One and Not Said Out Loud, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Two and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Eighty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and One and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-Nine*

Tuesday the sixth day of December, with that market open from six and a brighter day than the Monday before it with a wind up the quay from the south-west and spray coming over that mole head in the afternoon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is the hundred and tenth wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eleventh day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the eighth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-five thousand and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and forty-four marks and tenpence, and one hundred and twenty-four thousand four hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and seventy-seven marks, seven shillings and sixpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighty-second days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this ninth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and sixty-seventh day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixty-ninth day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and seventh day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventy-third night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninety-first day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and forty-sixth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixty-first day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventy-eighth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and ninth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its ninety-fifth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixty-eighth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its sixty-seventh day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fourteen days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in nine days. Three hundred and sixty-seventh days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Twenty envelopes were in that chest from about ten until about four and not one of them was opened, and the wage column did not move a mark, and the hundred and eleventh week stands fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of that Tuesday.

At about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week in the column at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and neither figure was read out to anybody.

One mark is one hundred and sixty pence and the whole of that column is sixteen thousand six hundred and forty pence of work done in September by twenty-six men, and not one of those twenty-six men has had a penny of it, and not one of the twenty-six has said out loud he is expecting it. Nobody has ever drawn a mark off that column and nobody has ever written a reason on it.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon with that spray coming over the top of the stone, and a hand-cart of coke drawn up from the store with a tarpaulin over it, and not one of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and there is nothing anywhere that anybody can set against it.

---

That market was shut for an hour in the middle of that Tuesday with that wind round the corner of it, and a man of about thirty-eight came through that shed at about two with a hand-cart and was not asked anything.

The page of that week went out of that harbor that day with ten lines on it, and about four of those ten were about a wall nobody had asked about, and the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place carried his two pails down that lane at about ten and up it at about eleven for the ninth day and said nothing.

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine asked the keeper how many wage Tuesdays there have been since the wages stopped.

The keeper said out loud that he is going to add it up once, in writing, and that he is not going to say the number out loud to anybody this month, and that he is not going to read it at that stone standing up.

About four of that nine said out loud that they would rather he did not add it up at all than add it up and keep it, and he said out loud that he is not going to be told what to do with a column by a room, and nobody in that room said anything back to that.

---

At about one as well, a man of about thirty-one of that market said out loud that he did not know who counted the figures on that wall, and that he had never once asked anybody about it.

About four of that nine said out loud that it was nobody's business, and one of that nine said out loud that it was nobody's job, and the man who said that said it about himself and about three other people, and nobody asked him which of the three other people he meant.

The keeper said out loud that a thing nobody has asked about is a thing nobody is going to be told about this month, and about four of that nine said out loud that that was the first sensible thing anybody had said about that wall in five weeks.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and nineteenth day of that run of days he has not missed, and nobody in that lane has ever once asked him anything, and that is the whole of what this run of fifty days has to say about him.

The man of about thirty-two came into that shed at about three and stayed until about five and said nothing about any of it, and about four of that nine went on talking for about ten minutes after he had come in, and he listened to all of it.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the writer of what each of those three says was on none of those nine hands, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a hundred and tenth Tuesday on which one question was put and answered with a refusal to say a number, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had eighty-six lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND TENTH WAGE TUESDAY WENT PAST WITH TWENTY ENVELOPES IN A LOCKED CHEST AND NO LINE OF TWENTY-SIX MOVED A MARK AND THE HUNDRED AND ELEVENTH WEEK FELLEN DUE AT THE CLOSE OF IT. ONE QUESTION WENT INTO THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED ON THIS DAY, BEING HOW MANY WAGE TUESDAYS THERE HAVE BEEN SINCE THE WAGES STOPPED, AND IT WAS ANSWERED WITH A FIGURE THAT WAS ADDED UP ONCE IN WRITING AND WAS NOT SAID OUT LOUD. THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED WAS NOT ADDED UP AND ITS TEN DAYS DO NOT STAND YET. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SECOND DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Tuesday the sixth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the ninth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was the hundred and tenth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eleventh day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the eighth day of December; that twenty envelopes were in that chest from about ten until about four and not one of them was opened, and that the wage column did not move a mark; that at about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week in the column at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and neither figure was read out to anybody; that at about one in that shed in about nine people about four of that nine asked the keeper how many wage Tuesdays there have been since the wages stopped; that the keeper said out loud that he is going to add it up once, in writing, and that he is not going to say the number out loud to anybody this month and is not going to read it at that stone standing up; that about four of that nine said out loud that they would rather he did not add it up at all than add it up and keep it, and that nobody in that room said anything back to that; that at about one as well a man of about thirty-one of that market said out loud that he did not know who counted the figures on that wall and that he had never once asked anybody about it, and about four of that nine said out loud that it was nobody's business and one of that nine said out loud that it was nobody's job, and that was a statement and not a question; that the keeper said out loud that a thing nobody has asked about is a thing nobody is going to be told about this month; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and nineteenth day of that run, and that nobody in that lane has ever once asked him anything; that the man of about thirty-two came into that shed at about three and stayed until about five and said nothing about any of it.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the keeper would add a number up once in writing and would not say it out loud is that a wage Tuesday is the one day in this harbor where the same words have been said in the same order for a hundred and ten weeks, and that a number said out loud at the end of that would be read as a verdict on somebody rather than as a count of Tuesdays; that the reason nobody asked who counted the figures on that wall is that the answer has been the same for every Sunday this harbor can remember, which is that nobody appointed them, and that a question with an answer nobody can change is a question this harbor has learned not to ask; that the reason one of that nine said out loud that it was nobody's job, and said it about himself and about three other people, is that a job in this harbor is a thing a person is given by somebody else, and that a thing four people go and do on a Sunday without being given it has no name and therefore no owner and therefore nobody's job, and that is the whole of it and it is not said to anybody; that the reason the man of about thirty-two came into that shed at about three and listened for two hours and said nothing is that a thing which has been said out loud in a room in his hearing about four people he knows by name is a thing a man may be asked about afterwards, and that he had been in that lane that morning and had seen that wall bare, and that he did not want to be asked about either.
