# Chapter 905

*Wednesday the Eleventh Day of January, the Forty-Fifth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Wednesday With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, That Piece of Wood Nineteen Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirty-Eight and Three Thousand and Sixty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirty-Seven and the Second at Three Thousand and Fifty-Nine*

Wednesday the eleventh day of January, with that market open from six and a black cloud standing beyond that point before six with a cold light under it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday is an ordinary working Wednesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside that run is on Sunday the fifteenth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twelfth day of January and there is no wage Tuesday after it inside that run. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Wednesday and nothing is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Wednesday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and eighteenth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine tallies advanced through that forty-fifth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is borne at its six hundred and third day. That bellwright is held into the six hundred and fifth day of not being asked anything by anybody. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are borne at the nine hundred and forty-third day. That bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its seven hundred and ninth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are seen at their five hundred and twenty-seventh day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are found at the four hundred and eighty-second day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is kept at its four hundred and ninety-seventh day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is held at its five hundred and fourteenth day. The man of about fifty-one is heard at the five hundred and forty-fifth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell abides on its hundred and thirty-first day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and thirty-sixth days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door continues on its hundred and fourth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in that harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, has been on that community's own page for a hundred and three days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fiftieth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-five days. Four hundred and third days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cloud stood black beyond that point before six and did not move all morning, and about four of that nine said out loud that a cloud that does not move before noon will be there when the boats come in.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Wednesday, and about four of that nine said out loud that a Wednesday with nothing on it is still a Wednesday somebody has to work through.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for nineteen days and nobody has touched it at any hour of that Wednesday, and about four of that nine said out loud that nineteen days is the longest any loose thing has lain in that shed without being moved to the side.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the two of them are still married, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it, and about four of that nine noticed that and about four did not.

---

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor seventy-one days by that Wednesday and stood at the back of that shed for about three hours of it and said nothing and asked nobody for anything.

About four of that nine said out loud at about three that seventy-one days without asking for one thing is a record nobody in that yard keeps but everybody remembers, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell has been on that board for its hundred and thirty-first day, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that yard will take it down to see what is under it.

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was three boats off that mole before noon under that cloud and a barrow of broken stone run out to the end of that quay, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fifty-fifth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Thirteen hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries thirteen against this day.

---

At about two the keeper mended a handle on that barrow with a strip of hide and a nail, and about four of that nine watched him do that and said out loud that a mended handle is better than a new one that does not fit.

About four of that nine said out loud that a Wednesday with no charge and no wipe leaves room for mending, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

That bell in that shed rang at about four and nobody in that shed stopped work for it, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bell at four means the covers go on and nothing more.

---

At about eleven two men carried a new lamp glass up to that mole head in a box of straw, and about four of that nine said out loud that a glass carried in straw is a glass that will get there whole.

About four of that nine said out loud at about noon that a cloud that stands all morning makes the light go early in that shed, and the keeper lit the lamp in that shed at about three and nobody said anything back to that.

Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head that Wednesday and answered one another at two hours of it, and the sound did not run together into one note and nobody on that quay tried to make it.

---

About half past five on that Wednesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and fifty words, and that cloud came first in it and the bench came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench within a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood and there is no figure of it on any page in that harbor.

"**That cloud stood black beyond that point before six and did not move all morning, and about four of that nine said out loud that a cloud that does not move before noon will be there when the boats come in. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Wednesday, and about four of that nine said out loud that a Wednesday with nothing on it is still a Wednesday somebody has to work through in that shed. That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for nineteen days and nobody has touched it at any hour of that Wednesday, and about four of that nine said out loud that nineteen days is the longest any loose thing has lain in that shed without being moved to the side by anybody. The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the two of them are still married, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it in that yard. The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor seventy-one days by that Wednesday and stood at the back of that shed for about three hours of it and said nothing and asked nobody for anything in that shed. About four of that nine said out loud at about three that seventy-one days without asking for one thing is a record nobody in that yard keeps but everybody remembers in that harbor. The leaf from the Cinder Bell has been on that board for its hundred and thirty-first day, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that yard will take it down to see what is under it at any hour. The ordinary work of that Wednesday was three boats off that mole before noon under that cloud and a barrow of broken stone run out to the end of that quay in that wind. From about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and fifty-fifth day of that run. Thirteen hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day in that shed. At about two the keeper mended a handle on that barrow with a strip of hide and a nail, and about four of that nine watched him do that and said out loud that a mended handle is better than a new one that does not fit in that yard. About four of that nine said out loud that a Wednesday with no charge and no wipe leaves room for mending in that shed. That bell in that shed rang at about four and nobody in that shed stopped work for it, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bell at four means the covers go on and nothing more in that yard. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Three hundred and eighteenth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-five days and is neither shut nor struck. One hundred and thirty-sixth days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and thirty-first day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and that wall of the room over the chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fiftieth days. It is four hundred and third days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, he has not been on the water, and no figure at all is set against any part of it. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. At about eleven two men carried a new lamp glass up to that mole head in a box of straw, and about four of that nine said out loud that a glass carried in straw is a glass that will get there whole in that wind. About four of that nine said out loud at about noon that a cloud that stands all morning makes the light go early in that shed, and the keeper lit the lamp in that shed at about three and nobody said anything back to that at any hour. Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head that Wednesday and answered one another at two hours of it, and the sound did not run together into one note and nobody on that quay tried to make it one. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head at first light yet still now.**"

Nobody was thanked for a Wednesday on which three boats went off that mole under a black cloud and a barrow handle was mended and nobody asked anything of anybody, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and twenty-two lines on it.

*First column, entered on Wednesday the eleventh day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the forty-fifth entry of that run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Wednesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday; that nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet and it was not read; that that piece of wood has been face up for nineteen days and nobody touched it; that thirteen hands went onto three sheets; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for one thing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nineteen days on a bench has not moved anybody to move it is that a thing nobody owns is a thing nobody in that yard has a place for, and that the bench it lies on is sat at four hours a day; that the reason nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for one thing is that a hundred and fifty-five days of sitting from about ten until about two is a sitting nobody questions; that the reason a barrow handle was mended is that a Wednesday with no charge leaves hands free for mending.*
