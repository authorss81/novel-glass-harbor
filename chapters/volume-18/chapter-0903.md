# Chapter 903

*Monday the Ninth Day of January, the Forty-Third Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Seventeenth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen, an Ordinary Working Monday With No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, That Piece of Wood Seventeen Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirty-Six and Three Thousand and Fifty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirty-Five and the Second at Three Thousand and Forty-Nine*

Monday the ninth day of January, with that market open from six and a cold wind coming off that point before six with a grey sky over that quay, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside that run is on Sunday the fifteenth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twelfth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and seventeenth time and it was walked on by none of it. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, is not read at any hour of that Monday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and sixteenth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counts moved through that forty-third day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is entered at its six hundred and first day. That bellwright is carried into the six hundred and third day of not being asked anything by anybody. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are kept at the nine hundred and forty-first day. That bench beneath that pump was found bare again on its seven hundred and seventh night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are held at their five hundred and twenty-fifth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are put at the four hundred and eightieth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is set at its four hundred and ninety-fifth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is laid at its five hundred and twelfth day. The man of about fifty-one is brought to the five hundred and forty-third day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is hung on its hundred and twenty-ninth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and thirty-fourth days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is kept on its hundred and second day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in that harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, has been on that community's own page for a hundred and one days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-eighth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-three days. Four hundred and first days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wind came off that point before six and rattled the doors of that shed, and about four of that nine said out loud that a wind off that point makes a cold morning for men on the handles of a barrow.

---

That cistern was filled for the two hundred and seventeenth time at about ten past ten with two men on the handles, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody walked on one single trip of it.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and did not once put it down in the middle, and not one figure of any kind stands in any page in that harbor against that man or against anything that he does.

About four of that nine said out loud at about eleven that fourteen trips by hand is a morning's work for two men and no more, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Monday, and about four of that nine said out loud that a rota morning is not a morning for talking.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for seventeen days and nobody has touched it at any hour of that Monday, and about four of that nine said out loud that seventeen days on a bench is longer than any job has ever waited.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the two of them are still married, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

---

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty-nine days by that Monday and stood at the back of that shed for about two hours of it and said nothing and asked nobody for anything.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell has been on that board for its hundred and twenty-ninth day, and about four of that nine said out loud that a leaf nobody can turn over is still a leaf that marks the days.

The ordinary work of that Monday was three boats off that mole before noon in that wind and a barrow of ash taken out to the far end of that quay, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fifty-third day of that run of days he has not missed.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

At about one a second hour went onto the back of that rota board under the first one with nothing between them, and about four of that nine said out loud that a rota board carries hours the way a shed carries dust, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

The keeper wiped his hands at about four and shut the covers of that cistern down tight, and about four of that nine watched him do that and wrote nothing down about it.

---

That bell in the room over that fish market rang at about noon and nobody in that yard went up to it, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bell nobody goes up to is still a bell that keeps the hour for men who work below it.

About four of that nine said out loud at about two that a Monday after a frost is a Monday for mending ropes in the open, and about four of that nine mended three fathoms of line on that bench and wrote nothing down about it.

---

About half past five on that Monday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and ten words, and that cistern came first in it and the bench came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench within a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood and there is no figure of it on any page in that harbor.

That cistern was filled for the two hundred and seventeenth time at about ten past ten with two men on the handles, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody walked on one single trip of it in that yard. The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and did not once put it down in the middle, and not one figure of any kind stands in any page in that harbor against that man or against anything that he does. About four of that nine said out loud at about eleven that fourteen trips by hand is a morning's work for two men in that yard. From about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and fifty-third day of that run. Twelve hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day in that shed. At about one a second hour went onto the back of that rota board under the first one with nothing between them. The keeper wiped his hands at about four and shut the covers of that cistern down tight, and about four of that nine watched him do that and wrote nothing down. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Three hundred and sixteenth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-three days and is neither shut nor struck. One hundred and thirty-fourth days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and twenty-ninth day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and that wall of the room over the chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-eighth days. It is four hundred and first days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, he has not been on the water, and no figure at all is set against any part of it. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. That bell in the room over that fish market rang at about noon and nobody in that yard went up to it, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bell nobody goes up to is still a bell that keeps the hour for men below it. About four of that nine said out loud at about two that a Monday after a frost is a Monday for mending ropes in the open, and about four of that nine mended three fathoms of line on that bench and wrote nothing down. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head at first light still.**"

Nobody was thanked for a Monday on which that cistern was filled for the two hundred and seventeenth time and an old man went up ten of fourteen trips and nobody wrote a figure against him, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and twenty lines on it.

*First column, entered on Monday the ninth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the forty-third entry of that run of fifty days: that the said day was a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside that run is on Sunday the fifteenth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twelfth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January; that that cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and seventeenth time and it was walked on by none of it, and that the old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen; that that piece of wood has been face up on that bench for seventeen days and nobody touched it; that twelve hands went onto two sheets; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for one thing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen is that ten trips is what his back will do before about eleven without stopping, and that a figure about that man's back would be a figure about the man himself; that the reason nobody wrote any figure against him is that a figure against a man would be a claim, and that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and none of them has ever been a man; that the reason a second hour went onto that rota board is that a rota board keeps hours and not reasons.*
