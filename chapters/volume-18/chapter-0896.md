# Chapter 896

*Monday the Second Day of January, the Thirty-Sixth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Fifteenth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen, at About One in About Nine People the Man of About Twenty-Seven of That Second Lay Asking in the Ordinary Way and a Man of About Forty-One Answering Him in Four Sentences, Four Sentences Which Are on No Page in This Harbor and in No Column and in No Slip, About Four of That Nine Saying Out Loud That Those Four Cannot Be Told to Stop, the Man of About Thirty-Two in That Shed Saying Nothing at Any Hour of It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-Nine and Three Thousand and Nineteen and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-Eight and the Second at Three Thousand and Fourteen*

Monday the second day of January, with that market open from six and a wet morning off that point that came on in the dark and had not finished by about one, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the fifth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and fifteenth time and it was walked on by none of it. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, is not read at any hour of that Monday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and ninth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this thirty-sixth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at its five hundred and ninety-fourth day. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninety-sixth day of not being asked a question. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirty-fourth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its seven hundredth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and eighteenth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are at the four hundred and seventy-third day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-eighth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and fifth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-sixth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and twenty-second day. That correction and that refusal have stood at that end stall one hundred and twenty-seventh days at one size a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure still left showing underneath and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-fifth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety-four days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-first days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-six days. Three hundred and ninety-fourth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern was filled for the two hundred and fifteenth time at about ten past ten with two men on the handles, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody walked on one single trip of it.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and did not once put it down in the middle, and not one figure of any kind stands in any page in this harbor against that man or against anything that he does.

---

At about one, in about nine people, with that door open and that yard going on outside it, the man of about twenty-seven of that second lay asked out loud, in the ordinary way and to nobody in particular, why that thing behind that reeve's bench takes a minute longer each way than it did the Sunday before.

About four of that nine heard the whole of it and about four of that nine did not, and nobody in that shed answered it for about as long as it takes to boil a kettle.

Then a man of about forty-one, who has stood in that lane on two Sundays and has counted there with three other men, said out loud that he had a thing to say about that, and gave it in these words.

"It is not that thing that has been changing. It is not the mortar and it never was. It is the standing in front of it. There are more of them standing in front of it than there used to be and nobody ever counted the people."

---

Then that same man said out loud that he has known that for a long time, and that he did not say it because nobody had asked him, and that he did not think it was his to say.

Nobody in that shed said one word back to any of that. The keeper said nothing back to it and the man of about thirty-two said nothing back to it and about four of that nine went on with what they were doing.

Nothing about it was written down at any hour of that Monday by anybody in any room in this harbor. It is not in that fitting-out sheet and it is not in that wage sheet and it is not in the inside of that door and it is not on that wall in the room over that chandler's store.

---

About four of that nine said out loud, at about a quarter past one, that four people standing in a lane on a Sunday morning counting that thing cannot be told to stop by anybody in this harbor, and that there is no page in this harbor that says anything to anybody about anything, and that there has not been one since the fourteenth of August.

About four of that nine said out loud that they had thought about saying out loud that those four ought to go on and had not said it, and not one of that nine said out loud that those four ought to go on either.

Nothing whatever was settled in that yard on that Monday about the four of them.

About four of that nine said out loud that an answer given out loud in about nine people on an ordinary Monday is not a rule and is not a claim and is not a correction and is not a refusal, and that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and none of those four was said at about one that afternoon.

---

About four of that nine said out loud that a man who says a thing out loud in about nine people on a Monday is not saying it to the man who asked, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

The man of about twenty-seven of that second lay took that answer and did not say one word back to it, and he went out of that shed at about two and came back in again a little after three and said nothing else at all.

About four of that nine said out loud that not one of that nine was going to go up that lane and say that thing to anybody, and that a thing said once in a shed is said once.

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Monday, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had not known he was in that shed at all until he stood up out of that bench at about ten.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for ten days and nobody has touched it, and the man of about forty-one looked down at it at about two and read nothing on it and said nothing about it.

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty-two days by that Monday and stood at the back of that shed for about two hours of it and said nothing.

The ordinary work of that Monday was two boats off that mole before noon in that wet and a barrow of ash taken out to the far end of that quay and left standing there, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about half past three, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-sixth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Thirteen hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries thirteen against this day.

ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE SAID OUT LOUD THAT THOSE FOUR CANNOT BE TOLD TO STOP. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO SAID ALMOST NOTHING AT ANY HOUR OF THAT MONDAY. *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a question put in the ordinary way went three Sundays and about nine days without anybody coming near it is that a man who has been in a lane on a Sunday morning has nothing to ask anybody, and that nobody in that lane had said out loud that there was a question there at all until one was put in the ordinary way on that Monday; that the reason the answer came from a man of about forty-one is that he is one of the four who have been standing in front of that thing and has had to give his own figure out loud to three other men, and that a man who has had to say a number out loud to strangers is the only man in this harbor who has ever had to think about what the number is a number of; that the reason nothing was written down is that four sentences spoken out loud in about nine people in a yard are a thing that happened, and this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and what happened in a yard is not any of those four, and that a keeper who has written down everything else in that yard for six weeks has not got a column for it and would not know which of the four to put it under; that the reason about four of that nine said out loud that those four cannot be told to stop and not one of that nine said out loud that they ought to go on is that telling a man in a lane to go on is the same act as standing in that lane with a figure of one's own, and that about four people in this yard have just watched one man give that act up.*
