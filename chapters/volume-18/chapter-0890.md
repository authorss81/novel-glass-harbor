# Chapter 890

*Tuesday the Twenty-Seventh Day of December, the Thirtieth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, the Hundred and Thirteenth Wage Tuesday, a Chest on That Step From About Ten Until About Four and Twenty Envelopes in It and None of Them Opened at Any Hour, Twenty-Six Lines at One Hundred and Four Marks Standing Unmoved and the Hundred and Fourteenth Week Fallen Due at the Close of It, at About Six and After That Reading the Column of the First Time Opened and Added Up Once in Writing and Not Read Out to Anybody, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-Three and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Eighty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-Two and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Eighty-Four*

Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of December, with that market open from six and an early frost that had every iron rail in that yard white by eight and a flat grey sea all day with no swell on it at any hour, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is the hundred and thirteenth wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a fitting-out line, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-ninth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January. That chest came out onto that step at about ten and stayed on it until about four with twenty envelopes in it and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody. That foot is not read at any hour of that Tuesday and stands at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and third days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this thirtieth day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and eighty-eighth day and no answer has come back off it. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninetieth day of not being asked a question. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody stand at the nine hundred and twenty-eighth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and ninety-fourth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and twelfth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and sixty-seventh day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-second day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its four hundred and ninety-ninth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirtieth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and sixteenth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and twenty-first days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eighty-ninth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for eighty-eight days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and thirty-fifth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty days. Three hundred and eighty-eighth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The hundred and thirteenth of those Tuesdays came round again, and a chest was put out on that step at about ten and taken off it at about four with twenty envelopes in it, and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that Tuesday.

At about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and both of those weeks stood on that sheet at the same hour of the same morning as on the four Tuesdays before it, one above the other, and nobody read either of them out.

The hundred and fourteenth week stood fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of it and did not move a mark, and that column has stood at twenty-six lines and one hundred and four marks since September and nothing has ever been drawn off it.

About four of that nine said out loud that a chest shut from about ten until about four has now been shut on one hundred and thirteen of those Tuesdays, and that the twenty envelopes in it have been in it since September and nobody in this harbor has ever given a reason for that.
The rule in the inside of that door has six lines on it and is on its eighty-ninth day, and about four of that nine said out loud that it is the only thing in this harbor written down by somebody on purpose that anybody is allowed to take back.

---

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-six days by that Tuesday, and has stood in that lane on eight Sundays and said nothing on any of the eight, and nobody has asked him for anything on any of the fifty-six days.

About four of that nine said out loud that fifty-six days is a long time for a man to be in a place without once saying what he is there for, and about four of those nine said out loud that there is nobody in this harbor to say it to.

That is not a question and nobody in that shed put it to anybody.

The woman of about thirty-one has stood at the back of that shed on every one of those Tuesdays since she came to that market, and on this one she did not turn round, and about four of that nine noticed that and about four of that nine did not.

---

Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head that afternoon and answered one another at two hours of it and the sound did not run together into one note and nobody on that quay tried to make it run together.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was two boats off that mole before noon on a flat sea and a hand-cart of coal taken up to the second lay and brought back with the straps cut again, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

That piece of wood was still lying face up on that bench at about five that afternoon and has not been taken off that bench by anybody since about ten past four on Friday, and about four of that nine walked past it and looked down at it and said nothing.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked at the far end of that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about eleven until about half past four, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it, and she does not know that he has been counting anything.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and sixteenth day and about four of that nine said out loud that a leaf is the only thing in this harbor that arrives from somewhere else without anybody here deciding when it will come.

Those four columns take one entry a day and five a day and no entry at all and no line in thirty days, and about four of that nine said out loud that a column that has stood empty for thirty days is not going to fill itself by being looked at.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fortieth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and ninety-six words, and that chest came first in it and the two men of that place came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and no count of any kind is in that reading and the count that was taken at about six was taken after that reading and is not in it.

Nobody was thanked for a Tuesday on which two figures of the same thing were written on one sheet at the same hour, the way the keeper has written them on every one of those Tuesdays, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and seven lines on it.

*First column, entered on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirtieth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was the hundred and thirteenth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-ninth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January; that that chest stood on that step from about ten until about four with twenty envelopes in it and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody; that at about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, and that both of those weeks stood on that sheet at the same hour of the same morning as they had stood on the four of those Tuesdays before it, one above the other; that the hundred and fourteenth week stood fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of it and did not move a mark; that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-six days and has stood in that lane on eight Sundays and said nothing on any of the eight, and that nobody has asked him for anything on any of the fifty-six days; that about four of that nine said out loud that there is nobody in this harbor to say it to, and that that is not a question and nobody put it to anybody; that that piece of wood has not been taken off that bench by anybody since about ten past four on Friday; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked at the far end of that yard over her own page from about ten until about four and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it, and that she does not know that he has been counting anything; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and fortieth day of that run; that twelve hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and the page of days carries twelve against this day; and that at about six that evening, after that reading and not in it, the column headed FIRST TIME on the face of that page of days was opened and added up once in writing, one figure to a day, being two on the first of these ten days, one on the second, nothing on the third, four on the fourth, one on the fifth, nothing on the sixth, three on the seventh, one on the eighth, one on the ninth and two on the tenth, which is fifteen, and that the fifteen are two men carrying a hand-cart of coke up that lane and down it again on the first of them because the cart would not go round at the bottom of it, and a figure of a boat chalked on the flat of that bench stone on the same day and not rubbed out; the hour the covers came off that cistern written on the back of that rota board on the second of them; nothing on the third; six men at three days worked on one line, that day written on the face of the line before the line was worked, a mole-head lamp glass carried up from the store by the man of about thirty-two, and a pencil put into that man's hand at about four on the fourth of them; a man in that shed saying out loud that he had put a cost on somebody else and had not known it on the fifth of them; nothing on the sixth; a line worked for one day only, a man of about thirty-one coming into that shed before ten, and a lamp left burning on that bench until about half past five on the seventh of them; four people in that lane saying their own figures out loud to one another on the eighth of them; the second figure of that foot said at one moment by three people in that shed on the ninth of them; and both of those weeks on one sheet at one hour and that column opened and added up in writing and read out to nobody on the tenth; and that a day with no figure in it has not been written as though it had nothing in it; that that column is on that page and on no other page in this harbor, that no figure of it was read out to anybody at any hour of that day, that it is not added up again and is not read out in a room and is not in that reading, and that it is taken once.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the column of the first time was taken at about six and not at about half past five is that a count of what a harbor has not done before cannot be made by the same people who are doing the doing, and that the only man in that yard who has been in nine of those ten days is the one man who does not keep it; that the reason a figure with no morning behind it is in that column and not in another is that a man putting a piece of wood down on a bench in the rain has not done a thing this harbor has not done before and has stopped a thing, and that those are not the same and only one of them is new; that the reason nobody said out loud that two weeks standing on one sheet at the same hour had not happened before is that nobody in that shed except the keeper had ever read the top of that sheet, and that six weeks of saying figures out loud after a settlement has not turned anybody's eye to the head of a wage column; that the reason there is nobody in this harbor to say what a man of about thirty-one is here for is that nobody has the standing to require an answer of anybody in it, and that this has been true since the fourteenth of August, and that eight Sundays of a man's silence and a hundred and forty days of a column's silence are the same silence and are not the same silence, and this harbor has not been told so either.*
