# Chapter 861

*Monday the Twenty-Eighth Day of November, the First Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Fifth Charge on That Cistern at About Eleven and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, a Figure for the Hundredth Time That Cistern Was Charged and a Figure for the Two Hundredth and Nothing at All for What the Seventh Sunday of This Run Is For, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety-Four and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Forty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Monday the twenty-eighth day of November, with that market open from six and a close grey morning with no wind in it at all and a hard rime standing on everything in that yard which does not get the sun before ten, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Monday is the first day of a run of fifty days and is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on tomorrow, being the twenty-ninth day of November. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about eleven with two men and a barrow, and it was the two hundred and fifth time that butt has been filled in this harbor, and not one of those fourteen trips was walked on. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and thirty-two marks, five shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and twenty-two thousand five hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and sixty-five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventy-fourth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this first day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and fifty-ninth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixty-first day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the eight hundred and ninety-ninth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixty-fifth night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eighty-third day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and thirty-eighth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fifty-third day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventieth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and first day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its eighty-seventh day. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety-two days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixtieth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its fifty-ninth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and six days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in one day. Three hundred and fifty-ninth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about eleven and it was the two hundred and fifth time, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off at about four, and nobody put a stick into it at any hour of that day.

The second man on those pails is an old man of about sixty-eight and has done ten of those fourteen trips every Monday and Thursday since the middle of August, and he did not once put the barrow down in the middle.

Those four new crews began their seventy-first day this morning and took the day's work off in about two hours, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody has said out loud what a seventy-first day is for, and then went back to the work in front of them.

The ordinary work of that Monday was three boats off that mole before noon on flat water and a bucket turned over out of the rain at that bench, and not one of those three came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

---

A man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place came down that lane at about ten with two pails and went back up it at about eleven, and on his way up he stood in front of that wall behind that reeve's bench for the length of a counted minute and said nothing to anybody about the fact that it is a wall and not a bench, and has been one and not the other for a hundred and thirty-four Sundays.

The man of about thirty-two wrote out the ten days of that run for himself on the back of a board off a broken crate and did not put it on the leaf, and it went ten days from Monday to Wednesday, and he read it twice, which is what he does with gauges.

---

At about one in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine said out loud that a run of fifty days which begins on a Monday carries seven Sundays in it and not eight.

They said it in about four different words, and the four words were that the wall behind that reeve's bench comes down seven times inside those fifty days and not eight, and that this harbor has a figure for the hundred and fortieth time it came down and a figure for the hundred and forty-first time, and that there is no figure of any kind for an eighth, and that nobody in this harbor has ever needed one before.

One of about four of them asked, in the ordinary way and to nobody in particular, what the seventh Sunday of that run is for when there is nobody at the end of it with a figure for an eighth, and about four of that nine turned and looked at about four other of that nine, and nobody in that shed answered it.

The keeper said out loud that a run of days is a run of days, and that he is not going to give that seventh Sunday a number until somebody tells him what the number is for, and that a number for nothing is the same as no number at all and he would rather have the second of those two.

About four of that nine said out loud afterwards that it was nobody's business until somebody asked, which is the first thing said a second time and not the same thing asked twice.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and eleventh day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about one, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about one until about three, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about the ten days he had written out on the back of that board, or about anything else at all.

Six hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and not one of those six was the hand that wrote what either sheet went against, and the page of days carries six against this day.

---

That shed stood empty at about five with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and the only figure of that wall inside a hundred yards of that shed is on a piece of wood in one man's own coat, and no page in this harbor has any part of it on it, and nobody in that shed has asked him for it.

That wall was up behind that reeve's bench all day with the mortar showing on one face of it, and nobody stood in front of it and counted it, and nobody in this harbor has a figure of any kind for it on any page, and that is a thing nobody in that shed said out loud.

Nobody was thanked for a cistern filled fourteen times by hand and for one question asked in a shed on a Monday and not answered, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had seventy-eight lines on it.

**THE TWO HUNDRED AND FIFTH CHARGE WENT ONTO THAT CISTERN AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND TOOK FOURTEEN TRIPS BY HAND AND WAS WALKED ON BY NONE OF IT. ONE QUESTION WENT INTO THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED ON THIS DAY, BEING WHAT THE SEVENTH SUNDAY OF THAT RUN OF FIFTY DAYS IS FOR WHEN THERE IS NO FIGURE OF ANY KIND FOR AN EIGHTH, AND NOBODY IN THAT SHED ANSWERED IT. THE COLUMN WAS NOT ADDED UP AND ITS TEN DAYS DO NOT STAND YET. NOTHING CAME INTO THE FOOT OF THAT SHEET AND NO LINE WAS WORKED ON IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND SEVENTY-FOURTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Monday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that a run of days is the one thing in this harbor that everybody can count and nobody has ever counted, because a run of days is everybody's and a day belongs to the whole of a harbor and not to the keeper of a leaf, and that is why he would not put a number on the seventh of seven until somebody said what the number was for; that the reason the shape of that run was said out loud on this first day and not left until the fiftieth is that a fifty-day run beginning on a Monday is one day shorter in Sundays than a fifty-day run beginning on a Sunday, and that this harbor has spent six weeks learning that the day of the week a run begins on is a figure and not a habit; that the reason nobody answered the question about the seventh Sunday is that the seventh Sunday is the first day of the second week of that run and that everybody in that shed knew what it was for and nobody could put it in a sentence that would survive being written down; that the reason a man of about thirty-two wrote ten days out on the back of a board instead of on the leaf is that the leaf keeps four columns and no fifth, and that a thing a man keeps for himself and does not enter up is the only kind of figure in this harbor that costs him nothing and tells him nothing.
