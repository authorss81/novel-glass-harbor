# Chapter 894

*Saturday the Thirty-First Day of December, the Thirty-Fourth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Saturday Carrying No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, Three of the Four Who Stood in That Lane on Sunday the Twenty-Fifth Day of December Coming into That Shed at About Four and Saying Out Loud That They Will Be Up That Lane in the Morning at Half Past Ten, the Man of About Thirty-Two Out on the Water in a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Not in That Yard at Any Hour of That Saturday, That Piece of Wood Eight Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-Seven and Three Thousand and Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-Six and the Second at Three Thousand and Four*

Saturday the thirty-first day of December, with that market open from six and a hard white frost on every rail in that yard before nine that took an hour to melt off the bars of the gate, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Saturday is an ordinary working Saturday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Monday the second day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Saturday and nothing is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Saturday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and seventh days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried across this thirty-fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at its five hundred and ninety-second day. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninety-fourth day of not being asked anything. The two things upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirty-second day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and ninety-eighth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and sixteenth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are at the four hundred and seventy-first day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-sixth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and third day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-fourth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and twentieth day. That correction and that refusal have stood at that end stall one hundred and twenty-fifth days at one size with the wrong figure still left showing underneath and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-third day and it has six lines on it and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety-two days. That wall in the room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and thirty-ninth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of those four columns stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-four days. Three hundred and ninety-second days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday was three boats off that mole before noon in that frost and a hand-cart of coal taken up to the second lay and brought back down again with the straps cut again, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

At about four three men came into that shed who had not been in that yard at any hour of the three weeks before it, and one of them was a man of about forty-one and one of them was a man of about thirty-three and one of them was a man of about fifty.

---

Those three stood in that doorway for about ten minutes and then one of them asked for a cup of water and about four of that nine gave him one, and nobody asked any of the three of them where they had come from and none of the three of them said.

About four of that nine said out loud that that yard has not had a stranger since a Tuesday and that not one of them has said out loud that it did not mind.

---

One of about four of that nine said out loud that the three of them will be up that lane in the morning at half past ten, and that they said it to nobody in particular, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that at any hour of it.

Nobody in that shed told any of those three to go and nobody in that shed told any of those three to stop, and nobody wrote one word of that Saturday down anywhere at any hour of it.

About four of that nine said out loud that three men who have never been in this yard have now said out loud in a working place that they will be somewhere at a particular hour in the morning, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that either.

That is not a question and nobody in that shed put it to anybody.

---

That correction and that refusal have stood at that end stall at one size and a hand's breadth apart for one hundred and twenty-five days, and the wrong figure is still showing underneath the correction and has not been struck out, and not one person in this harbor has ever said out loud which of the two of them is right.

The honest total of four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, is on that community's own page in that community's own hand and has been there ninety-two days, and about four of that nine said out loud that it is the only figure this harbor has ever published about itself and that nobody has ever thought of being a figure about anybody.

---

The man of about thirty-two was not in that yard at any hour of that Saturday. He went out on the water in a boat hired at the figure in chalk with two other men at about seven and came in at that mole after dark with frost in the seam of that coat and said nothing to anybody about any of it.

About four of that nine said out loud that a boat hired at the figure in chalk is a figure in chalk and not a line on that sheet, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and about four of that nine said out loud that a page is a thing a person can be said to be wrong about, and that there was nothing in that yard on that Saturday a person could be said to be wrong about, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

Neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it because he was not in that yard to say one word in.

---

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for eight days and nobody has touched it, and one of those three men who came in at about four looked down at it and read the top line of it and said nothing about it to anybody.

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty days by that Saturday and had stood in that lane on eight Sundays and said nothing on any of the eight, and about four of that nine said out loud that a man who comes down to the same quay every day for sixty days and says nothing at any hour of them is either waiting for one thing or is doing something of his own that nobody here has been told about, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

He stood at the back of that shed for about an hour of that Saturday and asked nobody for anything and said nothing.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench with a cup of tea he did not drink in his own two hands, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-fourth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a Saturday on which three men who had never been in that yard walked in and said out loud where they would be in the morning, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and eleven lines on it.

**THREE MEN WHO HAD NOT BEEN IN THAT YARD AT ANY HOUR OF THE THREE WEEKS BEFORE IT CAME INTO THAT SHED AT ABOUT FOUR AND ONE OF ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE SAID OUT LOUD THAT THEY WILL BE UP THAT LANE IN THE MORNING AT HALF PAST TEN. NOBODY TOLD ANY OF THEM TO GO AND NOBODY TOLD ANY OF THEM TO STOP. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO WAS OUT ON THE WATER ALL DAY AND CAME IN AFTER DARK. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS THREE HUNDRED AND SEVENTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the thirty-first day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirty-fourth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Saturday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Monday the second day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January; that nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that day and that nothing is owed to it and that the keeper did not read that foot at any hour of that Saturday; that at about four three men came into that shed who had not been in that yard at any hour of the three weeks before it, being a man of about forty-one, a man of about thirty-three and a man of about fifty; that one of about four of that nine said out loud that the three of them will be up that lane in the morning at half past ten, and that they said it to nobody in particular, and that about four of that nine said nothing back to that; that nobody in that shed told any of those three to go and nobody in that shed told any of those three to stop; that one of those three looked down at that piece of wood and read the top line of it and said nothing about it to anybody; that the man of about thirty-two was not in that yard at any hour of that Saturday and went out on the water in a boat hired at the figure in chalk at about seven and came in after dark; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four; that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty days and stood at the back of that shed for about an hour and said nothing; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and forty-fourth day of that run; and that ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason three men came into that shed at about four on that Saturday is that a man who has been standing in a lane at half past ten on a Sunday and has said a figure out loud to two other men has nothing left to do at home until the next Sunday morning, and that this harbor is the only place within four miles where anybody will have the three of them stand in a doorway and say nothing about it; that the reason nobody told them to go and nobody told them to stop is that there is no standing in this harbor to do either of those two things and there has not been one since the fourteenth of August, and that three men finding that out for themselves in about nine people on an ordinary Saturday is the ordinary way it is found out here; that the reason the man of about thirty-two was out on the water all day is not given in that shed by anybody and is not a thing he has told anybody, and that a boat hired at the figure in chalk is not a line on that sheet and cannot be checked against it by anybody afterwards; that the reason one of those three read the top line of that piece of wood and said nothing about it is that a man who has been in a lane for two Sundays knows there is a figure there and has never once been told whose it is, and that reading the top of a thing is not being given a thing.*
