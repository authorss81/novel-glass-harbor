# Chapter 881

*Sunday the Eighteenth Day of December, the Twenty-First Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-Seventh Time, That Wall Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Forty-One Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One and Not Going Up Whole, Fifty-Four Off and Fifty-Four Back and One Hundred and Forty-Nine the Whole of It, a Minute Longer at Each End Than It Ran the Sunday Before and Nobody in That Lane Saying Why, the Man of About Thirty-Two Standing in Front of It on the Third and Last Sunday of This Run and Writing That Figure on That Wood in His Own Hand, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Fourteen and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Forty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirteen and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Thirty-Nine*

Sunday the eighteenth day of December, with that market shut until seven on a Sunday and a dirty sky from the south-west that came onto that quay in three pushes before noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December and the next charge is on Monday the nineteenth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twentieth day of December. That wall behind that reeve's bench came off the face of itself at about half past ten and stood bare forty-one minutes and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at fifty-four minutes off and fifty-four back, one hundred and forty-nine the whole of it, and that is the hundred and thirty-seventh time anybody has done it to it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninety-fourth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this twenty-first day. The notice that went off on the seventeenth of May past stands at its five hundred and seventy-ninth day and nothing has come back along it. That bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and eighty-first day of going unasked. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody have now been nobody's for the nine hundred and nineteenth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and eighty-fifth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and third day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall has been at the back of it for the four hundred and fifty-eighth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is at its four hundred and seventy-third day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at its four hundred and ninetieth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twenty-first day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and seventh day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and twelfth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eightieth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-ninth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-six days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-one days. Three hundred and seventy-ninth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

At about half past ten the top course came off that wall and about four people stepped up to it and began to count, and it stood bare forty-one minutes and went back up at a quarter to one with the top course of it left short, one hundred and forty-nine the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-seventh time.

Both ends of it ran a minute longer than the two ends of the Sunday before ran, and about four of that nine had both of those figures in their heads before a quarter to one, and nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it.

One of those four was the man of about thirty-two and that is the third Sunday of this run he has stood in front of it, and one of those four was a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, and that is the seventh Sunday that man has stood in that lane and on every one of the seven he has kept his mouth shut.

A man came down that lane at about ten and was in it for the half hour it takes him, and he did not speak to any of those four and none of those four spoke to him.

---

Two men from the second lay carried a hand-cart of coke up that lane at about eleven and brought it back down it again empty, because the cart would not go round at the bottom of it, and they said nothing to anybody about it.

He wrote that figure on that wood in his own hand at about a quarter to eleven, standing at the foot of those steps with his back to about four of those four, and that was the hundred and thirty-seventh figure to go onto it and nothing said to him that it was the last one.

What he wanted off that wood that Sunday was the first figure on it, and he went to the top of that column and could not get to it, because the wood is worn smooth at that corner and there is no day against the first figure and none against the eleven under it.

He has been up that lane on a hundred and thirty-seven mornings, and he stood there with the wood in his hand for about ten minutes working at the top of that column and could not get back to the beginning of it, and the last thing he could bring up out of it was a face of a man's own hands and not a morning.

---

At about a quarter to twelve somebody chalked the figure of a boat on the flat of that bench stone, and it was still standing on that stone at half past five that afternoon.

He put that wood back inside that coat at about ten past eleven and stood with his hands in his pockets until a quarter to one, and about three of those four did not see him do that and one of that four saw him do it and has not said a word about it since.

At a quarter to one he said nothing out loud about either end of it, and about four of those nine had already gone back down that lane, and he went down it last and stopped on those steps at the bottom and said out loud to nobody that a hand-cart would not go round there.

That was the only thing he said out loud in that lane all day, and about four of that nine did not hear it and one of that nine heard all of it.

---

The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon in that dirty weather and a barrow of that coke drawn up from the store with no tarpaulin over it at all, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked at the far end of that yard over her own page from about ten until about half past two, and he was in that shed from about ten past eleven until about four, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a count with no day against the top of it is a count a man can go on with and nobody can go back to, and about four of those nine agreed with that and nobody said anything else about it.

One of about four of that nine said out loud that there is nothing wrong with a man wanting to be able to say a number, and that wanting to and being asked to are two different things, and about four of those nine had watched the two of them come apart in their own work some time in the autumn.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirty-first day of that run of days he has not missed.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor forty-seven days by that Sunday and has stood in that lane on seven Sundays and said nothing on any of the seven.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

**THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-SEVENTH TIME THAT WALL CAME DOWN WENT DOWN BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND STOOD BARE FORTY-ONE MINUTES AND WENT BACK UP AT A QUARTER TO ONE AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, FIFTY-FOUR AND FORTY-ONE AND FIFTY-FOUR AND ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-NINE THE WHOLE OF IT, AND BOTH ENDS RAN A MINUTE LONGER THAN THE TIME BEFORE AND NOBODY SAID WHY AND NOBODY ASKED. THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO STOOD IN FRONT OF IT FOR THE THIRD TIME IN THIS RUN AND WROTE THE FIGURE ON THAT WOOD IN HIS OWN HAND AND COULD NOT GET BACK TO THE FIRST ONE ON IT. THIS IS THE LAST SUNDAY OF THIS RUN HE STANDS IN THAT LANE IN. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND NINETY-FOURTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the eighteenth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the twenty-first entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was a wipe day and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December and the next charge is on Monday the nineteenth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twentieth day of December; that that wall came off at about half past ten and stood bare forty-one minutes and went back up at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, at fifty-four minutes off and fifty-four back and forty-one standing bare, one hundred and forty-nine the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-seventh time; that both ends of it ran a minute longer than the two ends of the Sunday before ran and nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it; that about four people stood in front of it from the moment it came off and counted, and that one of those four was the man of about thirty-two and one was a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, who has stood in that lane on seven Sundays and said nothing on any of the seven; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason he went to the top of that column on that Sunday and could not get to the first figure on it is that a column has no top anybody can find once the first of them is worn into the grain, and that a man who has been writing down a thing for a hundred and thirty-seven mornings has never had to look at the top of it before; that the reason he could not bring back a morning out of it is that nobody ever told him that his own wanting to know was the reason he started, and that wanting to be able to say a number is a thing a harbor cannot put on a page because it is not about the work; that the reason about four of those nine agreed with the sentence about a count nobody can go back to is that they have all got one of their own, being the weeks on that wage column and the days on those two lights and the days on that rule in a door, and that every one of those has a day against the top of it and his has not; that the reason nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it is that this harbor has spent six weeks finding out that a figure nobody says out loud cannot be checked, and that the four of them have all worked that out independently and have never once discussed it.*
