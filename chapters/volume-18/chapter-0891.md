# Chapter 891

*Wednesday the Twenty-Eighth Day of December, the Thirty-First Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Wednesday Carrying No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, a Stove Pipe Carried Up to That Second Lay on One Man's Back at About Eleven With Nobody to Carry Any of It, That Piece of Wood Five Days Now Face Up on That Bench and Nobody Going Near It, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-Four and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Ninety-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-Three and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Eighty-Nine*

Wednesday the twenty-eighth day of December, with that market open from six and a wet morning that came in off that point and turned into a cold drizzle by ten without ever becoming rain worth a coat, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday is an ordinary working Wednesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage and settles nothing on a fitting-out line, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-ninth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Wednesday, no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Wednesday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and fourth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried across this thirty-first day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past has now been out five hundred and eighty-ninth days and no reply has come down that road to anybody. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninety-first day of not being asked a question by anybody in this harbor. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody stand at the nine hundred and twenty-ninth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare once more on its six hundred and ninety-fifth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and thirteenth day. That six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and sixty-eighth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-third day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundredth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-first day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and seventeenth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and twenty-second days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninetieth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for eighty-nine days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and thirty-sixth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-one days. Three hundred and eighty-ninth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon in that drizzle, and a stove pipe standing on end in the corner of that shed because there was no cart in that yard with a chain long enough to lay it on.

At about eleven he took that pipe up to the second lay on his own back, and it took him the better part of twenty minutes going up that path, and he set it down twice on the way and did not set it down a third time before he got there.

About four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that yard had asked him to carry it and about four of that nine said nothing about that at all.

---

About four of that nine said out loud that the bend that pipe wants is in the second lay and not in that store, and one of about four of that nine said out loud that a man who says a thing like that in the middle of a morning like that one is a man who has looked at a pipe for longer than that morning. And the words were given in these. "There wants a bend in that before this gets any worse."

Nobody said anything back to that and nobody asked him a thing about it.

He said nothing else out loud at any hour of that Wednesday that anybody in that yard has written down.

---

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for five days, and about four people walked past it on that Wednesday and about four people did not look down at it.

That is a change in that yard. Nobody in that yard has said out loud that it is one, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody there has any way of telling a habit from an arrangement.

About four of that nine said out loud that a thing five days old on that bench is in nobody's way, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

About four of that nine said out loud that nobody has asked whose it is at any hour since Friday, and that nobody in this harbor has the standing to ask anybody that, and that there has not been one since the fourteenth of August.

---

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-seven days by that Wednesday, and he came into that shed at about eleven and stood at the back of it until about one and then went out again and said nothing.

About four of that nine said out loud that a man may stand at the back of a shed for fifty-seven days and not one person in this harbor has ever asked him what he is doing at the back of it.

That is not a question and nobody in that shed put it to anybody.

---

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about eleven until about half past four, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-first day of that run of days he has not missed.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

About half past five on that Wednesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and seventy-nine words, and that pipe came first in it and the five days came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench within a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood and there is no figure of it on any page in this harbor.

Nobody was thanked for a Wednesday on which a man carried a pipe up a path on his own back with nobody to carry any of it and a piece of wood went on sitting on a bench where nobody could say whose it was, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and eight lines on it.

**A STOVE PIPE WENT UP TO THAT SECOND LAY ON ONE MAN'S BACK AT ABOUT ELEVEN AND ABOUT FOUR OF THAT NINE SAID NOTHING ABOUT IT. THAT PIECE OF WOOD HAS BEEN FACE UP ON THAT BENCH FOR FIVE DAYS AND NOBODY HAS ASKED WHOSE IT IS SINCE FRIDAY, AND NOBODY IN THIS HARBOR HAS THE STANDING TO ASK. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS THREE HUNDRED AND FOURTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Wednesday the twenty-eighth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirty-first entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Wednesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twenty-ninth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January; that nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that day and that no line was worked on it and that none is owed to it, and that the keeper did not read that foot at any hour of that Wednesday; that at about eleven the man of about thirty-two took a stove pipe up to the second lay on his own back and set it down twice on the way and did not set it down a third time, and that nobody in that yard had asked him to carry it; that one of about four of that nine said out loud that there wants a bend in that pipe before the weather turns, and that nobody said anything back to that and nobody asked him a thing about it; that that piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for five days and that nobody has taken it off it and that nobody has been given it; that about four people walked past it on that Wednesday and about four did not look down at it, and that nobody in that yard has said out loud that that is a change; that about four of that nine said out loud that nobody has asked whose it is at any hour since Friday and that nobody in this harbor has the standing to ask anybody that; that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-seven days and came into that shed at about eleven and stood at the back of it until about one and said nothing; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and forty-first day of that run; and that nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody in that yard asked him to carry that pipe is that a yard does not ask, and that this has been the way of that yard since before any of them came to it, and that a man who carries a thing up a path in the rain because there is nobody else is not doing a kindness to anybody and would say so himself; that the reason about four people stopped looking down at that piece of wood is that looking at a thing every day for five days is a way of saying a thing about it, and that about four of those people have run out of anything to say about it, and that a thing nobody looks at is not on a page and is not gone; that the reason nobody has asked whose that wood is is that asking is the one act in this harbor that nobody has the standing to do, and that the standing was taken off this harbor on the fourteenth of August and has not come back since, and that a piece of wood lying face up on a bench is the only object in this harbor that is safe from every question anybody might have about it; that the reason a man stands at the back of a shed for fifty-seven days without saying one word is that nobody in this harbor has ever had a room in which that kind of thing could be put, and that he has not asked for one.*
