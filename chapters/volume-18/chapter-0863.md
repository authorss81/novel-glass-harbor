# Chapter 863

*Wednesday the Thirtieth Day of November, the Third Day of a Run of Fifty Days, Line One onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eight With the Fifth of December Written on the Face of It for the Day It Settles, Two Days of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Coal and Oil for Two Days and Seventy Yards of Line and Four Panes of Sheet Glass and Five Men at Two Days at Sixteen Pence a Man-Day, the Whole of That Line Seven Hundred and Ninety-Four Pence and Nothing Coming into the Foot of That Sheet Until Friday, Five Men Out at About Eight and In Wet at About Half Past Three and the Fifth of Them Nineteen and on His First Working Week, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Five Hundred and Ninety-Six and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Fifty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Forty-Nine*

Wednesday the thirtieth day of November, with that market open from six and a bright cold morning with the frost back hard in the shade of everything and that water going out at that mole head in long even rolls with no white on it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday is an ordinary working Wednesday and carries no charge on that cistern, no wipe, and nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the first day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the sixth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was settled on it, and what was worked on it this morning stands on the face of that line and on nothing else in that yard, that foot standing before this morning's work at one hundred and thirty-three thousand one hundred and eighty-eight pence out, being eight hundred and thirty-two marks, five shillings and eightpence, and one hundred and twenty-two thousand five hundred and forty-eight pence short, being seven hundred and sixty-five marks, twelve shillings and fourpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and seventy-sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this third day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and sixty-first day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixty-third day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and first day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and sixty-seventh night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eighty-fifth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fortieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fifty-fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventy-second day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and third day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its eighty-ninth day. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety-four days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixty-second day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its sixty-first day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and eight days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in three days. Three hundred and sixty-first days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Five men went out on that first line at about eight and came in wet at about half past three, and the fifth of them is nineteen and has been out on his first working week of any kind.

That line went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight with the fifth day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is Friday, and the work day and the day it settles are never the same day in this yard.

Its five parts are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence; coal and oil for two days at forty pence, being three shillings and fourpence; seventy yards of line at three pence the yard at seventy times three for two hundred and ten pence, being one mark, four shillings and twopence; four panes of sheet glass at twelve pence a pane at four times twelve for forty-eight pence, being four shillings; and five men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at five times two times sixteen for one hundred and sixty pence, being one mark.

The whole of that line is seven hundred and ninety-four pence, being four marks, twelve shillings and tenpence, and until that yard settles it at about four on the fifth day of December it is on the face of that line and nowhere else in that yard.

---

The keeper wrote those five parts down in their own order at about half past four and read the whole of the line back to nobody, and then put the sheet down on that bench stone with the date still showing on it.

The fifth of those five was out on his first working week, and a man of about thirty-one of that market looked at his hands twice that morning and at the rate on that slate and did not say out loud anything about the two.

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon and a barrow of glass out of the store and in again, and neither of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

Those four panes of sheet glass went into a lamp at the head of that mole at about eleven and that lamp has been out since the middle of the summer, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody has said out loud whose lamp that is.

The nine counters were carried to the close of that day and every one of them was the figure it stood at yesterday with one added, and the four columns took one in the first and five in the second, and the third of those four has had nothing under its heading since that boy wrote it.

About four of that nine said out loud that seventy yards of line at three pence the yard is a figure that has not gone up since the spring and that the coal has gone up twice since the autumn, and that the only part of that line where a man could see what a thing costs him in his own body is the last of the five parts of it.

About half past three those five came in and one of them asked for a dry coat and nobody in that yard had one, and about four of that nine said out loud that a wet man on a Friday is an ordinary Monday and gave him nothing.

---

At about one, in that shed, in about nine people, about four of that nine asked what those five men were paid.

That is one question put to one man at one time and it is not four, and the keeper answered it out loud with the figure off the line, being five men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day and one hundred and sixty pence of the whole of that line, being one mark, and he did not say who hired them, and neither the man who hired them nor the man who set that rate is in that shed.

About four of that nine said out loud afterwards that a line with a day written on the face of it is the only kind of writing in that yard that anybody can check, and the man of about thirty-two said out loud that it is checked by one man and not by nine.

---

The man of about thirty-two read that line from the other side of that bench stone at about half past four and said nothing about it at all, and about four of that nine did not know that he had read it.

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was two boats off that mole before noon and a barrow of glass out of the store and in again, and neither of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirteenth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Eight hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and not one of those eight was the hand that wrote what any sheet went against, and the page of days carries eight against this day.

*First column, entered on Wednesday the thirtieth day of November at about six in the evening in that shed, being the third entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Wednesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the fourth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the first day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the sixth day of December; that five men went out on that first line at about eight and came in wet at about half past three, and the fifth of them is nineteen and has been out on his first working week of any kind; that line one went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight with the fifth day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that the work day and the day it settles are never the same day in that yard; that its five parts are two days of a boat at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, seventy yards of line at three pence the yard at seventy times three, four panes of sheet glass at twelve pence a pane at four times twelve, and five men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at five times two times sixteen; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the figure of five men came out of that shed in a single voice on that Wednesday is that it is the only figure in this harbor that a stranger could arrive tomorrow and check against the men themselves, and that everybody in that shed knew that and nobody had said it out loud before; that the reason nobody said who hired them is that a name under a figure in this harbor is the one thing that turns a working day into a thing somebody is owed something by, and that six weeks of correcting figures has taught this harbor exactly what a name costs and not what it buys; that the reason a line with a day written on the face of it was called the only kind of writing anybody can check is that the day is the part of it that commits itself in advance, and that a page of days cannot commit itself about anything, and that this yard has been living on that difference for six weeks without ever having said so out loud; that the reason the man of about thirty-two read that line from the other side of that stone and said nothing about it is that a line with a date on it is the shape of the thing he keeps on a piece of wood in his own coat, and that the one difference between those two is that nobody has ever asked him for his.
