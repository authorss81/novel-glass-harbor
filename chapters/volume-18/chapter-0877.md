# Chapter 877

*Wednesday the Fourteenth Day of December, the Seventeenth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Wednesday Carrying No Charge and No Wipe and Nothing on a Wage, the Man of About Thirty-Two Standing at That Reeve's Bench at About Eleven for Ten Minutes With Nothing in His Hands and Nothing to Do There, the Slate Not Out of That Coat at Any Hour of This Day, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Ten and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Twenty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Nineteen*

Wednesday the fourteenth day of December, with that market open from six and a still bright morning with the first real cold of it standing in that yard at eight and thawing off the stonework by about eleven, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Wednesday is an ordinary working Wednesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighteenth day of December and the next charge is on Thursday the fifteenth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twentieth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was worked on it and none is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-seven thousand pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-six marks, three shillings and fourpence, and one hundred and twenty-six thousand three hundred and sixty pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-nine marks and ten shillings, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and ninetieth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this seventeenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventy-fifth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventy-seventh day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and fifteenth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and eighty-first night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninety-ninth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fifty-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixty-ninth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-sixth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and seventeenth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and third day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and eight days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-sixth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-fifth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twenty-two days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in seventeen days. Three hundred and seventy-fifth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Nothing happened on that Wednesday that had not happened on the Wednesday before it, and that is the first sentence anybody in that yard has been able to say about a day in three weeks.

The ordinary work of that Wednesday was three boats off that mole before noon and that barrow of the case and the four nails going back up to the second lay with a hand-cart instead of a barrow, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

---

The man of about thirty-two walked up that lane at about eleven on that Wednesday with nothing in his hands and stood at that reeve's bench for about ten minutes, and there was nothing to be done at that bench on an ordinary Wednesday.

About four people went past him in that lane in those whole ten minutes and not one of them said anything to him, because there is nothing to say to a man standing at a bench on a Wednesday morning.

---

He found out on that Wednesday that he had been going up that lane on a Sunday for as long as he could remember and had never once gone up it on a Wednesday, and that about half of what he had done in that lane in his life had been done in that lane on a Sunday.

He went back down that lane at about a quarter past eleven with his hands still empty, and about four of that nine did not know that he had been up there and one of that nine did.

---

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a lane with anything in it once a week is a lane and not a street, and one of about four of that nine said out loud that it is a lane because a street has shops on it and this one has a bench and a set of steps and nothing else anybody needs.

About four of that nine said out loud that there ought to be a sign at the bottom of it, and about four of those nine said out loud that a sign would be the fifth thing and nobody said anything back to that.
---

The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on that community's own page in that community's own hand and it went up that coast road on the first day of October with ten second hands on the face of it, and about four of that nine said out loud that ten hands have gone over that figure and not one of them has ever wanted it to be a different one.

---

The slate was not out of that coat at any hour of that Wednesday, and about four of that nine did not know that wood was in that room and one of that nine knew.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about two, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about a quarter past eleven until about four, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other one about anything at all.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twenty-seventh day of that run of days he has not missed.

Nine hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a Wednesday on which a man stood at a bench in an empty lane for ten minutes and learned something about his own habits and told nobody, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had ninety-four lines on it.

**THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO STOOD AT THAT REEVE'S BENCH AT ABOUT ELEVEN ON THIS WEDNESDAY FOR TEN MINUTES WITH NOTHING IN HIS HANDS AND FOUND THAT THERE WAS NOTHING TO BE DONE THERE, AND WENT BACK DOWN THAT LANE AT ABOUT A QUARTER PAST ELEVEN WITH HIS HANDS STILL EMPTY. THE SLATE WAS NOT OUT OF THAT COAT AT ANY HOUR OF THIS DAY. NO FIGURE OF THAT THING BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH IS ON ANY PAGE IN THIS HARBOR. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND NINETIETH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Wednesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason there was nothing to be done at that bench on an ordinary Wednesday is that everything anybody has ever done in that lane was done because something was coming down in it, and that a man who turns up without something coming down has brought himself and nothing else; that the reason about half of what he had done in that lane in his life had been done in that lane on a Sunday is that the day of the week had been doing the choosing for him and he had not once noticed that the day of the week was doing anything at all; that the reason about four of those nine said out loud that a sign would be the fifth thing and nobody said anything back is that this harbor has counted four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and has not decided that four is right, and that a word used for the fifth by nine people in a shed on a Wednesday is the ordinary way a fifth thing arrives; that the reason nobody in that shed was told where he had been at about eleven is that a man who has learned something about himself on a Wednesday is not a man anybody in a room is going to be told about.
