# Chapter 892

*Thursday the Twenty-Ninth Day of December, the Thirty-Second Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Rota Day and the Two Hundred and Fourteenth Charge on That Cistern at About Ten Past Ten and Fourteen Trips by Hand and Walked On by None of It, the Old Man of About Sixty-Eight Going Up Ten of Those Fourteen and Not One Figure of Any Kind Standing Against That Man, the Hour the Covers Came Off Written on the Back of That Rota Board for the Second Time and Nothing Written Down About It, That Piece of Wood Six Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-Five and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Ninety-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-Four and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Ninety-Four*

Thursday the twenty-ninth day of December, with that market open from six and a sky over that point that had no weather in it at all until about two and then put a hail on that quay for about half an hour, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Thursday is a rota day and carries a charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Monday the second day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January. That cistern went its fourteenth trip at about ten past ten and it was the two hundred and fourteenth time and it was walked on by none of it. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Thursday, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Thursday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and fifth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this thirty-second day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past stands at its five hundred and ninetieth day and nothing has come back off it. That bellwright is into the five hundred and ninety-second day of going unasked by anybody here. Those two things upon that crown that belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirtieth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and ninety-sixth night. That pair of lights about a mile off that point is at its five hundred and fourteenth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are at the four hundred and sixty-ninth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-fourth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and first day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-second day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and eighteenth day. The correction and the refusal at that end stall have stood side by side for one hundred and twenty-third days with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-first day and it carries six lines and it is not on any wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety days. That room over that chandler's store has had nothing on the inside of it for one hundred and thirty-seven days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of those columns stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-two days. Three hundred and ninetieth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Two men put fourteen trips up that cistern at about ten past ten between them, and the covers went on at about eleven and came off again at about four, and nobody put a stick into that water at any hour of that whole Thursday.

The old man of about sixty-eight went up ten of those fourteen with that barrow on his shoulder and did not once put it down in the middle, and not one figure of any kind stands in any page in this harbor against that man or against anything that he does.

He has been paid nothing for those trips, not a figure and not a half figure and not a penny of anything, and he has never once said out loud that he would ever like anything at all for them.

About four of that nine said out loud that a man may go up ten of those fourteen every Monday and Thursday since the middle of August and that this harbor has not once written down how many Mondays and Thursdays that is, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

At about one a man wrote the hour the covers came off that cistern on the back of that rota board with the stub of chalk, and there was one hour already written there from the Monday before, and he wrote the second one underneath it and did not put a line between the two of them.

About four of that nine said out loud that two figures on a board with a person standing at the top of a ladder is not a record and is not anything anybody can check, and one of about four of that nine said out loud that a figure nobody can check is at least a figure nobody can rub out by accident either.

The keeper said out loud that a thing a man keeps in his own head has the one advantage over that board, which is that it cannot be rubbed out, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for six days and nobody has touched it, and one of about four of that nine said out loud that he has stopped looking down at it, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had not stopped and that they were watching themselves not stop.

That is not a question and nobody in that shed put it to anybody.

About four of that nine said out loud that six days is longer than a thing ought to lie on a bench in a working yard, and about four of that nine said out loud that there is nowhere in this harbor for it to go instead.

About half past two he put his own hand flat on that bench on the far side of that wood, about a foot from it, and kept it there for about as long as it takes to say a thing out loud to nobody about a bend in a pipe, and then took it away again and went back to what he had been doing in that shed.

The woman of about thirty-one has stood at the back of that shed on every one of those days since she came to that market, and on this one she turned about half way through the afternoon and looked at that wood for about two seconds and then turned back again, and about four of that nine noticed that and about four of that nine did not.

---

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-eight days by that Thursday, and he came into that shed at about half past nine and stood at the back of it and asked nobody for anything and said nothing.

About four of that nine said out loud that a man who comes into a shed the same hour every day and says nothing is a man who has decided something, and not one of that nine said out loud what.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about half past three and then took it inside, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

---

The ordinary work of that Thursday was two boats off that mole before noon and a barrow of sand run up to the second lay and left standing in that yard in a heap with a tarpaulin over one end of it only, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-second day of that run of days he has not missed.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

A SECOND HOUR WAS CHALKED ON THE BACK OF THAT ROTA BOARD AT ABOUT ONE AND NOBODY WROTE DOWN WHY. *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Thursday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody in that yard has written down how many Mondays and Thursdays that old man has gone up those stairs is that a figure about a man's back is a figure about a man, and that this harbor has three kinds of thing it may write and not one of them is that; that the reason a second hour went onto the back of that board under the first one with nothing between them is that a rota is a board and the back of a board is not a place where a thing begins, and that nobody there has said so out loud and one man said out loud that it does not matter; that the reason one man has stopped looking down at that piece of wood is that a thing looked at every day becomes a thing being watched, and that this is a fact about the looking and not about the wood; that the reason about four of that nine said out loud that there is nowhere in this harbor for it to go instead is that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and a piece of wood with a hundred and thirty-seven mornings on it is none of the four kinds of thing this harbor may put up in a room, and that a keeper who has worked that out in his own head has still got nowhere to write it.*
