# Chapter 873

*Saturday the Tenth Day of December, the Thirteenth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, Line Four onto That Fitting-Out Sheet at About Eight With the Fifteenth of December Written on the Face of It for the Day It Settles, Seven Men Out at About Eight and In Wet at About Half Past Two, Two Days of a Boat Hired at the Figure in Chalk and Coal and Oil for Two Days and Forty Yards of Line and a Case and Four Nails and Seven Men at Two Days at Sixteen Pence a Man-Day, the Whole of That Line Seven Hundred and Fifty Pence, Being Four Marks, Nine Shillings and Twopence, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Six and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Five and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Saturday the tenth day of December, with that market shut until seven on a Saturday and a hard blue morning off that point with the cold standing in the stonework until eleven and a shower coming through at about two, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Saturday is an ordinary working Saturday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eleventh day of December and the next charge is on Monday the twelfth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the thirteenth day of December. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was settled on it, and what was worked on it this morning stands on the face of that line and on nothing else in that yard, that foot standing before this morning's work at one hundred and thirty-six thousand two hundred and fifty pence out, being eight hundred and fifty-one marks, seven shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and twenty-five thousand six hundred and ten pence short, being seven hundred and eighty-five marks and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eighty-sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this thirteenth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and seventy-first day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and seventy-third day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and eleventh day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventy-seventh night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and ninety-fifth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and fiftieth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and sixty-fifth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and eighty-second day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirteenth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its ninety-ninth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and four days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its seventy-second day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its seventy-first day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and eighteen days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirteen days. Three hundred and seventy-first days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

Seven men went out on that fourth line at about eight and came in wet at about half past two, and seven is the whole of the labour on it and there is not an eighth man in it.

That fourth line went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight with the fifteenth day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is Monday, and not one penny of it comes into the foot of that sheet before Monday afternoon.

Its five parts are two days of a boat hired at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight for three hundred and thirty-six pence, being two marks, one shilling and fourpence; coal and oil for two days at forty pence, being three shillings and fourpence; forty yards of line at three pence the yard at forty times three for one hundred and twenty pence, being ten shillings; a case and four nails at thirty pence, being two shillings and sixpence; and seven men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at seven times two times sixteen for two hundred and twenty-four pence, being one mark, five shillings and fourpence.

The whole of that line is seven hundred and fifty pence, being four marks, nine shillings and twopence, and the keeper read the total back to nobody.

---

The man of about thirty-two stood at the end of that stone from about half past eight and watched those seven go out and watched them come back in wet, and he had the wooden slate out of that coat in his own hand for about a minute and put it back before anybody came round that shed.

What he wanted off that wood that Saturday was the first entry on it, and the first entry is in his own hand, and he cannot remember standing in that lane on the morning he wrote it.

There are about twenty of them up there in his own writing that he does not remember putting there, and no day is against any of the twenty, and he had never noticed that the hand and the memory come apart at the same place on that wood.

He did not say that out loud to anybody, and about four of that nine did not know he had had the wood in his hand.

---

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a case and four nails at thirty pence is the one part of that line anybody could look at, and that a man could count them and see they were four.

About four of that nine said out loud that nobody in that yard has ever counted four nails before, and that a line which ends in something a stranger can count is the best line there is, and the keeper said nothing back to that.

---

The ordinary work of that Saturday was three boats off that mole before noon and a barrow of forty yards of that line run out to the second lay and brought back a quarter short, and neither of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and twenty-third day of that run of days he has not missed.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until one, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed with her for half an hour, and neither of them said one word to the other one about anything at all.

Nine hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries nine against this day.

---

About half past five on that Saturday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and eighteen words, and that fourth line came first in it and the thing about the hand and the memory came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench inside a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood in one man's own coat and there are about twenty of them on it that nobody living has a memory of.

Nobody was thanked for a Saturday on which a man found out that the writing on a piece of wood is older than his memory of writing it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had ninety lines on it.

THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO FOUND ON THAT PIECE OF WOOD ABOUT TWENTY FIGURES IN HIS OWN HAND THAT HE DOES NOT REMEMBER PUTTING THERE AND NO DAY AGAINST ANY OF THEM, AND SAID NOTHING OUT LOUD TO ANYBODY ABOUT IT. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTY-SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Saturday the tenth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirteenth entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Saturday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eleventh day of December and the next charge is on Monday the twelfth day of December; that seven men went out on that fourth line at about eight and came in wet at about half past two, and that seven is the whole of the labour on it and there is not an eighth man in it; that line four went onto that fitting-out sheet at about eight with the fifteenth day of December written on the face of it for the day it settles, and that day is Monday, and that not one penny of it comes into the foot of that sheet before that Monday afternoon; that its five parts are two days of a boat at the figure in chalk at two times one hundred and sixty-eight, coal and oil for two days at forty pence, forty yards of line at three pence the yard at forty times three, a case and four nails at thirty pence, and seven men at two days at sixteen pence a man-day at seven times two times sixteen; that the whole of that line is seven hundred and fifty pence, being four marks, nine shillings and twopence, and that the keeper read the total back to nobody; that the man of about thirty-two stood at the end of that stone from about half past eight with the wooden slate out of that coat in his own hand for about a minute of it and put it back before anybody came round the corner of that shed; that the first entry on that wood is in his own hand and he cannot remember standing in that lane on the morning he wrote it, and that there are about twenty of them up there that he does not remember putting there and no day against any of the twenty; that he did not say that out loud to anybody, and that about four of that nine did not know he had had the wood in his hand and one of that nine knew; that one of about four of that nine said out loud that a case and four nails at thirty pence is the one part of that line anybody could look at; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of the second lay for one thing, being the hundred and twenty-third day of that run; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about one and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about anything at all; that nine hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day and the page of days carries nine against this day.

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Saturday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason a line ends in a case and four nails is that a yard has learned to write down the parts of a thing it can be asked about later, and that four nails is a figure a stranger can count and every other part of that line is a figure only that yard can check; that the reason the hand and the memory come apart at the same place on that wood is that a man begins writing before he begins keeping, and that the first part of it was answering somebody and not recording a thing, and that he has never had the whole of that in one piece in his own head before that Saturday; that the reason nobody was told about it is that telling it out loud in that shed would make about four of that nine curious about the wood, and that a thing about four people are curious about is a thing with owners, and that is the whole of what he was protecting and it is not the wood.
