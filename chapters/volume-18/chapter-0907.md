# Chapter 907

*Friday the Thirteenth Day of January, the Forty-Seventh Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Friday With No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, That Drawer in the Corner of That Room Opened Once and the Outside of It Written On Again in a Keeper's Hand, That Piece of Wood Twenty-One Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Forty and Three Thousand and Seventy-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirty-Nine and the Second at Three Thousand and Sixty-Nine*

Friday the thirteenth day of January, with that market open from six and a bell ringing off that mole before six with a grey morning behind it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Friday is an ordinary working Friday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside that run is on Sunday the fifteenth day of January and the next charge is on Monday the sixteenth day of January and there is no wage Tuesday after it inside that run. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Friday and nothing is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Friday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and twentieth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine accounts stood through that forty-seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is kept at its six hundred and fifth day. That bellwright is kept into the six hundred and seventh day of not being asked anything by anybody. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are borne at the nine hundred and forty-fifth day. That bench beneath that pump was kept bare again on its seven hundred and eleventh night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are found at their five hundred and twenty-ninth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are read at the four hundred and eighty-fourth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is marked at its four hundred and ninety-ninth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is found at its five hundred and sixteenth day. The man of about fifty-one is heard at the five hundred and forty-seventh day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is set on its hundred and thirty-third day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and thirty-eighth days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is laid on its hundred and sixth day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in that harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, has been on that community's own page for a hundred and five days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fifty-second days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-seven days. Four hundred and fifth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That bell was ringing off that mole before six and carried across that flat water, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bell off that mole before six means the boats will be away early.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Friday, and about four of that nine said out loud that a Friday after a rota day is a quiet day in that shed.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for long days and nobody has touched it at any hour of that Friday, and about four of that nine said out loud that long days on a bench are three weeks by any man's count and nobody wrote it down as one.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the two of them are still married, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

---

At about three about four people went up to that room over that store with the keeper and stood round while he opened that drawer in the corner of it once, and nobody said one word out loud about what was inside it at any hour.

The keeper wrote on the outside of that drawer again in his own hand what was in it and the day it went in, and about four people standing in that room read it and said nothing back to it at any hour.

Nothing in that harbor explains that drawer and nothing said out loud in that room explained it, and a child was told the practice and not the contents and was not told what the figures are.

That wall of that room over that store stood bare while that drawer was opened and shut, and nothing went onto it at any hour of that Friday.

---

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor seventy-three days by that Friday and stood at the back of that shed for about three hours of it and said nothing and asked nobody for anything.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell has been on that board for its hundred and thirty-third day, and about four of that nine said out loud that a leaf on its hundred and thirty-third day is still the only thing nobody can turn.

The ordinary work of that Friday was two boats off that mole before noon in that grey morning and a barrow of sand run up to that second lay, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fifty-seventh day of that run of days he has not missed.

Twelve hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries twelve against this day.

---

At about four the keeper came down from that room and went on with his work in that shed, and about four of that nine said out loud that a drawer opened once in the afternoon is still shut by evening, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

At about eleven two men mended a rope on that quay in the open and left the cut ends in a heap, and about four of that nine said out loud that a rope mended in the open before noon will hold through the afternoon tide.

About four of that nine said out loud at about noon that a Friday the thirteenth is a Friday like any other in that yard, and nobody in that shed knocked on wood or turned round twice, and about four of that nine laughed at nothing and went on working.

Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head that Friday and answered one another at two hours of it, and the sound did not run together into one note and nobody on that quay tried to make it one.

---

About half past five on that Friday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and ninety words, and that bell came first in it and that drawer came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench within a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood and there is no figure of it on any page in that harbor.

"**That bell was ringing off that mole before six and carried across that flat water, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bell off that mole before six means the boats will be away early. The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Friday, and about four of that nine said out loud that a Friday after a rota day is a quiet day for working men. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Three hundred and twentieth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-seven days and is neither shut nor struck. One hundred and thirty-eighth days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and thirty-third day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and that wall of the room over the chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and fifty-second days. It is four hundred and fifth days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, he has not been on the water, and no figure at all is set against any part of it. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. At about eleven two men mended a rope on that quay in the open and left the cut ends in a heap, and about four of that nine said out loud that a rope mended in the open before noon will hold through the afternoon tide in that harbor. About four of that nine said out loud at about noon that a Friday like any other in that yard needs no mark on it, and nobody in that shed knocked on wood or turned round twice, and about four of that nine laughed at nothing and went on working in that shed. Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head that Friday and answered one another at two hours of it, and the sound did not run together into one note and nobody on that quay tried to make it one in that harbor. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head at first light yet still now here still now.**"

Nobody was thanked for a Friday on which a drawer was opened once and written on again and nothing explained and a wall stood bare beside it, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and twenty-four lines on it.

*First column, entered on Friday the thirteenth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the forty-seventh entry of that run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Friday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday; that nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet and it was not read; that at about three about four people went up to that room over that store and the keeper opened that drawer in the corner once and wrote on the outside of it again what was in it and the day it went in; that nothing went onto that wall of that room at any hour; that that piece of wood has been face up for three weeks; that twelve hands went onto two sheets; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for one thing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason that drawer was opened once is not given out loud by anybody in that harbor and is not a thing the keeper has told anybody, and that what is inside it is not a thing anybody in that room said out loud; that the reason the keeper wrote on the outside of it again is that a drawer that holds a rule carries what is in it and the day it went in on its face; that the reason nothing went onto that wall is that a bare wall in a room that hears nothing is a wall nobody writes on without being told.*
