# Chapter 888

*Sunday the Twenty-Fifth Day of December, the Twenty-Eighth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-Eighth Time, That Wall Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Forty-Two Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One and Not Going Up Whole, Fifty-Five Off and Fifty-Five Back and One Hundred and Fifty-Two the Whole of It, a Minute Longer at Each End Than It Ran the Sunday Before and Nobody in That Lane Saying Why, About Four People in Front of It Counting Who Were Not There Before and the Man of About Thirty-Two Not One of Them, the Man of About Thirty-One of That Lowfen Reed's Own Place on His Eighth Sunday in That Lane Saying Nothing, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-One and Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Seventy-Four*

Sunday the twenty-fifth day of December, with that market shut until seven on a Sunday and hard rain coming straight down on that quay from about nine until about one and a dirty afternoon after it, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage and settles nothing on a fitting-out line, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Monday the twenty-sixth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the twenty-seventh day of December. That wall behind that reeve's bench came off the face of itself at about half past ten and stood bare forty-two minutes and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at fifty-five minutes off and fifty-five back, one hundred and fifty-two the whole of it, and that is the hundred and thirty-eighth time anybody has done it to it. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand four hundred and forty-two pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-five marks, three shillings and sixpence, and one hundred and twenty-seven thousand eight hundred and two pence short, being seven hundred and ninety-eight marks, ten shillings and twopence, is not read at any hour of that Sunday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and first days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried through this twenty-eighth day. The notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and eighty-sixth day and no answer has come back off it. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and eighty-eighth day of not being asked a question. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody stand at the nine hundred and twenty-sixth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and ninety-second night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and tenth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and sixty-fifth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eightieth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its four hundred and ninety-seventh day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and twenty-eighth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and fourteenth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and nineteenth days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its eighty-seventh day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for eighty-six days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and thirty-third days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in twenty-eight days. Three hundred and eighty-sixth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

About four people who had not stood in that lane before came up it at about half past ten and stepped up to that wall and began to count, and it stood bare forty-two minutes and went back up at a quarter to one with the top course of it left short, one hundred and fifty-two the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-eighth time.

Both ends of it ran a minute longer than the two ends of the Sunday before ran, and nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it.

None of those four had stood in front of that thing on any Sunday before, and not one of that nine could say afterwards where any of the other three had come from.

---

The man of about thirty-two went up that lane at about ten and stood well down it with his back to a drainpipe and did not go to that wall and did not stand in front of it, and about four of that nine did not know he had been in that lane at all.

He stayed where he was for the whole of that hour and a bit over, and then went back down that lane, and he did not have that piece of wood in his hand at any hour of that Sunday.

The four of them said their own figures out loud to one another for the first time in this run, and about two of those four figures were a minute apart, and nobody in that lane told any of them to do that and nobody in that lane told any of them to stop.

There is no page in this harbor that says one word to any of them about that Sunday or about any other Sunday, and there is no board and there is no drawer in any room in this harbor that has one figure of any of it in it.

About four of that nine said out loud that tomorrow is a charge day and that the covers come off that cistern at about four, and that not one of them has any way of finding out whether four people will stand in that lane at half past ten tomorrow.

---

A man came down that lane at about ten and was in it for the half hour it takes him and went away again before a quarter to one, and he did not speak to any of those four and none of those four spoke to him, and about four people in that lane would not be able to describe him this afternoon.

A man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place stood in that lane from about half past ten until about a quarter to one and watched four people he had never seen count there and said nothing at any hour of it, and that is the eighth Sunday he has stood in that lane and he has not said one word on any of the eight.

He had been in that harbor fifty-four days and had asked nobody for anything on any of the fifty-four, and about four of that nine said out loud that they have given up trying to work out what he is here for.

---

The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on that community's own page in that community's own hand, and about four of that nine said out loud that it is the only figure in this harbor that nobody has ever thought of being a figure about anybody.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon in that rain and a bucket turned over out of it at that bench and a hand-cart of coke left standing in the open because there was nowhere dry to put it, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

That piece of wood was still face up on that bench in that shed at about six that evening and nobody had touched it, and the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked at the far end of that yard over her own page until about two and said not one word to him about anything.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and thirty-eighth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Thirteen hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries thirteen against this day.

---

About half past five on that Sunday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about one thousand and one words, and that wipe came first in it and the four who were not there before came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and there is no figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench on any page in this harbor and the one there was is lying face up on a bench in that shed and no figure of it and no count of any kind is in that reading.

Nobody was thanked for a Sunday on which four people who had never stood in that lane counted a thing none of them could describe and the man who used to keep the figures stood well down that lane with his back to a drainpipe, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and five lines on it.

**THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-EIGHTH TIME THAT WALL CAME DOWN WENT DOWN BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND STOOD BARE FORTY-TWO MINUTES AND WENT BACK UP AT A QUARTER TO ONE AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, FIFTY-FIVE AND FORTY-TWO AND FIFTY-FIVE AND ONE HUNDRED AND FIFTY-TWO THE WHOLE OF IT, AND BOTH ENDS RAN A MINUTE LONGER THAN THE TIME BEFORE AND NOBODY SAID WHY AND NOBODY ASKED. ABOUT FOUR PEOPLE STOOD IN FRONT OF IT AND NOT ONE OF THE FOUR HAD STOOD THERE BEFORE, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO WAS NOT ONE OF THEM. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS THREE HUNDRED AND FIRST DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

that that wall came off at about half past ten and stood bare forty-two minutes and went back up at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, at fifty-five minutes off and fifty-five back and forty-two standing bare, one hundred and fifty-two the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-eighth time; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason four people who had never stood in that lane were standing in it is that about four people in this harbor have been saying out loud for six weeks that nobody knows who puts that thing up, and that a lane in which nobody knows what happens in it is a lane anybody may walk into on a Sunday; that the reason the man of about thirty-two stood well down it with his back to a drainpipe is that he had given away the only figure of it there was on Friday, and that a man who has done that does not come back to watch other people do the counting; that the reason nobody in that lane told those four to go on or to stop is that nobody in this harbor has the standing to tell anybody to do anything, and that a practice with nobody standing over it is not a practice and is not a rule and has no name in this harbor; that the reason a man of about thirty-one stood there and watched four strangers and said nothing for the eighth Sunday is that he has been waiting seven Sundays for somebody to say something and has not been disappointed yet, and that about four of that nine have now given up trying to work out what he is here for and one of that nine has not.*
