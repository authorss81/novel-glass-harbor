# Chapter 904

*Tuesday the Tenth Day of January, the Forty-Fourth Day of a Run of Fifty Days, the Hundred and Fifteenth Wage Tuesday, Twenty-Six Lines and One Hundred and Four Marks Standing Unmoved, the Chest With Twenty Envelopes in It Shut From About Ten Until About Four With Not One Envelope Opened, and the Hundred and Sixteenth Week Fallen Due at the Close of It, an Ordinary Working Tuesday With No Wipe and No Charge and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, That Piece of Wood Eighteen Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirty-Seven and Three Thousand and Fifty-Nine and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Thirty-Six and the Second at Three Thousand and Fifty-Four*

Tuesday the tenth day of January, with that market open from six and a low mist hanging along that quay before six with no wind off that point, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is the hundred and fifteenth wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside that run is on Sunday the fifteenth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the twelfth day of January and there is no wage Tuesday after it inside that run. Twenty-six lines stand at one hundred and four marks and do not move, and that chest with twenty envelopes in it is shut from about ten until about four with not one envelope opened, and the hundred and sixteenth week falls due at the close of it. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, is not read at any hour of that Tuesday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and seventeenth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine records ran through that forty-fourth day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past stays at its six hundred and second day. That bellwright holds into the six hundred and fourth day of not being asked anything by anybody. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody continue at the nine hundred and forty-second day. That bench beneath that pump is bare again on its seven hundred and eighth night. Those two lights about a mile off that point stand at their five hundred and twenty-sixth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall appear at the four hundred and eighty-first day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street is entered at its four hundred and ninety-sixth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is counted at its five hundred and thirteenth day. The man of about fifty-one is kept at the five hundred and forty-fourth day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell remains on its hundred and thirtieth day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and thirty-fifth days side by side at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door abides on its hundred and third day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in that harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence, being two marks, seven shillings and twopence, has been on that community's own page for a hundred and two days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-ninth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-four days. Four hundred and second days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That mist hung low along that quay before six and hid the far end of it from plain view, and about four of that nine said out loud that a mist on a wage morning keeps the chest shut tighter than any lock does.

---

That chest was carried out onto that step at about ten and stood there shut until about four, and twenty envelopes lay in it and not one of them was opened at any hour of that Tuesday.

About four of that nine said out loud at about eleven that twenty-six lines at one hundred and four marks is a column that does not move on a wage Tuesday, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

At about four that chest was carried back inside and the hundred and sixteenth week was fallen due at the close of it on all twenty-six lines, and the keeper wrote the week down and said nothing out loud about it.

---

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past five and said almost nothing at any hour of that Tuesday, and about four of that nine said out loud that a wage Tuesday is not a day for asking about anything.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for eighteen days and nobody has touched it at any hour of that Tuesday, and the keeper set that chest down clear of it at about ten.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and the two of them are still married, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

---

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor seventy days by that Tuesday and stood at the back of that shed for about two hours of it and said nothing and asked nobody for anything.

The leaf from the Cinder Bell has been on that board for its hundred and thirtieth day, and about four of that nine said out loud that a hundred and thirty days on a board is a long time for a leaf nobody turns.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was two boats off that mole before noon in that mist and a barrow of coke drawn up from the store with a rope over it, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and fifty-fourth day of that run of days he has not missed.

Ten hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

That bell in that shed rang at about ten and at about four and nobody in that shed answered it, and about four of that nine said out loud that a wage Tuesday keeps its own hours without anybody calling them.

About four of that nine said out loud at about three that a chest shut from about ten until about four is a chest nobody in that yard needs to watch, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

At about noon the keeper chalked the date on the back of that wage board and rubbed it out again, and about four of that nine said out loud that a date chalked and rubbed out is still a date somebody tried to keep.

About four of that nine said out loud at about one that a wage Tuesday with no envelope opened is a Tuesday nobody in that yard will remember for anything but the mist, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

That room over that fish market stood shut all that Tuesday with its long table bare, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bare table in a shut room is not a thing anybody in that yard needs to write down.

---

About half past five on that Tuesday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about nine hundred and thirty words, and that chest came first in it and the bench came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench within a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood and there is no figure of it on any page in that harbor.

That chest was carried out onto that step at about ten and stood there shut until about four, and twenty envelopes lay in it and not one was opened at any hour of that Tuesday. At about four that chest was carried back inside and the hundred and sixteenth week was fallen due at the close of it on all twenty-six lines, and the keeper wrote the week down and said nothing out loud about it. Ten hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day in that shed. That bell in that shed rang at about ten and at about four and nobody in that shed answered it at any hour. About four of that nine said out loud at about three that a chest shut from about ten until about four is a chest nobody needs to watch. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into this yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and twenty-eight plus one thousand six hundred and eighty less one thousand six hundred and eighty is twenty-eight, and the twenty-eight is under that slate, being two shillings and fourpence, which is six hundred and twelve pence short of the wage of four marks, being three marks eleven shillings. Three hundred and seventeenth days is how long that yard has gone without a tin in it. That boy's hand of twenty-two heads the middle of those four columns and he is off the water for the eighth time and his surname is on no page. The fourth of them is at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in forty-four days and is neither shut nor struck. One hundred and thirty-fifth days is what that pair has stood at that stall, one size and a hand's breadth apart, with the wrong figure left showing under that correction. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and thirtieth day. The sheet with the fifteen gaps stands at fifteen and that wall of the room over the chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-ninth days. It is four hundred and second days since anything came out of the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head, he has not been on the water, and no figure at all is set against any part of it. The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. At about noon the keeper chalked the date on the back of that wage board and rubbed it out again, and about four of that nine said out loud that a date chalked and rubbed out is still a date somebody tried to keep in that shed. About four of that nine said out loud at about one that a wage Tuesday with no envelope opened is a Tuesday nobody in that yard will remember for anything but the mist on that quay. That room over that fish market stood shut all that Tuesday with its long table bare, and about four of that nine said out loud that a bare table in a shut room is not a thing anybody in that yard needs to write down at any hour. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head at first light yet still now.**"

Nobody was thanked for a Tuesday on which twenty-six lines stood at one hundred and four marks unmoved and twenty envelopes lay shut from about ten until about four and a further week fell due, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and twenty-one lines on it.

*First column, entered on Tuesday the tenth day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the forty-fourth entry of that run of fifty days: that the said day was the hundred and fifteenth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day; that twenty-six lines stand at one hundred and four marks and did not move and that chest with twenty envelopes was shut from about ten until about four with not one opened and the hundred and sixteenth week fell due at the close; that nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet and it was not read; that that piece of wood has been face up for eighteen days and nobody touched it; that ten hands went onto two sheets; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for one thing.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason twenty-six lines at one hundred and four marks did not move is that a wage column is a column that keeps weeks and not pence, and that a further week fallen due is a week and not a payment; that the reason not one envelope was opened is that a chest shut from about ten until about four is a chest nobody in that yard opens for any reason on a wage Tuesday; that the reason nobody asked a man of about thirty-four for one thing is that a bench sat at for a hundred and fifty-four days asks nothing of anybody.*
