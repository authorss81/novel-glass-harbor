# Chapter 897

*Tuesday the Third Day of January, the Thirty-Seventh Day of a Run of Fifty Days, the Hundred and Fourteenth Wage Tuesday, a Locked Chest With Twenty Envelopes in It on That Step From About Ten Until About Four and Not One of Those Twenty Envelopes Opened at Any Hour, Twenty-Six Lines at One Hundred and Four Marks Standing Unmoved and the Hundred and Fifteenth Week Fallen Due at the Close of It, That Piece of Wood Eleven Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Thirty and Three Thousand and Twenty-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-Nine and the Second at Three Thousand and Nineteen*

Tuesday the third day of January, with that market open from six and a hard white morning that had that quay hard enough by nine to walk on and a sky over that point like a lid by noon, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Tuesday is the hundred and fourteenth wage Tuesday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe, and it moves nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the fifth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January. That chest came out onto that step at about ten and stood on it until about four with twenty envelopes in it and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody. Twenty-six lines stand at one hundred and four marks and the hundred and fifteenth week stands fallen due on all twenty-six of them at the close of that Tuesday and not one line of the twenty-six moved a mark. That foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, is not read at any hour of that Tuesday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and tenth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried across this thirty-seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and ninety-fifth day. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninety-seventh day of not being asked a question. The two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirty-fifth day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its seven hundred and first night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and nineteenth day. The six lines at the back of that end stall are at the four hundred and seventy-fourth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-ninth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and sixth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-seventh day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and twenty-third day. That pair at that end stall has stood one hundred and twenty-eighth days a hand's breadth apart at one size with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-sixth day and it has six lines on it and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety-five days. That wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and forty-second days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of those columns stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-seven days. Three hundred and ninety-fifth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

The hundred and fourteenth of those Tuesdays came round again, and that chest was put out on that step at about ten and taken off it at about four with twenty envelopes in it, and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that Tuesday by anybody.

At about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, one above the other, the way he has written them on every one of those Tuesdays.

Twenty-six lines at one hundred and four marks stood there all day and moved nothing, and that column has stood at that figure since September and nothing has ever been drawn off it.

About four of that nine said out loud that a chest shut from about ten until about four has now been shut on a hundred and fourteen of those Tuesdays, and that nobody in this harbor has ever given a reason for that.

---

One of about four of that nine said out loud that a man asked a question in that shed yesterday in about nine people and another man answered him out loud, and that neither of the two of them wrote one word of it down on anything.

About four of that nine said out loud that not one of that nine knows what a man does with a thing he has heard and cannot write down, and that about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

Nobody in that shed said the answer of yesterday out loud a second time at any hour of that Tuesday, and nobody paraphrased it, and it is not on any page in this harbor.

---

A man of about fifty-eight of that market came through that shed at about two, looked at that piece of wood, said nothing at all and went out, and about four of that nine said out loud that nobody has ever asked that man a thing about anything at any hour of any day this run has had, and that about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for eleven days and nobody has touched it, and the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past four and said almost nothing at any hour of that Tuesday.

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place stood at the back of that shed all morning and said nothing and asked nobody for anything, and that is the sixty-third day he has been in that harbor.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

The ordinary work of that Tuesday was three boats off that mole before noon on a flat sea and a hand-cart of coal taken up to the second lay and brought back with the straps cut again, and not one of those came near that fitting-out sheet.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-seventh day of that run of days he has not missed.

Eleven hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries eleven against this day.

---

Nobody was thanked for a Tuesday on which twenty envelopes stood in a locked chest from about ten until about four and nobody in that yard said out loud what a man does with a thing he has heard and cannot write down, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and fourteen lines on it.

NOBODY IN THAT SHED SAID THE ANSWER OF MONDAY OUT LOUD A SECOND TIME OR PARAPHRASED IT, AND IT IS NOT ON ANY PAGE IN THIS HARBOR. *First column, entered on Tuesday the third day of January at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirty-seventh entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was the hundred and fourteenth wage Tuesday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the eighth day of January and the next charge is on Thursday the fifth day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the tenth day of January; that that chest stood on that step from about ten until about four with twenty envelopes in it and not one of those twenty envelopes was opened at any hour of that day by anybody; that at about eleven the keeper wrote the number of that week at the head of that sheet and wrote the number of the week now fallen due underneath it, one above the other, the way he has written them on every one of those Tuesdays; that twenty-six lines at one hundred and four marks stood there all day and moved nothing; that the hundred and fifteenth week stood fallen due on all twenty-six of those lines at the close of that day and did not move a mark; that about four of that nine said out loud that a chest shut from about ten until about four has now been shut on a hundred and fourteen of those Tuesdays and that nobody has given a reason for that; that one of about four of that nine said out loud that a man asked a question in that shed on Monday and another man answered him out loud and that neither wrote one word of it down on anything; that about four of that nine said out loud that not one of that nine knows what a man does with a thing he has heard and cannot write down; that nobody in that shed said the answer of Monday out loud a second time at any hour of that Tuesday and nobody paraphrased it; that that piece of wood has been face up on that bench for eleven days and that the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about ten until about half past four and said almost nothing; that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor sixty-three days and stood at the back of that shed all morning and said nothing; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about four and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and forty-seventh day of that run; and that eleven hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Tuesday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason the answer of Monday has not been said out loud a second time is that a second telling of it would be a claim, and that this harbor has four kinds of thing it may put up in a room and a man repeating a thing somebody else said in a yard is none of those four; that the reason about four of that nine said out loud that not one of that nine knows what a man does with a thing he has heard and cannot write down is that every figure this harbor has ever written down has had a place to be written down in before anybody wrote it, and that a thing said out loud in about nine people on an ordinary Monday has no such place and may never have one, and that this is a cost and not a fault; that the reason twenty-six lines have stood at one hundred and four marks for a hundred and fourteen of those Tuesdays is not given out loud by anybody in that shed and is not a thing the keeper has been asked about, and that a chest with twenty envelopes in it shut for four hours a week is a thing this harbor has decided about twice and written down nowhere; that the reason the man of about thirty-one has been in that harbor sixty-three days without once saying what he is here for is that nobody has the standing to require an answer of anybody in it, and that this has been true since the fourteenth of August and that a man who is owed an answer by nobody at all is a man who cannot be given one even by somebody who would give it.*
