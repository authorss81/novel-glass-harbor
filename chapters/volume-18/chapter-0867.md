# Chapter 867

*Sunday the Fourth Day of December, the Seventh Day of a Run of Fifty Days, a Wipe and the Hundred and Thirty-Fifth Time, the Wall Behind That Reeve's Bench Off at About Half Past Ten and Standing Bare Thirty-Nine Minutes and Back Up at a Quarter to One and Not Going Up Whole, Fifty-Two Off and Fifty-Two Back and One Hundred and Forty-Three the Whole of It, a Minute Longer at Each End Than It Ran the Sunday Before and Nobody in That Lane Saying a Word About Either End of It, About Four People in Front of It Counting and One of Them a Man of About Thirty-One of the Lowfen Reed's Own Place Saying Nothing for the Fifth Sunday Running, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Seventy-Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Five Hundred and Ninety-Nine and the Second at Two Thousand Eight Hundred and Sixty-Nine*

Sunday the fourth day of December, with that market shut until seven on a Sunday and a dirty sky out of the west-south-west and the flat water of Saturday gone back into a chop by eight, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Sunday is a wipe day and carries no charge on that cistern and moves nothing on a wage, and the next charge inside this run is on Monday the fifth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the sixth day of December, and the next wipe after this one is on Sunday the eleventh day of December. The wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare until a quarter to one and went back up unrepaired and not whole, at fifty-two minutes off and fifty-two back and thirty-nine standing bare, one hundred and forty-three the whole of it and a minute longer at each end than it ran the time before. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet today and no line was settled on it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-three thousand nine hundred and eighty-two pence out, being eight hundred and thirty-seven marks, five shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-three thousand three hundred and forty-two pence short, being seven hundred and seventy marks, eleven shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is two hundred and eightieth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried to the close of this seventh day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and sixty-fifth day and no answer has come back off it. The bellwright of this harbor is into the five hundred and sixty-seventh day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and fifth day. The bench beneath that pump lay bare again on its six hundred and seventy-first night. The two lights about a mile off that point are at their four hundred and eighty-ninth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and forty-fourth day. The paper of four lines in Fish Street is at the four hundred and fifty-ninth day. The nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street is at the four hundred and seventy-sixth day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and seventh day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its ninety-third day. That pair at that end stall has stood ninety-eight days side by side with the wrong figure still left showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its sixty-sixth day and it has six lines on it and it is not on any wall. The honest total of four hundred and six pence is on its sixty-fifth day of being on that community's own page. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and twelve days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in seven days. Three hundred and sixty-fifth days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---

That wall came off at about half past ten and about four people stood in front of it from the moment it came off, counting.

One of the four was the man of about thirty-two and one of the four was a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, and that is the fifth Sunday that man has stood in that lane, and on every one of the five he has kept his mouth shut.

It stood bare thirty-nine minutes and it went back at a quarter to one with the top course of it left short and one whole face of it showing the mortar behind, and that is the hundred and thirty-fifth time anybody has done it to it.

Fifty-two minutes off and fifty-two back and thirty-nine standing bare, one hundred and forty-three the whole of it, and both ends ran a minute longer than the two ends of the last one ran, and nobody in that lane said a word about either end of it.

A man came down that lane at about ten and was in it for the half hour it takes him and went away again before a quarter to one, and he did not speak to any of the four and none of the four spoke to him, and about four people in that lane could not tell this afternoon what he looks like.

---

Nobody in that lane asked anybody anything about that wall, and that includes the man who was in it for the half hour, and that has been true on every one of the Sundays this harbor can remember.

That is the fifth week running in which about four of that nine have stood in that lane while that wall was off, and about four of those four are the same four as last Sunday, and nobody in that lane has ever counted the people standing in front of it.

Both ends of it ran a minute longer than the ends of the last one ran, and about four of those four wrote both of those figures down in their own heads at about a quarter to one and compared them there and afterwards on their own way home, and that comparison was not said out loud to anybody.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor seven days by that Sunday and has asked nobody for anything on any of the seven, and about four of that nine said out loud that they had stopped trying to work out what he was here for.

Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head and answered one another at two hours a day, and about four of that nine said out loud that the sound of them does not merge into one note and that nobody in this harbor has ever tried to make it into one note.

Nine hulls lay about two sea miles off that mole head and did not move.

The ordinary work of that Sunday was two boats off that mole before noon in that chop and a bucket turned over out of the rain at that bench, and neither of those two came near that fitting-out sheet, and nothing in the world is set against either half of it.

---

The man of about thirty-two said out loud, to nobody in particular, that it was a minute each end again.

The man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place said nothing, which is the fifth Sunday, and he has never once been the one holding a slate and never once been the one with a figure.

---

At about half past eleven, in that lane, in about nine people, three questions were put out loud in the ordinary way and to nobody in particular.

One of about four of them asked whether that bench is due to be painted, and nobody in that lane answered him. A second man asked whether that cistern's covers come off at four or at half past four this time of year, and nobody in that lane knew and nobody said. A third asked whether the tide turns before that wall goes up or after, and about four of those nine said out loud that all three of those things had to be thought about, and none of the three was answered, and all three of them went back to counting.

That is three questions and not nine and not four, and none of the three of them is about that wall, and none of the three of them was put to the man who put it back up, who had gone by then.

---

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of the second lay came through that lane at about eleven and sat at that bench for an hour, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and seventeenth day of that run of days he has not missed.

About four of that nine counted that wall in front of him for an hour without saying one word to him or about him, and he has never once been asked by anybody in this harbor what he is doing there.

Ten hands went onto the faces of three sheets that left that harbor that day, and not one of those ten was the hand that wrote what any sheet went against, and the page of days carries ten against this day.

---

**THE HUNDRED AND THIRTY-FIFTH TIME THAT WALL CAME DOWN WENT DOWN BEHIND THAT REEVE'S BENCH AT ABOUT HALF PAST TEN AND STOOD BARE THIRTY-NINE MINUTES AND WENT BACK UP AT A QUARTER TO ONE AND DID NOT GO UP WHOLE, FIFTY-TWO AND THIRTY-NINE AND FIFTY-TWO AND ONE HUNDRED AND FORTY-THREE THE WHOLE OF IT, AND BOTH ENDS RAN A MINUTE LONGER THAN THE TIME BEFORE AND NOBODY SAID WHY AND NOBODY ASKED. THREE QUESTIONS WENT INTO THE COLUMN OF THE ASKED ON THIS DAY, BEING WHETHER THAT BENCH IS DUE TO BE PAINTED, WHETHER THAT CISTERN'S COVERS COME OFF AT FOUR OR AT HALF PAST FOUR THIS TIME OF YEAR, AND WHETHER THE TIDE TURNS BEFORE THAT WALL GOES UP OR AFTER, AND NONE OF THE THREE WAS ANSWERED. THE COLUMN WAS NOT ADDED UP. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS TWO HUNDRED AND EIGHTIETH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Sunday the fourth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the seventh entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was a wipe day and was not a rota day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next charge inside this run is on Monday the fifth day of December and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the sixth day of December and the next wipe is on Sunday the eleventh day of December; that the wall behind that reeve's bench came off the wall at about half past ten and stood bare thirty-nine minutes and went back up at a quarter to one and did not go up whole, at fifty-two minutes off and fifty-two back and thirty-nine standing bare, one hundred and forty-three the whole of it, being the hundred and thirty-fifth time; that about four people stood in front of it from the moment it came off and counted, and that one of the four was the man of about thirty-two and one of the four was a man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place, who has stood in that lane on five Sundays and said nothing on any of the five; *Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Sunday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason about four of that nine asked about a bench, a cistern's covers and a tide at about eleven on a Sunday is that a lane where four people have stood for an hour will find something to talk about, and that the something it finds is whatever is nearest the hand; that the reason a man of about thirty-one of that place has said nothing on five Sundays running is that he has never once had the thing in his hands that would let him say something, and that a man with nothing in his hands and no figure in his head is reduced to standing there; that the reason the man of about thirty-two said out loud that it was a minute each end again is that a figure which has risen by one a week for a hundred and thirty-five weeks is the only figure in this harbor that anybody trusts, and that he trusts it because he wrote it himself and nobody has ever come at him about it; that the reason nobody in that lane has asked anybody anything about that wall, including the man who is in it for the half hour, is that a wall which is up for a week and down for half an hour is not a thing anybody thinks of as a person, and that is the whole of the difficulty and there is no name on this page for it.
