# Chapter 893

*Friday the Thirtieth Day of December, the Thirty-Third Day of a Run of Fifty Days, an Ordinary Working Friday Carrying No Charge on That Cistern and No Wipe and Moving Nothing on a Wage and Settling Nothing on a Fitting-Out Line, No Body New in That Yard at Any Hour of It, That Piece of Wood Seven Days Face Up on That Bench, the Bag at Twenty-Eight Pence, the Four Columns Closing at Six Hundred and Twenty-Six and Three Thousand and Four and None and One Hundred and Twenty-Five, the First Column at Six Hundred and Twenty-Five and the Second at Two Thousand Nine Hundred and Ninety-Nine*

Friday the thirtieth day of December, with that market open from six and a bright cold morning that put a rim of white on every rail in that yard before nine and a north wind off that point from about one that did not stop until dark, a yard below that market with a shed standing in it, and about nine people standing in that shed at half past five that afternoon.

**This said Friday is an ordinary working Friday and carries no charge on that cistern and no wipe and moves nothing on a wage, and it settles nothing on a fitting-out line and works none, and the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Monday the second day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January. Nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that Friday and nothing is owed to it, that foot standing at one hundred and thirty-eight thousand nine hundred and six pence out, being eight hundred and sixty-eight marks, two shillings and twopence, and one hundred and twenty-eight thousand two hundred and sixty-six pence short, being eight hundred and one marks, eight shillings and tenpence, against ten thousand six hundred and forty pence in, and it is not read at any hour of that Friday. A hundred and sixty-eight pence came into that yard at about ten and a hundred and sixty-eight went out of it at about four, and under that slate is twenty-eight pence, being two shillings and fourpence, and that shed is three hundred and sixth days without a tin opened in it.**

**Nine counters were carried across this thirty-third day. That notice of the seventeenth of May past is at its five hundred and ninety-first day. That bellwright goes into the five hundred and ninety-third day of not being asked a question. Those two objects upon that crown which belong to nobody are at the nine hundred and thirty-first day. That bench beneath that pump was bare again on its six hundred and ninety-seventh night. Those two lights about a mile off that point are at their five hundred and fifteenth day. The six-line sheet at the back of that end stall is at the four hundred and seventieth day. That paper of four lines in Fish Street stands at its four hundred and eighty-fifth day. That nine-line sheet with a mark on it in that front street stands at its five hundred and second day. The man of about fifty-one is at the five hundred and thirty-third day of not speaking about six names. The leaf from the Cinder Bell is on its hundred and nineteenth day. That correction and that refusal at that end stall have stood one hundred and twenty-fourth days a hand's breadth apart with the wrong figure still showing under the correction and not struck out. The rule in the inside of that door is on its ninety-second day and it carries six lines and it is on no wall in this harbor. The honest total of four hundred and six pence has been on that community's own page for ninety-one days. The wall of that room over that chandler's store has stood bare for one hundred and thirty-eighth days. Over the middle of those four columns is a boy's hand of twenty-two with nothing under it, and he is off the water for the eighth time. The fourth of them stands at one hundred and twenty-five and has taken no line in thirty-three days. Three hundred and ninety-first days is what the left side of that man of about twenty-nine's head has gone without, and he is not on the water.**

---
That notice of the seventeenth of May past has been out of that office for five hundred and ninety-first days, and it has been taken down and put up twice by two different men told to do it by nobody in particular, and the pin that holds it in is bent at an angle.

About four of that nine said out loud that a thing taken down and put up twice by two different men is not going anywhere, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

A day with nothing on it in this harbor is not a day with nothing in it, and the keeper said that out loud at about four to nobody at all, and about four of that nine said out loud that a day with no figure on it is still a day somebody worked.

Nine buoys of nine shapes lay off that mole head that Friday and answered one another at two hours of it, and the sound did not run together into one note and nobody on that quay tried to make it.

---

The ordinary work of that Friday was two boats off that mole before noon and a barrow of broken stone run out to the end of that quay and left standing in a heap with nothing over it at all.

Nobody came into that yard on that Friday who had not been in it at some hour of the three weeks before it.

About four of that nine said out loud that three weeks is not a long time and about four of that nine said out loud that in that yard it is, and about four of that nine said nothing back to that.

---

That piece of wood has been face up on that bench in that shed for seven days and about four people walked past it that Friday and none of them said anything about it and none of them put a hand on it.

The man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about eleven until half past five and said nothing out loud at any hour of that Friday that about four of that nine heard, and one of about four of that nine said out loud that that is the longest a man has gone in that shed since he began.

Nobody said anything back to that.

---

The man of about thirty-one of that Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-nine days and came into that shed at about ten and stayed until about two.

The woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about two and then took it inside, and neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it.

From about ten until about two a man of about thirty-four of that second lay sat at that bench, and not one person in that lane asked him a thing, and that is the hundred and forty-third day of that run of days he has not missed.

That piece of wood lay face up on that same bench for four hours of that Friday, with the sleeve of that man's coat against the edge of it, close enough that either of them could have put a hand out and moved it without standing up, and neither of them did. The man of about thirty-two looked down at it more than once in that four hours and did not touch it and did not say one word about it, and what he was doing with his hands the whole of that time was keeping them in his own coat pockets. What he was thinking about, at that bench, in a working shed, with nobody in that yard asking him anything at all, was that a piece of wood which has lain face up for seven days is now part of that yard in a way that nothing else in that yard is, and that this is what he got for putting it down, and that he cannot think of one way of taking it back that would not be worse than leaving it where it lies.

Eight hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day, and the page of days carries eight against that day.

---

About half past five on that Friday the day's whole was said out loud at that stone standing up, in about eight hundred and twelve words, and the ordinary work came first in it and the seven days came in the middle of it and the four columns came at the end of it, and the only figure of that thing behind that reeve's bench within a hundred yards of that shed is on that piece of wood and there is no figure of it on any page in this harbor.

The hearing in the left side of the man of about thirty-two is not priced. Nine hulls sit about two sea miles off that mole head and thirty-three days of this run have gone by without a word.**"

Nobody was thanked for a Friday on which nobody new came into that yard and a man stood in that shed for six hours without saying one word, and that shed stood empty by about six with its door standing open on a slip nobody was on, and that page of that week had a hundred and ten lines on it.

**NOBODY CAME INTO THAT YARD ON THAT FRIDAY WHO HAD NOT BEEN IN IT AT SOME HOUR OF THE THREE WEEKS BEFORE IT, AND THE MAN OF ABOUT THIRTY-TWO WENT SIX HOURS IN THAT SHED AND SAID NOTHING AT ANY HOUR OF IT. THAT PIECE OF WOOD IS SEVEN DAYS FACE UP ON THAT BENCH AND HAS NOT BEEN TOUCHED BY ANYBODY. THE BAG HOLDS TWENTY-EIGHT PENCE, BEING TWO SHILLINGS AND FOURPENCE, AND THIS YARD IS THREE HUNDRED AND SIXTH DAYS WITHOUT A TIN IN IT.**

*First column, entered on Friday the thirtieth day of December at about six in the evening in that shed, being the thirty-third entry of this run of fifty days: that the said day was an ordinary working Friday and was not a rota day and was not a wipe day and was not a wage Tuesday, and that the next wipe inside this run is on Sunday the first day of January and the next charge is on Monday the second day of January and the next wage Tuesday is on Tuesday the third day of January; that nothing came into the foot of that fitting-out sheet on that day and that nothing is owed to it and that the keeper did not read that foot at any hour of that Friday; that nobody came into that yard on that Friday who had not been in it at some hour of the three weeks before it, and that about four of that nine said out loud that three weeks is not a long time and that in that yard it is; that that piece of wood has been face up on that bench for seven days and that about four people walked past it that Friday and that none of them put a hand on it; that the man of about thirty-two was in that shed from about eleven until about half past five and said nothing out loud at any hour of that day that about four of that nine heard; that the man of about thirty-one of the Lowfen Reed's own place had been in that harbor fifty-nine days and came into that shed at about ten and stayed until about two; that the woman of thirty, who holds no office, worked that yard over her own page from about ten until about two and then took it inside, and that neither of the two of them said one word to the other about any part of any of it; that from about ten until about two nobody asked a man of about thirty-four of that second lay for one thing, being the hundred and forty-third day of that run; and that eight hands went onto the faces of two sheets that left that harbor that day.*

*Second column, not checkable from a mole, entered on the said Friday at about six in the evening in that shed, and no name is against it: that the reason nobody new came into that yard on that Friday is that four people stopped saying out loud in public that somebody ought to, and that four people going quiet is not a decision and is not a rule and leaves no mark of itself on anything; that the reason the man of about thirty-two said nothing for six hours is that a man who has put a thing down in a shed in the rain on a Friday and had four people tell him to his face that a count handed over is not a count has nothing to add to a yard that has gone quiet, and that this is not peace and is not a sulk and is not anybody keeping a figure about himself; that the reason nobody said anything back when one of about four of that nine said out loud that that was the longest a man has gone in that shed since he began coming into it is that a man who is not speaking cannot be remarked upon, and that this harbor has found that a convenient arrangement at least twice before.*
